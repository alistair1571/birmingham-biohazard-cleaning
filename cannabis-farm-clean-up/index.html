Cannabis farm clean up in Birmingham

Removal of growing equipment, waste and contamination, then deep cleaning, mould treatment and a proper paper trail for your insurer or lender. For landlords, agents, housing providers and owners across Birmingham and the West Midlands.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

If the police have just handed your property back, you're not the first landlord to walk into this, and the first call is the hard one. Tell us what you've found and we'll tell you what happens next.

A property that has housed a cannabis farm is rarely just untidy. Walls and ceilings are often soaked by months of humidity, mould spreads through the plaster and joists, electrics are frequently tampered with, and fertiliser, soil and chemical containers are left where they were dropped. Police remove the plants and leave. Everything else is the owner's problem.

Most landlords get the call from an officer, not from the tenant, and then face the same questions at once. Is it safe to go in? Who deals with the electricity? Will the insurer pay? How long will it be empty? Can it be re-let, and what will a lender or the council want to see?

This page answers those in the order they usually come up. We can arrange a team to clear and clean the property, give you the price in writing first, and provide the records an insurer or lender will ask for. We can't rewire or rebuild a ceiling, and we'll say so plainly, because the clean is one stage in getting the property back on the market, not the whole of it.

What's included

  • Removal of growing equipment: lights, ducting, fans, filters, timers, tents, pots and hydroponic kit
  • Removal of soil, plant waste, fertiliser, nutrient and chemical containers, taken away as controlled waste with documentation
  • Stripping out of contaminated materials, such as soaked carpets, damaged board and insulation, where they can't be saved
  • Mould treatment and cleaning of affected walls, ceilings and lofts, with advice where surfaces need to be replaced rather than cleaned
  • Cleaning and sanitising of each room used for growing or storage, plus the routes between them
  • Odour removal, once the source has gone
  • Dated before-and-after photos and a written summary of the work, ready for your insurer, mortgage lender or council

Who calls us

  • Landlords and letting agents whose tenant has been raided or has abandoned the property: Landlords and letting agents
  • Housing associations and managing agents that need a documented response and a property returned to a lettable state
  • Owners of a house or flat they've bought or inherited that turns out to have been a grow house
  • Commercial landlords with a warehouse, shop unit or garage that was used for growing
  • Executors and families who've found signs of growing while clearing a relative's property

First, what to do if the police have just called

  1. Don't enter until the police say you can.The property may be an active crime scene, and the electrics, wiring and fumes can be dangerous. If a fire service or police officer tells you to keep out, stay out.
  2. Ask the officer for a crime reference numberand the name of the officer in charge. Insurers, lenders and your managing agent will all ask for it.
  3. Don't switch the power back on.Illegal growing often involves bypassed meters, new cabling and overloaded circuits. Contact your electricity supplier or network operator, and have a qualified electrician inspect the installation before anything is reconnected.
  4. Photograph everythingfrom the doorway, before moving anything. Keep dated pictures of the property from before the tenancy, too, if you have them.
  5. Tell your insurer straight away.Policies differ. Some exclude cannabis cultivation, some cap what they pay, and some require regular inspections. Don't assume you're covered, and don't assume you aren't.
  6. Call us.We can talk through the scope, take a view from photographs and plan the clean before you're back inside.

What the police leave behind

People tend to assume that once the plants have gone, the problem has gone too. Typically, it hasn't.

  • Equipment. The police take the plants and sometimes samples. Lights, fans, ducting and tanks are often left.
  • Moisture and mould. Months of heat and humidity can soak plasterboard, insulation, lofts and timber. Mould can run behind surfaces you can't see.
  • Electrical damage. Bypassed meters, scorched fittings and homemade wiring are common, and make the property unsafe to occupy until a qualified electrician has put it right.
  • Structural changes. Holes cut through ceilings and floors for ducting, removed internal walls and sealed or blacked-out windows.
  • Chemicals and waste. Fertiliser, pesticide and nutrient containers, compost and soil.
  • The smell. It soaks into carpet, plaster and soft furnishings, and lingers long after the plants have gone.

Cleaning is one stage: the order that saves time

A cannabis farm recovery usually involves several trades, and doing them in the wrong order costs time and money. A sensible sequence is:

  1. Safety and evidence.Police release, electrical isolation and photos.
  2. Clearance and decontamination.That's us: equipment out, waste taken away, contaminated materials stripped, surfaces cleaned, mould treated.
  3. Assessment.An electrician, and a surveyor or builder where there's structural damage, can now see what they are dealing with.
  4. Repair.Rewiring, ceiling and floor repairs, drying and redecoration.
  5. Certification and re-letting.An electrical safety report, gas safety check and any other documents your insurer, lender or council needs.

Starting repairs before the property has been cleared and dried tends to hide the damage, and mould that is painted over comes back.

Insurance, lenders and your tenancy

What follows is a general guide rather than professional advice, and policies vary widely.

  • Insurance. Read your policy wording for cannabis cultivation, illegal use, malicious damage and unoccupied-property conditions. Ask whether there is a cap, and keep every quote and invoice. We provide itemised quotes and photo records so they can support a claim.
  • Your mortgage lender. Many lenders expect to be told if a buy-to-let has been used for crime or has been damaged badly. Check your terms before you do.
  • The tenancy. If the tenant has abandoned the property, don't simply remove their belongings and re-let. Take advice on the proper process first.
  • Void periods. The property earns no rent while this is going on. Telling us your target re-let date lets us plan the clearance to fit it.
  • Council contact. If you suspect the property was used for growing and the police haven't been involved, or the property is in poor condition, your council's environmental health or housing team can advise on your responsibilities.

If you've bought or inherited the property

If you suspect a house or flat has been used for growing, a survey and an electrical report matter more than the price. Warning signs include tampered meters and heavy cabling, blacked-out windows, damp that seems out of proportion, extractor ducting in the loft, signs of cut or replaced joists, and a persistent musty or sweet smell. We can quote for clearance and decontamination from photographs, so you know what you are committing to before you exchange.

What affects the price

No two grow houses are alike. What moves the price:

  • How much equipment and waste there is, and how many rooms were used
  • How far the damp and mould has spread, particularly into lofts and ceilings
  • How much has to be stripped out, such as carpets, board and insulation
  • Volume of chemicals and soil that need disposing of as controlled waste
  • Access: stairs, parking and distance from the street
  • Timing and urgency, including a target re-let date

You'll receive a quote in writing, itemised, before any work starts. Photos are enough for most quotes; a visit is free if the job is larger.

Cannabis Farm Clean Up FAQs

Can I go into the property after a police raid?

Only when the police confirm that you can. Even then, treat it with caution. Electrics may be live and unsafe, the air may be damp and full of mould spores, and there may be chemicals. Have it inspected before anyone lives or works there.

Will my insurance pay for the clean-up?

Possibly, but it depends on the policy. Some insurers exclude cannabis cultivation or cap what they pay, so check the wording and tell your insurer before authorising work. Keep the quote, the crime reference number and photos.

Do you do the electrical and structural repairs too?

No. We clear and decontaminate. Rewiring and structural repairs need a qualified electrician and builder, and we can tell you what to expect so you can plan them.

How long does it take?

A small, contained grow can take a day or two. A whole property with mould and extensive stripping-out can take longer. Your quote will include an estimate, and drying time is separate from cleaning time.

Will the smell and mould come back?

Not if the source is dealt with. Removing contaminated materials, treating the mould and drying the building properly is what stops it returning. Painting over damp plaster is what makes it come back.

Is the property safe to re-let afterwards?

Not until it has an up-to-date electrical safety report, any repairs completed and the other checks your insurer, lender and council need. We provide the records of the clean; the rest has to come from the right trades.

Do you deal with HMOs, flats, garages and commercial units?

Yes. Tell us what type of property it is and who can approve the work.

What happens to the waste?

It is removed by a registered waste carrier, and you receive the paperwork. Chemicals and contaminated waste are handled as controlled waste, not put in a skip.

Is it discreet?

Yes. Nothing about the property is discussed with neighbours. If privacy matters, tell us when you call and we'll plan arrival and access with that in mind.
