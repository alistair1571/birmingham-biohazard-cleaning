If it has just happened

Biohazard cleaning in Birmingham: what to do in the first hour

Finding blood, bodily fluids, sewage, needles or the aftermath of a death or an incident at a property is frightening, and the instinct is to start cleaning straight away. Please hold off. A few calm steps in the first hour keep you safe, protect the evidence an insurer may ask for, and make the specialist's job quicker and cheaper.

Do

  • Keep people and pets outClose the door to the affected room and keep children and animals away until it has been cleaned.
  • Wait for the police if they are involvedDo not go back into a scene until the police tell you the property has been released.
  • Photograph from the doorwayWide photos help your insurer and let the team quote faster. Do not move anything to get a better angle.
  • Ventilate if it is safeOpen a window in the affected room and keep the doors to unaffected rooms shut.
  • Write things downA police reference if you have one, when you found the problem, and who has been in the room.

Do not

  • Scrub, mop or vacuumIt spreads contamination and can put particles into the air you breathe.
  • Pick up needles or sharpsIf anyone has been pricked, get medical advice from NHS 111 or A&E straight away.
  • Throw away soaked itemsCarpets, mattresses and furniture can be evidence for an insurer. Leave them for the team to remove and record.
  • Mix cleaning productsSome combinations give off harmful gases, and household products will not disinfect properly anyway.
  • Stay in a room with a very strong smellA strong smell is a sign of heavy contamination. Keep out and call for advice.

On the day

What happens when the specialist team arrives

Most people have never seen a biohazard clean-up, and not knowing what to expect adds to the stress. Here is how a properly run job usually goes.

1. Assessment

The team walks the property with you, or reviews your photographs first, to see what has been affected and what has soaked in. Hard surfaces can normally be cleaned and disinfected. Porous materials such as carpet, underlay, floorboards, mattresses and upholstery often cannot be made safe once fluids have soaked into them, and are removed instead.

2. Containment and protection

The work area is separated from the rest of the property so that contamination does not travel. Technicians wear protective suits, gloves and respiratory protection, and bring their own equipment and products rather than using yours.

3. Removal, cleaning and disinfection

Contaminated materials are removed and bagged, then surfaces are cleaned and disinfected with products suited to the type of contamination. Odour is treated at the source, which usually means removing what is causing it. Masking a smell with fragrance does not last.

4. Waste and paperwork

Contaminated waste leaves the property in sealed containers and has to go through authorised channels, covered in more detail below. You should be told where it is going.

5. Handover

Ask for photographs and a short written summary of what was cleaned, removed and treated. Insurers, landlords, housing providers and probate solicitors often want it.

How long does it take?

A single contained incident can often be dealt with in a few hours. A heavily affected room, a death where fluids have soaked through a floor, or a hoarded property can take several days. The team should give you a realistic estimate when they quote, and tell you early if the job turns out bigger than it looked.

What moves the price up or down

  • The type of contamination, because sharps, decomposition and sewage each need different protection and time
  • How many rooms are affected, and whether stairwells, communal areas or outbuildings are involved
  • How much porous material has to be removed rather than cleaned
  • The volume of waste and how it has to be disposed of
  • Odour treatment, which can add targeted extra work
  • Access, such as stairs without a lift, parking distance and key collection
  • Urgency, since out-of-hours and same-day attendance usually costs more

What to have ready for a quick, accurate quote

The postcode, what happened and roughly when, which rooms are affected, photographs taken safely from the doorway, whether the police are involved, whether you own, rent or manage the property, and whether an insurance claim is likely. With that, many teams can give a firm price without a visit, and will tell you straight away if they need to see the property first.

Responsibility

Who is responsible for biohazard cleaning, and who pays?

Many people assume someone official will deal with the property after an incident. In most cases nobody does, and it falls to whoever is responsible for the building.

After a death at home

The police, paramedics and funeral directors deal with the person who has died, not the property. Cleaning is normally down to whoever owns or is responsible for the home: a surviving householder, the executor of the estate, or the landlord if it was rented. Work can only start once the person has been removed and the property has been released by the police or coroner, if they were involved.

After a crime or a serious incident

Police do not clean up crime scenes. Once their investigation ends and the property is released, the owner is responsible for arranging the clean-up, and for the cost unless insurance covers it. Do not enter the scene until you have been told it is safe to.

In a rented property

Who pays depends on the tenancy agreement and on what caused the problem. Landlords usually arrange the clean-up and may try to recover costs from a tenant or deposit, which can lead to disputes. If you are a tenant, do not attempt the clean-up yourself, and get advice from Citizens Advice or Shelter if you are asked to pay.

Hoarding

The householder is normally responsible, but landlords, housing associations and councils sometimes get involved where there is a fire risk or access problem. Anyone, including a family member, can refer a household to West Midlands Fire Service for a Safe and Well visit, where firefighters can talk through hoarding and fire safety with residents who are willing.

Insurance

Cover varies a great deal. Some buildings, contents and landlord policies contribute towards biohazard clean-up, others exclude it, and the cause matters. Tell your insurer as early as you can, keep photographs and every receipt, and ask for an itemised quote you can pass on. Check with the insurer before you agree to expensive work if you can, and never throw away evidence before they have seen it.

Choosing a specialist

What to ask before you hire a biohazard cleaner

Blood, bodily fluids and the materials they soak into are hazardous waste, not ordinary rubbish. The Health and Safety Executive says waste contaminated with blood-borne viruses is hazardous unless it has been made safe, and most of it is treated as clinical or infectious waste. In England, hazardous waste has to be handled by authorised businesses: carriers are registered, disposal sites hold environmental permits, and waste movements are recorded on a consignment note.

That is why the questions below matter. Any good team will be glad to answer them.

  • Are you a registered waste carrier? Ask for the registration number. GOV.UK has a public tool for checking registered carriers.
  • Where will the waste go, and will I see the paperwork? Ask for the waste documentation, such as the consignment note where one applies.
  • Do you hold public liability and employers' liability insurance? Ask to see the certificate rather than taking it on trust.
  • Can I have an itemised written quote? It should say what is included, such as waste, odour treatment and removal of materials, and what is not, such as repairs.
  • What will I get when you finish? Photographs and a written summary of what was cleaned, removed and treated.
  • How will you protect our privacy? Ask about arrival arrangements, vehicles and who will be told.
  • What training do your technicians have? A named course or accrediting body tells you more than the word "certified".

Be cautious of anyone who quotes a single price over the phone without asking what has happened, or who asks for full payment in cash up front.

FAQ

Your questions answered

How quickly can someone attend?

Our phone line is open 24 hours a day. Response times depend on the job and on team availability, so call and describe the situation for the quickest answer.

Is the service discreet?

Yes. These jobs are often sensitive, and the teams we connect you with are asked to work quietly and keep every detail private.

Will I be judged?

No. The teams see homes in every condition and treat every customer with kindness and respect.

Do you handle waste disposal?

Yes. Contaminated and hazardous waste has to be carried and disposed of by a registered waste carrier. The teams we work with take care of this as part of the job.

Do you work with landlords, agents and housing associations?

Yes. We work with homeowners, families, landlords, letting agents, housing associations and commercial property managers.

How much will it cost?

Every job is different. Cost depends on the type of contamination, the size of the area and how much has to be removed. You will get a free, no-obligation quote, and photographs help us estimate quickly.

Can I get a quote without a visit?

Often, yes. Photographs and a description are enough for many jobs. For larger or more complex properties the team may visit to assess first.

Is biohazard cleaning covered by insurance?

Sometimes. Cover depends on the policy and the cause. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and request an itemised quote to support any claim.

Do you only do biohazard cleaning?

No. As well as biohazard clean-ups, we can connect you with teams for fire and smoke damage, flood and water damage, and odour removal.

Which areas do you cover?

We cover Birmingham, the city centre and Sutton Coldfield, the Black Country towns of Wolverhampton, Walsall, Dudley, West Bromwich, Smethwick, Oldbury, Tipton, Wednesbury, Bilston, Willenhall, Darlaston, Sedgley, Halesowen, Stourbridge and more, plus Solihull, Coventry, Lichfield and Tamworth. If your postcode is nearby but not listed, call and ask.

How long does a biohazard clean take?

A single contained incident can often be finished in a few hours. A heavily affected room, a death where fluids have soaked into a floor, or a hoarded property can take several days. The team should give you a realistic estimate with the quote and tell you early if the job turns out bigger than it looked.

Can I stay in the property while the work is done?

Usually not in the affected areas, and sometimes not in the property at all, depending on the contamination and the protective measures needed. The team will tell you what is safe for you and for any children or pets, and will keep the work area separate from the rest of the home.

Do I need to be there?

Not always. For many jobs the team can arrange access through a landlord, managing agent or family member, and agree how you will be updated. Some people prefer to be there at the start to walk the team through, and then leave them to it.

Will the smell definitely go?

In most cases it can be removed or greatly reduced once the contaminated materials have been taken out and the source treated. If fluids have soaked deep into floorboards or a subfloor, extra treatment or replacement may be needed, and a good team will say so in the quote rather than promise a result they cannot guarantee.

What happens to my belongings?

Hard items can often be cleaned and kept. Porous items such as mattresses, carpets and upholstery that fluids have soaked into usually cannot be made safe and are removed. Tell the team about anything of value or sentimental importance before they start, and ask them to set it aside.

How will I know it is safe afterwards?

Ask for photographs and a short written summary of what was cleaned, removed and treated, including anything that could not be fully resolved. That record is also what insurers, landlords and solicitors usually ask for.

What if the police are still involved?

Do not go back into the property until the police tell you it has been released, and do not start cleaning. Once it is released, call us and we will pass your details to a team who can attend. Keep any police reference number, because your insurer may ask for it.

I am a tenant. Can you help?

Yes, but speak to your landlord or letting agent first, because they may need to authorise the work, and who pays depends on your tenancy agreement and the cause. Do not attempt the clean-up yourself. If you are asked to pay and are unsure whether that is fair, Citizens Advice or Shelter can help.
