Pigeon guano cleaning in Birmingham

Safe removal and disinfection of pigeon droppings from lofts, roof spaces, balconies, ledges, fire escapes and commercial buildings. Across Birmingham and the West Midlands.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

Please don't sweep it dry. Ring us first, and we'll tell you what's safe to do and what isn't.

Pigeon droppings look harmless, and a thin layer on a window ledge might be. But a loft, a flat roof or a balcony with months or years of build-up is a different matter. Dried guano turns to dust, and the dust can be breathed in. Wet guano is slippery, smells and can drain into gutters, soak insulation or run into stonework.

People usually reach this page in one of three situations. You've opened the loft hatch and found birds have been roosting there. You own or manage a building where droppings are on the walkway, the fire escape or the roof. Or you rent or lease a flat with a balcony that has become a roost, and you don't know who's supposed to deal with it. This page answers those questions: how risky it is, who's responsible, what a proper clean involves, and how to stop it coming back.

Ring us and a suitably equipped team can be lined up, with the cost confirmed in writing before any work begins.

Is pigeon guano dangerous?

For most people, a short exposure to a small amount outdoors is unlikely to cause harm. Heavy accumulations, particularly in enclosed spaces such as lofts, are what matter.

Droppings can carry organisms that cause illness, including psittacosis, a rare infection that can cause a flu-like illness and sometimes pneumonia, and salmonella, which causes diarrhoea. Some sources also name fungal infections such as histoplasmosis and cryptococcosis. The usual route is breathing in dust or droplets from dry or disturbed droppings, which is why the way you clean matters more than the amount you see.

Keep children, older people, and anyone with a respiratory condition or a lowered immune defence out of the area. If someone develops flu-like symptoms or a cough after working in or near a pigeon-fouled space, they should tell their GP what they've been doing. This is general information, not medical advice.

Why you shouldn't clean it yourself

The standard advice from health and safety bodies is to keep dust down, avoid contact and use proper respiratory protection. At home that is harder than it sounds:

  • Don't sweep or brush it dry. It puts the fine dust in the air.
  • Don't pressure-wash it. It sprays contaminated droplets around, onto neighbours' windows and into the air.
  • A paper dust mask isn't enough. Proper protection for heavier jobs is a tight-fitting respirator, correctly fitted, with suitable filters.
  • Lofts are awkward. Poor light, low ceilings and fragile boards or ceilings make it easy to fall or put a foot through the ceiling below.
  • Roofs and ledges need working-at-height precautions.
  • Waste has rules. Larger quantities are often classed as hazardous waste. It can't just go in the household bin.

For a very small amount on a ground-level windowsill, wet it, wipe it up wearing disposable gloves and bag it, and wash your hands. For anything more, we'd advise a specialist.

Whose job is it?

This is the question people struggle to find an answer to.

  • Homeowners. It is your property, so the clean-up is yours to arrange. Home insurance generally doesn't cover pests or gradual damage, but check your policy.
  • Tenants. Tell your landlord or agent in writing, and send photos. Don't start work in the loft or on the roof yourself.
  • Leaseholders and flat owners. Droppings on a shared roof, communal balcony or walkway are normally for the freeholder or managing agent to deal with. A balcony that is part of your own flat may be your responsibility. Check your lease.
  • Landlords and agents. If a property has a roosting problem, deal with it before inspections, repairs or new tenants.
  • Businesses and building managers. You have duties under health and safety law to protect staff, visitors and contractors, which usually means a written risk assessment before any clean. Droppings on access routes, loading bays or food areas count.
  • Neighbours' or council buildings. If the birds are roosting on someone else's roof or the council's land, report it to them. We can only work on the property with the owner's permission.

This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Cleaning is only half of it: stopping pigeons coming back

If the birds can still get in or roost, the mess comes back. A clean-up doesn't prevent that, and no honest cleaner will say otherwise. The fixes are proofing measures, such as netting, spikes, wires, sealing gaps and repairing broken vents, and they are done by a bird-proofing or pest control specialist.

The order matters:

  1. Survey and proofing plan.Work out how they are getting in and which deterrents suit the site.
  2. Clean-up.Clearing the guano first lets the proofing team see the damage and fit things properly.
  3. Proofing.Block the access points, then check them regularly.

If you tell us the birds are still there when you call, we'll help you work out the right sequence. Wild birds and their active nests are protected by law in the UK, so if there are eggs or chicks it may be necessary to wait or to take advice before anything is disturbed.

What gets damaged

Guano is more than a health problem. Left to build up, it can:

  • block gutters and downpipes, causing leaks and damp
  • soak loft insulation, which then has to be replaced
  • stain and eat into stonework, brickwork, paint and some metals
  • cover solar panels and cut their output
  • create slip hazards on stairs, fire escapes and entrances
  • attract insects and other pests into the building

If your loft has been used for a long time, ask us to check the insulation and any boards or ceilings you cannot see, because the damage often continues under what looks fine.

Birds, mites and what they leave behind

When birds have been nesting in a roof space for a long time, they can leave bird mites, fleas and other insects behind. Those can bite and move into living areas once the birds have gone. If you've noticed small insects or itchy bites since the birds were removed, tell us when you call, because it changes the plan. A pest control specialist can then deal with the insects once the nest material has been cleared.

What affects the price

Sites differ widely, so each one gets its own quote. The main drivers:

  • How much guano there is and how long it has built up
  • Where it is. Loft, roof or ledge, and how hard it is to reach
  • Whether access equipment is needed for working at height
  • Whether insulation or other materials have to be removed
  • How much waste has to be disposed of
  • Scale, from one balcony to a whole roof
  • Timing, including evening and weekend work for businesses

You'll receive an itemised quote in writing before anything starts. A few photos usually do the job.

Pigeon Guano Cleaning FAQs

Are pigeon droppings really dangerous?

They can be, mainly in heavy or enclosed build-ups, because the dust can carry organisms that cause illness. A little on a ledge in the open carries much less risk. The way it's cleaned is what protects you.

Can I clean up pigeon droppings myself?

A small amount outdoors, yes, with care: wet it first, wear disposable gloves, bag it and wash your hands. A loft, a large build-up or anywhere the dust can build up in a closed space should be left to a specialist.

Will you stop the pigeons coming back?

We clean and disinfect. Stopping birds from returning needs proofing from a specialist, and we'll help you plan the order.

Do you remove nests and dead birds?

Yes, where it's legal to. If the nest is active, the law protects it, so take advice first.

What about the insulation?

If it's soaked with droppings it generally has to go, because it can't be cleaned. We'll tell you after looking.

Do you clean commercial buildings, car parks and fire escapes?

Yes. Tell us the type of site, how it's accessed and when it's quiet, and we'll plan around it.

How long does it take?

A balcony may take a few hours. A loft or a large roof can run to a day or longer, and the quote will give you an estimate.

Will my insurance cover it?

Home insurance often doesn't cover pests and gradual damage, but policies vary. Ask your insurer, and keep the quote and photos.

How do I get rid of the smell?

By removing the droppings and nest material and cleaning the surfaces beneath. Air fresheners just cover it.

Where does the waste go?

It is bagged and removed by a registered carrier, and you can ask for the paperwork.
