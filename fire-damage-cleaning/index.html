Fire damage cleaning in Birmingham

Soot, smoke residue, ash and firefighting water cleaned from your home or business, with the photos and paperwork your insurer will ask for. Covering Birmingham and the wider West Midlands around the clock.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

The fire service has gone, the shock hasn't, and there's a long list of things to deal with. Ring us, and we'll help you put the cleaning in the right place on that list.

After a fire, the damage you can see is often the smaller part of it. Soot travels through the whole building, settles into carpets and soft furnishings, coats the inside of cupboards and light fittings, and works its way into plaster and floor voids. The water used to put the fire out soaks floors and ceilings, sometimes several rooms away from the flames. Both keep doing harm for as long as they are left.

Most people reading this page are in the first week or two. You may not have been told what happens next, who to call first, whether you're allowed to clean anything, or whether your insurer will want to use their own contractor. So this page is written around those questions in the order they tend to arise, from what to do before you go back in, to what a fire clean involves and what happens to your belongings.

If you'd like a team to take it on, get in touch. The cost is confirmed in writing first, and nothing is started until you or your insurer agree.

What's included

  • A survey of the fire, smoke and water damage, with photos taken before anything is moved
  • Removal of debris, ash, burnt materials and anything that can't be salvaged
  • Dry and wet cleaning of soot from walls, ceilings, floors, fittings, appliances and contents that can be saved
  • Cleaning and treatment of areas the fire never reached but the smoke did
  • Extraction of firefighting water, and drying where it is needed (see our flood damage clean up page)
  • Odour treatment once the residue has gone, including smoke smells in fabric and plaster
  • Fire debris and burnt waste taken away lawfully, with the paperwork to show for it
  • A written report and itemised photos you can pass to your insurer, loss adjuster, landlord or lender

When people call us

  • A kitchen fire that has left grease, soot and smoke in the whole house
  • An electrical or appliance fire in a flat, where the damage runs into neighbouring homes
  • A fire in a rented home, where the landlord or agent has to restore the property: Landlords and letting agents
  • A fire at a shop, restaurant, office or workshop, where you need to reopen
  • Fire in a garage, loft or outbuilding that has spread soot into the main house
  • An arson or vandalism attempt where the police have now released the building
  • Smoke damage without much burning, such as a chimney or boiler fault: Smoke damage cleaning

Before you go back in

  1. Wait for the fire service to say it's safe.Fires can weaken floors, ceilings and roofs, and the building may not be safe even if it looks fine. In serious cases the council can restrict access.
  2. Ask for the fire service's incident number or report.Your insurer is likely to want it.
  3. Don't switch on the electricity or gasor use appliances that were near the fire until a qualified electrician or Gas Safe engineer has checked them.
  4. Contact your insurer(or your landlord or agent if you rent) as soon as you can. Ask them what they want you to do about cleaning and emergency repairs.
  5. Secure the property.Windows and doors damaged by the fire or the fire service may need boarding up. Your insurer can usually arrange this.
  6. Photograph or film everything,before anything is moved or cleaned.
  7. Keep receiptsfor hotels, food, clothing and other emergency costs. Insurers often reimburse them.
  8. Be wary of cold callers.Anyone who turns up uninvited offering to "handle your claim" or asking for money in advance should be treated with suspicion. Your insurer will already have your details.

If the shock has left you shaken, you aren't alone. The British Red Cross offers practical and emotional support after a house fire, and a GP or NHS 111 can help if you feel unwell.

Can I start cleaning before the insurer arrives?

This is the question that trips people up, and the answer is: not without checking.

  • Don't throw things away until your insurer or loss adjuster has had a chance to see them, or has told you to go ahead. Photos are good evidence, but sometimes they want the item itself.
  • Don't scrub soot off the walls. Wiping soot with water or the wrong cleaner can smear it into the surface, and make it harder to remove.
  • Do open windows to ventilate, if it is safe and secure to do so.
  • Do take photos, and write a list of damaged contents as you go.

Soot is acidic and becomes harder to remove the longer it stays, so waiting too long isn't ideal either. A call to the insurer usually settles it: ask whether they want to appoint their own cleaner, or whether you may choose. Many policies let you instruct your own contractor, but terms differ, so ask first and get it in writing. We can provide the itemised quote and photos that support a claim, and you stay in control of the decision.

What the fire leaves behind

  • Soot and ash on every surface, including some that are far from the fire
  • Greasy smoke residue, especially after kitchen fires, which smears if wiped dry
  • Smoke odour held in carpets, curtains, plaster and mattresses
  • Firefighting water in floors, ceilings and wall cavities, with the risk of mould if it isn't dried
  • Heat and corrosion damage to metal, glass, plastic and electronics
  • Damaged structure, such as charred joists or cracked plaster, which needs a surveyor or builder, not a cleaner

What can be saved?

It varies by material and by how close the item was to the heat.

  • Often cleanable: hard surfaces, metal, glass, ceramics, many hard furnishings and solid wood
  • Sometimes cleanable: clothing, curtains, bedding and some soft furnishings, by professional laundering or specialist cleaning
  • Often not: carpets and mattresses that are heavily affected, food, medicines, cosmetics and anything that has been near intense heat
  • Check before use: electronics, appliances and anything with a battery. Have an electrician or engineer test them

Food, drink and medicines that were exposed to heat, smoke or firefighting water should generally be thrown away. For anything of sentimental or financial value, such as photographs or paperwork, tell us before we start and we'll set it aside.

Where we stop and other trades begin

A fire recovery usually involves several people, and the order matters:

  1. Fire service and, where needed, police and the council.
  2. Insurer or loss adjuster.
  3. Boarding up and making safe.
  4. Clearance, soot removal, drying and cleaning.That's us.
  5. Surveyor, electrician, gas engineer and builder,for structure, wiring and repairs.
  6. Redecoration and reinstatement.

We don't do structural repairs, rewiring or reinstatement, and we'll tell you which parts of the job need another trade. Cleaning first, though, lets those trades see the real extent of the damage.

If it's a rented property, a block or a business

Landlords and agents. Tell your insurer straight away, keep the fire report, and document the property before and after. A tenant may be entitled to alternative arrangements, depending on the tenancy, so check your agreement. The landlords page has more.

Tenants. Let the landlord or agent know, and your own contents insurer if you have one. Don't start cleaning the landlord's fixtures and fittings before talking to them.

Flats. Smoke and water travel between flats. Tell the managing agent so shared areas and neighbouring homes can be assessed together.

Businesses. Tell us your trading hours and what stock, equipment or records need saving. Food premises will need documented cleaning before reopening. Business interruption cover may pay for lost income, so check your policy.

None of this is legal or insurance advice, and policies vary.

What affects the price

Fire cleans range from one smoke-stained room to a whole building. What moves the price:

  • How far the damage extends, including rooms that only got smoke
  • The type of residue. Grease from a kitchen fire and dry soot need different treatment
  • The amount of debris and burnt material to clear
  • Whether water extraction and drying are needed
  • The amount of contents to be cleaned or packed
  • Access: upper floors, stairs and parking
  • Timing, including emergency and out-of-hours work

You'll have an itemised quote in writing, so you can pass it to your insurer. Photos help us give it quickly.

Fire Damage Cleaning FAQs

How soon should cleaning start?

As soon as the property is safe, the insurer is happy and the fire service has finished. Soot gets harder to remove over time, and water damage leads to mould. Don't start until you've checked with your insurer.

Will my insurance cover fire damage cleaning?

Often, yes, under buildings or contents cover, but it depends on your policy. Ring your insurer first, and ask what they require. Keep photos, receipts and the fire report.

Can I use my own cleaner or do I have to use my insurer's?

Many policies allow either, but some insurers prefer to appoint a contractor. Ask before you commit.

Is the smoke smell permanent?

Not usually. Removing the soot and residue, then treating the odour in fabrics and plaster, normally clears it. It can take more than one visit if the smoke is deeply embedded.

Is it safe to stay in the house?

Not until the fire service has said it's safe and the utilities have been checked. Even then, soot and smoke odour make the building unpleasant and may be an irritant, particularly to people with breathing problems.

What about the water used to put out the fire?

It needs removing and drying quickly. Left alone, it causes mould and damages floors and ceilings. Tell us where it came from.

Can you clean electronics and appliances?

Sometimes, but have an electrician or engineer test them before use. Don't plug in anything that was near the fire.

Do you do commercial fire cleaning?

Yes. Tell us your trading hours, the type of business and any records, stock or equipment to be saved.

How long does it take?

One room can be done in a day, but a whole property may take much longer, depending on the damage and how long the drying takes. You'll get an estimate with the quote.
