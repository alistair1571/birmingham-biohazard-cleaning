Needle sweeps and sharps clean up in Birmingham

Trained teams search for, collect and safely dispose of discarded needles and sharps, then give you a written record that the area was swept. Homes, gardens, communal areas and commercial sites across Birmingham and the West Midlands.

Found a needle, or worried there might be more? Don't pick anything up. Ring us for straight advice on the next step.

One needle in a garden is a worry. A property where you know needles were used is a bigger one, because you can't see the ones you haven't found. They end up in bedding, down the back of sofas, in coat pockets, behind radiators, under floorboards, in bin bags and in long grass. Anyone who then moves furniture, clears a room, cuts the grass or lets a child play outside is at risk.

That is why a needle sweep is a search, not a quick tidy. It means working through the space in a set order, with the right tools and light, collecting every sharp into an approved container and disposing of it lawfully. Nobody should do it by hand, and it isn't a job for a vacuum or a dustpan.

This page covers what most people need in the first hour: whether it's a council job or yours, what to do with a needle you can see, what to do if someone has been pricked, and what a sweep involves. Call us if you want a team arranged, and you'll have the cost confirmed in writing first.

What's included

  • A risk assessment of the area before any search begins
  • A systematic sweep of the agreed rooms, outbuildings, gardens or communal areas, including the places needles hide
  • Collection with long-handled tools into approved, puncture-proof sharps containers
  • Cleaning and disinfection of any surface a needle or blood has touched
  • Lawful disposal of the full containers by a registered carrier, with documentation
  • A written sweep record: what was searched, what was found, and when
  • Advice on follow-up sweeps if the property is still being cleared, or if the problem keeps recurring

You've found a needle: who deals with it?

This is the first question, and the answer depends on where it is.

  • On a street, in a park, on a pavement or in another public place: report it to Birmingham City Council. Councils generally remove needles from public land, and many say they aim to do so quickly. Give the exact location and roughly how many there are.
  • In your own garden, house or building: it's generally the owner's job, and the council won't usually attend. Some councils will quote for removal on private land, so it is worth asking.
  • In a rented home: tell the landlord or agent. Don't clear it yourself.
  • On commercial premises: tell the manager or owner, who should deal with it under their risk assessment.
  • In a school, nursery or workplace: tell the person in charge straight away, so trained staff can deal with it.

What to do right now

  1. Don't touch it.Gloves do not stop a needle going through. Don't bend, cut, recap or try to break it.
  2. Keep children and pets away.Mark the spot with something sturdy, such as a cone or an upturned bucket, if you can do so without touching the needle.
  3. Don't put it in a household bin, recycling or a vacuum.Don't flush it either.
  4. Make a note of where it is.A photograph helps, if you can take one from a distance.
  5. Stop clearing the room.If the needle turned up while you were tidying, stop. There may be more.
  6. Report it or call us,depending on who is responsible, as above.

If someone has been pricked or scratched

This is a medical issue, not a cleaning one. A discarded needle can carry blood-borne infections, although the chance of catching one is low. It's still worth treating seriously.

  • Encourage the wound to bleed gently, and don't suck it.
  • Wash it under running water. Don't scrub it.
  • Cover it with a clean dressing.
  • Get medical advice immediately, usually at A&E or through NHS 111. Treatment that reduces the risk of infection works best when it is given quickly, so don't wait to see how it feels. If you can, tell them what the needle was found in and where.
  • Don't take the needle with you unless a clinician asks for it.
  • For a pet, ring a vet.

This is general guidance, not medical advice. If you are unsure, ring NHS 111.

Why it's not a DIY job

People often ask if they can just pick it up with tongs and a plastic bottle. Here is why a sweep is different.

  • You can't see them all. The one you found is rarely the only one. Searching a room properly means going through bedding, clothing, cushions, bins and gaps.
  • Handling is where injuries happen. Most needlestick injuries come from handling, not from walking past. Trained teams work with long-handled tools and puncture-resistant containers, and don't reach blindly into bags and pockets.
  • Disposal is regulated. Sharps can't go in household bins or recycling. They have to be collected by a registered carrier.
  • A record matters. If a landlord, agent, insurer or council asks whether the property was checked, a written sweep record answers it.

What a sweep involves

  1. Assessment first.The team looks at the area, what has been used there and what else is in it, such as clutter, soft furnishings or standing water.
  2. A methodical search.The team works through the space in a set order, using good lighting and handheld tools, and checks the places needles are most often left: bedding and mattresses, sofas, pockets, cupboards, bathrooms, under floorboards and behind appliances, plus gardens, hedges, sheds, gutters and bins.
  3. Safe collection.Each sharp goes straight into an approved container. Containers are sealed before anything leaves the property.
  4. Cleaning.Surfaces that needles or blood have touched are cleaned and disinfected.
  5. Handover.You receive the sweep record and disposal paperwork.

In a hoarded or cluttered home, one pass isn't enough. Needles are uncovered as items are moved, so we plan staged sweeps alongside the clearance.

For landlords, agents and businesses

Landlords and letting agents. Before any inspection, repair visit, viewing or new tenancy, think about the people who will be entering the property. Contractors and your own staff shouldn't be sent in until it has been swept and signed off. Keep dated photos, a note of who found what and when, and the sweep record. It helps with deposit discussions, insurers and the next tenant.

Housing associations and managing agents. Communal areas such as stairwells, bin stores and lift lobbies can be swept on a schedule. If it's a recurring problem, tell us, and we'll talk through regular sweeps.

Businesses. For a car park, yard, retail unit or office, a sweep can be scheduled outside opening hours. The written record supports your own risk assessment.

If the property also needs waste or contamination cleaning, our biohazard cleaning page and human waste cleaning page explain what that involves, and the work can be combined in one visit.

Needle Sweep Service FAQs

Can I put a needle in my household bin?

No. Sharps must not go in household waste or recycling. If you find one on public land, report it to the council. On private property, arrange for it to be collected safely.

Will the council clear needles from my garden?

Councils generally deal with public land, and their position on private land differs. Ask Birmingham City Council what they will do for your address. If they can't help, or you need it done quickly, we can arrange a sweep.

Is it dangerous to walk past a needle?

The risk comes from handling or stepping on one, not from being nearby. Keep children and pets away, and don't pick it up.

How can you be sure every needle has been found?

Nobody can guarantee that, and we won't pretend otherwise. A methodical search of the places they hide gets as close as it can, and the sweep record shows what was covered. If you have reason to think more are hidden, such as in a cluttered room, tell us.

Do you do regular sweeps?

Yes, where there is a recurring problem, such as communal areas or commercial land. Ask for a repeat schedule when you request a quote.

What if my child or pet has been pricked?

Get medical advice straight away through A&E or NHS 111, or call a vet for a pet. Don't wait to see if anything happens.

Will the sweep be discreet?

Yes. If you'd like low-key arrival and access, say so when you call.

Do I have to be there?

No. Access can be arranged through you, a landlord or an agent.

Do you work with landlords and housing providers?

Yes, and we provide the record that agents, insurers and councils tend to ask for.

How long does a sweep take?

A small garden or single room may take under an hour or two. A cluttered house or a large site can take a day or more. Your quote includes an estimate.
