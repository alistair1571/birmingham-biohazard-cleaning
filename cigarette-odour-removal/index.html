Cigarette odour removal in Birmingham

Removal of nicotine and tar residue from walls, ceilings, floors and soft furnishings, followed by odour treatment. For landlords, tenants, buyers, sellers and businesses throughout Birmingham and the wider West Midlands.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

If you've already tried washing, airing and repainting, and the smell is still there, the cause is probably something nobody has cleaned yet.

Years of indoor smoking leave more than a smell. Tobacco smoke deposits a sticky film of tar and nicotine on every surface, soaks into carpet, underlay, curtains and mattresses, and settles into plaster, ceiling tiles, vents and the dust inside light fittings. Heat and damp bring it back out. That is why a flat can smell fine on a cool morning and stale by the afternoon, and why a freshly painted wall can turn yellow-brown again within weeks.

People reach this page for different reasons. A landlord has a property back from a heavy smoker and can't re-let it. A tenant wants their deposit back and is worried about being charged. Someone has viewed a house they love and can smell what the previous owners left behind. A pub, office or care home needs a room that no longer smells of the past. Whichever you are, the principle is the same: remove the residue first, then deal with whatever is left, then seal and redecorate in the right order.

If you'd like help, ring or send an enquiry. You'll see the price on paper before anyone is booked.

What's included

  • An inspection to find where the residue is heaviest, including ceilings, light fittings, window frames, vents and the backs of cupboards
  • Washing and cleaning of walls, ceilings, woodwork, windows, fixtures and hard floors to lift nicotine and tar
  • Deep cleaning of curtains, upholstery, mattresses and carpets that can be saved, with honest advice on those that cannot
  • Odour treatment of the room and any remaining absorbed smell
  • Guidance on sealing, priming and repainting so the stain doesn't come back through
  • A last sniff test after the property has dried and aired
  • Photos and a short report for a landlord, agent, buyer or insurer

Why painting and air fresheners don't work

  • Air fresheners, plug-ins and candles put a scent on top. They don't touch the residue.
  • Opening windows thins the air but leaves the film on the walls.
  • Painting over nicotine is the classic mistake. Nicotine and tar can bleed through ordinary emulsion, leaving yellow or brown patches, and the smell comes back through the paint.
  • Washing walls with a quick wipe usually moves the film around rather than lifting it, and it can leave streaks.
  • Steam-cleaning the carpet alone doesn't reach the underlay or the floor beneath.
  • Ozone machines are sometimes sold for this. They don't remove the residue, and they can irritate the lungs, so they are not suited to a home you live in.

What works is a proper sequence: dry clean or wash to lift residue, deep-clean soft furnishings, replace what is beyond saving, treat the odour, seal where needed, then repaint.

It is also in the places you don't look

When people smoke for years, the smell settles in:

  • Ceilings, where the smoke rises, and which are often the most stained
  • Light fittings, extractor fans and vents, which collect a sticky coating
  • Window frames and blinds
  • Behind and inside cupboards and wardrobes
  • Underlay and the floor beneath carpet
  • Curtains, mattresses and upholstered furniture
  • Heating and ventilation, so that the smell returns when the heating comes on
  • Dust that has built up over time

If a team cleans just the visible surfaces, the smell returns. That's why we ask about every room and all fabrics.

Is it a health problem?

Secondhand smoke is a recognised health hazard, and the residue it leaves is sometimes called thirdhand smoke. Studies have found nicotine and other tobacco residues in homes after the smoking has stopped, but research on how much harm that residue causes is still limited. It is reasonable to want it gone, especially for children, anyone with asthma, or anyone with a breathing condition. We won't tell you it is dangerous beyond what is known, and we won't tell you it's nothing. If you or your family have breathing problems, ask your GP. Treat this as a general guide rather than medical advice.

What can be saved?

  • Walls and ceilings: usually cleanable, then sealed and repainted. Heavily stained plaster or ceiling tiles may need replacing.
  • Hard floors and woodwork: generally cleanable.
  • Curtains, bedding and clothing: often rescued by laundering or specialist cleaning. Some heavy curtains and blinds are better replaced.
  • Carpets and underlay: carpet can sometimes be deep-cleaned. Underlay that has absorbed years of smoke often has to be replaced.
  • Mattresses and soft furnishings: often difficult to treat. We'll tell you what is realistic.

We won't promise the smell is 100% gone in every case. A property smoked in for decades may need new carpet, underlay, curtains and some plaster as well as cleaning. You'll be told that up front.

For landlords, tenants and letting agents

None of this is legal advice.

Landlords and agents. The strongest position comes from evidence. A dated check-in inventory with photographs, a dated check-out report, and quotes that itemise the work all help if there is a dispute about the deposit. Make sure your tenancy agreement states clearly whether smoking, and vaping, are allowed. Vape residue can build up too, and a standard no-smoking clause may not cover it. See the landlords page.

Tenants. If you smoked indoors, expect that cleaning may be needed. Having the property professionally cleaned and keeping the receipt and photos can help if a landlord later claims a deduction. If you didn't smoke there, or the smell was there when you moved in, show your check-in inventory and any photographs.

Deposit disputes. If the landlord and tenant disagree, the tenancy deposit scheme the deposit is protected in usually provides a free dispute process. Adjudicators look at evidence, such as inventories, photos and invoices, and consider fair wear and tear. Ask your scheme how it works.

Letting agents. Put the quote, photos and the cleaning report on the property file.

If you're buying, selling or moving into a smoked-in home

  • Buyers. If you can smell smoke, ask when the property was last occupied, ask whether the carpets and curtains are included, and ask us for a quote from photographs before you decide. Expect that a property that has been smoked in for a long time may need more than a coat of paint.
  • Sellers. Treat the smell before viewings, not after. Cleaning thoroughly and then repainting does more than airing the house, and photographs of the work can reassure buyers.
  • New occupants. Do the cleaning before you move furniture in. It is far easier in an empty room.

We don't give property valuations or promises about sale prices.

Cigarette Odour Removal FAQs

Can cigarette smell be removed completely?

Often removed or greatly reduced, once the residue has been cleaned and the odour treated. A property with decades of heavy smoking may need new carpet, underlay, curtains or some plaster as well.

Will repainting get rid of it?

Not on its own. Nicotine and tar can bleed through ordinary paint, and the smell stays in what's beneath. Clean first, then seal and paint.

Can I do it myself?

You can make a start on a light case, with detergent, hot water and plenty of rinsing. For a heavy case, ceilings, soft furnishings or a property you're about to let or sell, a specialist will get further.

Is vape residue the same?

Not quite. Vaping tends to leave a lighter, stickier film and a sweet smell, and it can still build up on surfaces. The cleaning approach is similar.

Will it come back?

If the residue has been cleaned and the property is no longer smoked in, it should not. If it keeps returning, something absorbent is still holding it.

How long does it take?

A one-bed flat might need a couple of days plus airing. A big house, or a long-term smoker's home, could need longer.

Can you help with a deposit dispute?

We can provide a written quote, photos and a summary of the work. We can't act as an adjudicator or give legal advice.

Do you clean commercial premises?

Yes. Pubs, offices, hotels and care homes tend to want work done out of hours, so tell us your times.

Will you have to replace my carpets?

Sometimes. We'll tell you which are worth cleaning and which aren't.
