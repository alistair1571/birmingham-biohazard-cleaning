Flood damage clean up in Birmingham

Water extraction, removal of soaked materials, controlled drying and disinfection after floods, burst pipes, appliance leaks and storm damage. For homes, landlords and businesses in Birmingham and the wider West Midlands.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

Water is still coming in? Stop it first if it's safe to, then ring us. We'll tell you what to do before anyone arrives.

Most flood damage isn't done by the flood. It's done by what happens in the days afterwards. Water soaks into plasterboard, floor screed and timber, moves sideways and up walls, and sits in places you can't see. Mould can begin within a couple of days. Wooden floors cup, chipboard swells, and a house that looks nearly dry on the surface can hold water for weeks.

If you're reading this with water on the floor, you probably want four things: to stop it, to know who to ring, to avoid making it worse, and to find out what it will cost and who pays. This page works through those in order, and explains what a drying job involves, because that is where most people don't know what to expect.

Get in touch and a team can be arranged for you, with the price in writing before the work begins.

What's included

  • Extraction of standing water, with a check for water that has gone under floors and behind skirting
  • Removal of materials that are too wet to save, such as carpet, underlay, soaked board and insulation
  • Moisture readings that map where the water has gone, and are repeated to show it drying
  • Drying with industrial dehumidifiers and air movers, set up to suit the building
  • Cleaning of affected areas, and disinfection where the water was dirty
  • Antimicrobial treatment where mould is a risk
  • Contents handling: what can be moved, dried or cleaned, and what has to be thrown away
  • Dated photos, moisture readings and a written summary for your insurer, loss adjuster or landlord

What caused it?

  • A burst or leaking pipe, tank or radiator
  • An escape of water from a flat above or from a neighbouring property
  • A washing machine, dishwasher or boiler leak
  • A blocked or overflowing bath, sink or toilet
  • Heavy rain and surface water entering through doors, air bricks or the garden
  • A failed roof, gutter, flashing or window after a storm
  • River or groundwater flooding
  • A flooded cellar or lower ground floor: see flooded basement clean up
  • Sewage or foul water: that is a different job, covered on our sewage clean-up page

What to do in the first hour

  1. Stop the source, if you can do so safely.Turn off the stopcock for a burst pipe, and the supply to a failed appliance.
  2. Think about the electrics.Don't touch switches, sockets or appliances in wet areas. If it's safe and the consumer unit is dry and reachable, turn off the power. If not, call your electricity supplier or an electrician.
  3. Keep everyone out of deep or dirty water.Floodwater from outside can carry sewage and chemicals.
  4. Record the damage on camerabefore you move anything. Mark the water level on walls if you can.
  5. Call your insurer or landlord.Many policies cover escape of water, but insurers sometimes want to see damage before it is cleared, and some have their own drying contractors.
  6. Lift what you can safely lift.Rugs, small furniture, cushions, books and valuables can be raised off wet floors or moved to dry rooms.
  7. Call us.We can talk through what can wait and what can't, and arrange extraction.

If you hear running water when everything is off, see wet patches in the ceiling, or notice a sudden jump in your water bill, you may have a hidden leak. A plumber can trace it.

Clean water, grey water or dirty water: why it matters

Where the water came from changes how it needs to be handled.

  • Clean water, from a burst supply pipe or a tank, is the least risky, but it becomes a hazard if it sits, because it picks up dirt and encourages mould.
  • Grey water, from a washing machine, dishwasher or sink, carries soap and some bacteria.
  • Dirty water, from river or surface flooding, drains or toilets, can carry sewage, bacteria and chemicals. Porous materials it touches usually have to go, and the area needs disinfecting, not just drying.

Tell us where yours came from. If it came from a drain or a toilet, our sewage clean-up page has more.

The part nobody warns you about: drying

Taking the water off the floor is the quick bit. Drying the building takes much longer.

  • Surfaces dry faster than structure. The visible surface can feel dry in days, whilst walls, screed and timber may need weeks, and sometimes longer for solid walls and thick floors.
  • Take out what slows it down. Wet carpet and underlay hold water against the floor, so removing them speeds the process up.
  • Don't rush it with heat alone. Fierce heating can dry the surface and trap moisture deeper in, and can damage timber. Proper drying uses controlled heat, air movement and dehumidifiers together.
  • Keep windows and doors managed. With a dehumidifier running, open windows let damp air in. Equipment is set up for the room.
  • Measure, don't guess. Moisture meters show whether a wall is really dry, by comparison with a part of the building that wasn't affected.
  • Don't redecorate or re-plaster too soon. Sealing damp walls leads to peeling, stains and mould. Insurers often want proof that the building is dry before repairs or redecoration begin.
  • Expect noise and electricity. Drying equipment is loud and uses power, which is a cost to ask your insurer about.

Clean and dry fast, then repair slowly. That's the best order, and it prevents a second round of damage.

Mould: the clock is ticking

Mould can start within two or three days in damp, warm conditions. It shows as dark spots on walls and ceilings, a musty smell, or flaking paint. Mould that keeps coming back usually means the building is still wet underneath.

  • Dry the area as soon as possible.
  • Don't paint over it.
  • Treat visible mould as part of the clean, and fix the cause.
  • People with asthma, allergies or weak immune systems should keep away from heavily mouldy areas.

Claiming on insurance

Treat what follows as a general guide only.

  • Ring early. The insurer may appoint a loss adjuster and drying contractors. Ask what they will cover and what you can arrange yourself.
  • Check your cover. Buildings insurance usually covers the structure, floors and fittings. Contents insurance covers belongings. Check for an excess.
  • Photograph and record. Keep the dated photos and receipts for emergency costs.
  • Don't throw things out until the insurer agrees, unless they are a health risk, and photograph them first.
  • Ask about alternative accommodation if you can't live there while the property dries.
  • If you're uninsured or underinsured, tell us. We can talk through priorities, and your council or local charities may be able to point you to support.

We can provide an itemised quote, moisture readings and photos to support a claim.

If you rent, own a flat or run a business

Tenants. Tell your landlord or agent immediately, in writing. Move your belongings if it is safe, and ask your own contents insurer about your things.

Landlords and agents. Escape of water is a common cause of voids. Keep records throughout, and look at whether your cover includes lost rent. See the landlords page.

Flats. Leaks travel down through floors. Find out where it started, and tell the managing agent, because more than one home may be affected.

Businesses. Give us your trading times and list the stock, equipment and records at risk. Your insurer can advise on business interruption cover.

What affects the price

  • Quantity of water, and how long it has stood
  • The type of water, since dirty water adds disinfection and disposal
  • The materials affected, and how much needs to come out
  • The area, and the number of floors involved
  • Drying time and equipment, which is often the biggest cost
  • Access and timing, including emergency call-outs

Deeper water that stands for longer tends to cost much more to put right, so speed saves money. You'll get a written quote before work begins, and a few photos speed things up.

How it works

Flood Damage Clean Up FAQs

How long does it take to dry a property?

Surfaces can dry in days, but walls, floors and timber often take weeks, and some buildings longer. The team measures moisture and tells you when it is dry enough to repair.

Will insurance cover it?

Often, particularly for burst pipes and escape of water, but it depends on your policy and the cause. Ring your insurer first, and keep photos and receipts.

Can I use a household dehumidifier?

It helps in a small, lightly damp space, but won't dry a flooded building. Serious damage needs proper equipment and measurement.

Will mould grow?

It can, if the building stays damp. Prompt extraction, drying and treatment greatly reduce the risk.

Can we live there while it dries?

That depends on the water, the electrics and how far it spread. Not while there is deep or dirty water, or while the electrics are suspect. Ask us or your insurer.

What if it came from the neighbour's flat?

Tell your landlord or managing agent, and your insurer. Responsibility depends on the cause, so keep records.

Can you do repairs, plastering and decorating?

No. We extract, clean and dry. Plumbing, plastering and rebuilding need other trades. Drying first lets them see what needs fixing.

Do you work with businesses?

Yes. Tell us when you need to be open, and we will plan around it.

How soon can someone come?

Someone answers around the clock. Say it's an emergency and you'll be given the earliest realistic slot.
