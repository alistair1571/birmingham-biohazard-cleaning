After rodent clean up in Birmingham

Removal of droppings, urine-soaked material, nests and dead rodents, followed by thorough disinfection and odour treatment. For homes, landlords and food businesses across Birmingham and the West Midlands.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

The rats or mice may be gone. What they left behind usually isn't. Ring us and we'll tell you what needs doing, and in what order.

Pest control gets rid of the rodents. It doesn't clean up after them, and nobody tells you that until the technician has driven away and you are standing in a kitchen cupboard, a loft or a cellar full of droppings, chewed packaging and a smell you can't place.

Rats and mice contaminate far more than they eat. Droppings and urine end up on worktops, in cupboards, behind appliances, in insulation and under floors. Some of what they carry, such as salmonella and the bacteria that cause leptospirosis, can make people ill, so wiping up with a damp cloth is not the same as making a space safe again.

This page covers the questions people ask once the treatment is done or under way: what's safe to do yourself, what has to be thrown away, what happens to dead rodents in the walls and under the floor, who pays, and how to know the job is finished. If you'd like a team to take it on, get in touch and the cost will be confirmed in writing first.

  • Cleaning and disinfection of every contaminated surface, including the cupboards, voids, skirting gaps and behind appliances
  • Removal of contaminated insulation, and replacement advice where it can't be saved
  • Checks of places that are hard to see, such as under kitchen units, behind panels and in lofts
  • Treatment of the smell from urine and from decomposing animals
  • Contaminated waste bagged, taken away by a registered carrier and documented
  • Photos and a short report of what was done, for a landlord, agent, insurer or environmental health officer

First: is the infestation actually over?

The order matters. If rodents are still there, cleaning first is a waste of money, because they will foul the area again. A reasonable order is:

  1. Treatment.A pest controller deals with the rodents, finds how they got in and blocks it.
  2. Confirmation.The pest controller tells you the activity has stopped. Some bait stations and traps stay in place for a while.
  3. Clean-up.Droppings, nests, dead animals and contaminated material are removed, and surfaces are disinfected.
  4. Repairs and proofing.Holes are sealed, damaged insulation and boards replaced, and chewed cables checked by an electrician.

If you are not sure that step 2 has happened, ask the pest controller. If you tell us when you call, we can work with them on timing. We don't remove live rodents or fit proofing, and we'll tell you if a job needs a pest controller first.

What to do right now

  • Don't sweep or vacuum droppings and nests. It turns dried waste into dust that you can breathe in.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone vulnerable out of the affected room, loft or cupboard.
  • Ventilate the space by opening windows for a while before going in, if you can.
  • Wear disposable gloves, cover any cuts and grazes with waterproof dressings, and don't touch your face.
  • Wet droppings with disinfectant before wiping if you have to deal with a small amount.
  • Wash your hands thoroughly afterwards, and don't eat, drink or smoke in the area.
  • Bag contaminated items securely and keep them away from food and children until they can be disposed of properly.
  • Don't try to move a dead rodent from inside a wall or ceiling void yourself.

For a few droppings on a hard, easily cleaned surface in a cupboard, careful wet cleaning may be enough. If it's in insulation, a loft, under floorboards, in a lot of places, or you are unsure how long it has been going on, call a specialist.

Is rodent waste really dangerous?

It can be, although serious illness is uncommon. Rat urine can carry the bacteria that cause leptospirosis (Weil's disease), which starts like flu and is rare in the UK but can become serious. Droppings and contaminated food can carry salmonella and other bacteria. The usual routes in are cuts and grazes, hand-to-mouth contact, and breathing in dust.

Keeping the dust down, covering cuts, wearing gloves and washing hands are the standard precautions, and they are the same ones the Health and Safety Executive recommends for work in rat-infested areas. If flu-like symptoms follow a clean-up in a rat-affected area, mention the exposure to your doctor. Treat this as a general guide rather than medical advice.

The kitchen question: what do I throw away?

Food areas are what most people worry about, and it's where cleaning needs to be most careful.

  • Throw away any open food, packaging that has been gnawed and anything that droppings or urine have touched, including pet food and bird seed.
  • Check sealed tins and jars, and wash and disinfect the outside before opening.
  • Empty and clean every cupboard and drawer where signs have been seen, not just the one with obvious damage.
  • Look behind and under the cooker, fridge, dishwasher and washing machine, where droppings and nesting build up.
  • Disinfect worktops, shelves and handles, and wash crockery and utensils that were in the affected area.
  • If in doubt, throw it out.

For a business, there is more to consider. Environmental health will want to see that the problem has been dealt with, and what was cleaned. Ask for photos and a dated record, and keep them with your pest control reports.

The smell, and animals in the walls

A rotten, sweet smell that gets worse in warm weather often means an animal has died somewhere you can't reach, such as a wall cavity, under floorboards or above a ceiling. It's common after poison baiting, and it is one of the main reasons people call us.

  • The smell usually fades as the body dries out, but it can last for weeks.
  • Sometimes the body can be reached through a floorboard or a small opening. We'll tell you honestly whether it can, and what it would involve.
  • Removal can mean lifting boards or opening a small section of ceiling or wall. Where that happens, the repair would be a separate job.
  • Air fresheners and sprays don't remove the cause.
  • Chemical neutralising of residual smell, once the source has gone, helps.

If you've noticed flies or maggots, say so when you call.

Insulation, wiring and hidden damage

  • Loft insulation. It absorbs urine and holds droppings and nesting material. If it is badly contaminated it usually has to come out, because it can't be washed.
  • Chewed cables. Rodents gnaw wiring, which can cause a fire risk. If you've seen chewed cables, or lights flicker or trip, get an electrician in.
  • Pipes and ducting. Check for gnawed pipes, flexible ducting and damp patches.
  • Stored belongings. Cardboard boxes, fabric and papers in lofts, garages and cellars are often nested in. Tell us what you want kept.

If you're a landlord, agent or tenant

Landlords and agents. Keep dated photos, the pest control reports and the cleaning record. If a tenant has complained, document what was done and when. A property shouldn't be re-let until the treatment is signed off and the clean is finished.

Tenants. Report it to the landlord or agent promptly, in writing, with photos. Many councils also offer pest control or advice, so ask yours. Don't throw out or bin the landlord's fittings, such as insulation or carpets.

Managing agents and housing providers. In a block, rodents move between flats through shared voids. We can work through the affected units and communal areas in order, with a record for each.

None of this is legal advice.

After Rodent Clean Up FAQs

Do you get rid of the rats and mice?

No. That is pest control. We clean up after the rodents have been dealt with. If you still have activity, call a pest controller first.

Is rodent waste dangerous?

It can carry bacteria that cause illness, so it should not be swept dry or vacuumed. Protective gear, damp methods and good hygiene matter. Serious illness is uncommon.

Can I clean up after mice myself?

A small amount in a cupboard or on a hard surface, yes, with care. Anything in a loft, in insulation, in a large area or in a kitchen full of food should be assessed by a specialist.

How do I know when it's safe to go back?

When the area has been cleaned, disinfected and dried, and the pest controller confirms the rodents are gone. We'll tell you when each area is ready.

What if there's a dead rat in the wall?

Tell us. Whether it can be reached depends on where it is. We'll say honestly what's possible and what it would involve.

Will insurance cover it?

Often not, because many home policies exclude pests and gradual damage. Check yours, and keep the quote and the pest control report.

Do you work in restaurants and takeaways?

Yes. Tell us your opening hours and what the environmental health team has asked for.

Will the smell go?

Usually, once the source and contaminated materials have been removed. A body in a wall can take longer.

How long does it take?

A kitchen or garage might take a few hours. A full loft or a long infestation may take a day or more. The quote gives an estimate.
