Who Cleans Up After a Death in a Property?

Published 8 October 2026

What happens first

When someone dies at home, the police, paramedics or doctor are usually involved, and the coroner may be informed. The funeral director arranges for the person to be taken into their care. None of these services clean the property.

Who is responsible for cleaning

The responsibility usually falls to the owner of the property. That might be a surviving partner, the family, the executor of the estate or the landlord if the property was rented. It is not a job for the family to tackle themselves, particularly where bodily fluids are involved.

When can the clean-up start?

Once the person has been removed and the property has been released by the police or coroner, if they were involved. Ask the officer or funeral director if you are not sure.

What a specialist cleaner does

  • Removes contaminated bedding, carpets and furnishings
  • Disinfects affected surfaces
  • Treats odours at the source
  • Disposes of contaminated waste lawfully
  • Treats personal belongings with care

Looking after yourself

This is a hard time. If you need someone to talk to, the Samaritans are available any time on 116 123.

This guide is general information, not legal advice.
