Sewage clean up in Birmingham

Emergency removal of foul water and sewage, full disinfection and drying, so your home or business is safe to use again. Across Birmingham and the West Midlands, day or night.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

Sewage inside the building is an emergency. Ring now, and we'll tell you what to do in the next ten minutes as well as who can attend.

Sewage is not the same as clean flood water. It is a mix of human waste, bacteria, viruses and parasites, and when it comes up through a toilet, floor drain or manhole it gets into carpets, plasterboard, floorboards and anything else porous. A mop won't make that safe. The smell, and the risk, stay behind long after the water has gone.

Most people who land on this page are standing in it, or have just been told it is coming. So the page is built around the questions that come up in the first hours: is this the water company's problem or mine, what do I do before anyone arrives, what will the water company and my insurer actually cover, and what is left for a specialist to do.

Ring us, tell us what you can see, and we'll arrange the right team for the property. You'll have the price in writing before anyone starts.

What's included

  • Pumping out and removal of standing foul water and solids
  • Removal of soaked carpets, underlay, rugs and other porous items that can't be made safe
  • Washing down and disinfection of floors, walls, skirting and fittings
  • Moisture checks, with drying equipment where walls and floors have taken on water
  • Inspection of voids, such as under floorboards and behind kitchen units, where sewage settles
  • Deodorising of the building after the contamination is out
  • Contaminated waste removed by a registered carrier, with records for your insurer or landlord

What has gone wrong?

  • Sewage coming up through a toilet, bath or shower after a blockage or surcharging drain
  • A flooded basement, cellar or garage after heavy rain overwhelms the sewer
  • Foul water in the garden or yard from a manhole or broken drain
  • A burst or failed soil pipe, or a leak from the flat above
  • A broken pump in a basement or cellar WC that has overflowed
  • A rental or HMO where a tenant or the managing agent needs it dealt with: Landlords and letting agents
  • A shop, restaurant, office or other business that has to reopen
  • Ordinary toilet overflow or vomit rather than sewer flooding: Human waste cleaning

Who is responsible: the water company or you?

This decides who you call first, and it catches many people out.

  • A public sewer, or a drain shared with neighbours. The sewer owner is responsible for the blockage or collapse. In Birmingham and most of the West Midlands that is Severn Trent Water. Their emergency line is 0800 783 4444 and is open at all hours.
  • A private drain that serves only your property. The blockage is generally the property owner's responsibility, even though the pipe sits outside the front door.
  • River, surface water or groundwater flooding. That isn't sewer flooding, and is handled by other bodies, such as the Environment Agency or the council. It can still contain sewage, so the hygiene advice below still applies.
  • Not sure? Ask Severn Trent. They can tell you which pipe is involved and who owns it.

It is worth ringing them even when you think the drain is yours. Don't try to rod or clear the blockage yourself while sewage is rising.

What the water company will and won't do

When the public sewer caused the flooding, the sewer owner usually carries out a basic clean of the outside area and disinfects hard surfaces such as paths and patios. Inside, it may pump out standing water and remove solids.

What it normally doesn't do:

  • remove or replace carpets, flooring or furniture
  • dry out the property
  • repair structural damage
  • provide alternative accommodation
  • pay for damaged contents

That gap is where most households are left stranded, and it is the work a specialist team and your insurer usually fill. In some cases the sewer owner also pays compensation under a statutory scheme, but it depends on the cause and on whether the flooding was internal or external, so ask them for the current rules for your address. This is general information, not legal advice.

What to do right now

  1. Get everyone out of the affected area, particularly children, older people, pregnant women, pets and anyone with a weakened immune system.
  2. Don't touch electrics or gas appliances that sewage has reached.Turn the power off at the consumer unit only if the unit itself is dry and the route to it is safe. If it isn't, call your electricity supplier. Appliances that were submerged need testing by a qualified electrician or Gas Safe engineer before use.
  3. Don't flush toilets or run taps and washing machines, which can push more waste into the system.
  4. Photograph and film the damagebefore moving or cleaning anything. Your insurer will want it.
  5. Ring your insurer.Do it the same day, even if you think the water company is at fault.
  6. Call the water companyif the flooding may be coming from a public sewer.
  7. Call us.We'll talk you through what can wait, what can't, and when the team can be with you.

If you do have to wade through it, wear waterproof boots or waders and rubber gloves, don't touch your face, and scrub your hands under warm running water with soap afterwards. Keep cuts and grazes covered.

Is it safe to stay?

It depends on how far it has spread. A small amount of sewage on a tiled floor in one room is a very different situation from a flooded ground floor.

  • Keep children and pets out of affected areas until the clean-up is done, and for at least two days afterwards.
  • Don't add heat to affected rooms in the first couple of days. Warmth lets bacteria survive longer. Ventilate instead, and keep windows open where you can.
  • Throw away food that has been in contact with sewage, and don't eat fruit or vegetables from a flooded garden.
  • Tap water is normally fine once you have run it for a few seconds. If you think the water supply is contaminated, say so when you call.
  • Gardens. Don't dig, rake or let children play on contaminated ground. How long to wait depends on the season and the soil, and runs from around a week and a half in warm, dry weather to nearly a month in cold, wet weather.
  • If you or anyone in the household is sick with stomach upset, diarrhoea or a fever after sewage exposure, see a GP and mention the flooding.

If the house is unusable, ask your insurer about alternative accommodation as early as you can.

Why sewage needs more than a bucket and bleach

Raw sewage is contaminated with E. coli, salmonella and norovirus, among other organisms. Porous materials soak it up. Chipboard flooring, plasterboard, insulation, carpet and underlay, mattresses and upholstered furniture can seldom be made safe once they have been saturated, and the smell can come back whenever the room warms up or gets damp.

Household bleach can disinfect a hard surface that has been cleaned first, but it doesn't reach into materials, and it can react dangerously with other cleaning products. A specialist approach is to remove what can't be saved, clean before disinfecting, allow proper contact time, dry the structure, and then check again. Skipping the drying stage is how mould turns up weeks later.

What stays and what goes

Hard, non-porous items, such as tiles, plastic, glass and sealed wood, tend to survive a proper wash-down. Porous items tend not to. As a guide:

  • Usually removed: carpets, underlay, rugs, mattresses, upholstered furniture, soft toys, cardboard boxes, damaged plasterboard and insulation
  • Often cleanable: tiled floors, sealed hard floors, plastic and metal furniture, many kitchen fittings
  • Needs a closer look: solid wood, laminate, appliances, books and papers of value

Tell us what matters most to you, such as photographs and documents, and ask for a list of anything taken away. Your insurer may want it.

If you're a landlord, managing agent or tenant

Landlords and agents. A tenant may say the property is unfit to live in, and you may need to act quickly. Keep dated photos, the name of the person you spoke to at the water company and a record of every call. If a tenant is displaced, check what your policy says about loss of rent. The landlords and letting agents page has more.

Tenants. Tell your landlord or agent straight away and in writing. Don't pay for a specialist clean until you have spoken to them, and don't remove the landlord's carpets or fittings without asking.

HMOs and flats. A shared drain can flood several homes. Tell the managing agent so one instruction covers the whole building, and tell us which flats and communal areas are affected.

If it's your business

Sewage in a shop, restaurant, care setting or workplace usually means closing until the area is cleaned and signed off. Tell us your opening hours, who has to be informed and what your insurer needs. We'll plan the work around when you want to reopen, and the waste documentation and written record of the work help with insurers and environmental health.

What affects the price

Sewage jobs vary from one room to a whole ground floor, so we quote each one separately. The main factors:

  • How much and how deep. A thin film on tiles is a small job. Several inches across a ground floor is much larger
  • What it has soaked into. Carpet and plasterboard add removal work
  • How long it has been there. Delay lets it soak deeper
  • Whether drying is needed, and for how many days
  • How many rooms or how large a building
  • Access and timing, including out-of-hours emergencies

You'll get a quote in writing before work starts, itemised so you can pass it to an insurer or landlord.

Sewage Clean Up FAQs

Is sewage dangerous?

Yes. It carries bacteria, viruses and parasites that can make people seriously ill, and children, older people, pregnant women and anyone with a weakened immune system are at greater risk. Avoid contact, and wash your hands carefully after any exposure.

Who pays for sewage clean-up?

It depends on the cause. If a public sewer failed, the sewer owner covers the basic clean-up of the area they are responsible for. Most of the rest, including soaked floors, contents and drying, usually falls to your buildings and contents insurance. Private drain faults are normally the property owner's responsibility. Check your policy and keep the quote and photos.

Will my home insurance cover it?

Often, yes, although policies differ and some limit cover for gradual damage or for certain drain faults. Ring your insurer the same day, and ask for an itemised quote from us to support a claim. This is general information, not insurance advice.

Can I clean it myself?

A very small spill on a hard floor can be handled with gloves, hot soapy water and a household disinfectant. If it has reached carpet, floorboards or plasterboard, or covers more than a room, we'd advise a specialist. You can't see how far it has gone.

How long does it take?

Removal and disinfection can take a few hours to a day. Drying a structure that has soaked up water can take several days. Your quote will give an estimate.

Is the water in my taps safe?

Mains supply is normally safe after running the tap for a few seconds. If you think the supply has been contaminated, tell us and the water company.

Will the smell go?

Yes, once the contaminated materials have gone, the area is disinfected and dried, and the odour treated. Smell from material left in place is the usual reason it returns.

Do you work out of hours?

The phone line is open around the clock. Tell us it's an emergency, and we'll tell you the earliest the team can be there.

Will it happen again?

If the cause was a heavy storm or a public sewer, it may. Ask the water company what they plan to do. At home, a non-return valve on your drain, keeping fats and wipes out of the system and regular checks can reduce the risk.
