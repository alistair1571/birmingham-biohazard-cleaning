After death cleaning in Birmingham

Gentle, discreet clean-up and odour removal after a death at home, so you don't have to face it yourself. Across Birmingham and the West Midlands, day or night.

Losing someone is hard enough. If they died at home, and especially if they weren't found for days or weeks, you may now be facing something nobody prepares you for: a property that needs specialist cleaning before anyone can safely go back in, and a smell that won't clear on its own.

This page answers the questions families, landlords and housing teams ask us most often in that first call: what has to happen before cleaning can start, who arranges it, what the team actually does, what happens to belongings, and what affects the cost. There's no judgement here, no pressure, and nothing you need to describe in more detail than you want to.

After-death cleaning removes anything the death has contaminated, disinfects every affected area and treats the odour at its source. Call us and we'll find the right team for the property and agree the price with you in writing first. The team deals with everything physical. You choose how involved you want to be.

What's included

  • Removal of contaminated bedding, mattresses, carpets, underlay and soft furnishings that can't be saved
  • A look beyond the visible stain, at underlay, floor joints, skirting gaps and neighbouring rooms, because fluids travel further than they appear to
  • Treatment of floors, subfloors and other porous surfaces, or removal where they can't be made safe
  • Cleaning followed by full disinfection of every affected room, with proper contact and drying time
  • Odour treatment at the source, not air fresheners or fragrance
  • Careful handling of belongings, with items set aside on request
  • Contaminated waste taken away by a registered carrier, so none of it is left for you to deal with

Situations we help with

  • A death at home that went undiscovered for days or weeks (an unattended death)
  • Contamination of carpets, flooring or furniture by body fluids
  • A relative's home that needs cleaning before probate, a sale or a clearance
  • A death in a rented home, for landlords and agents: Landlords and letting agents
  • Housing associations, managing agents and councils that need a fast, documented response
  • Flats and HMOs, where odour has reached communal areas or neighbouring homes

What has to happen before cleaning can start

It can be hard to know what's going on in the first few days. The exact order varies, but it usually runs like this:

  1. Call 999.For a sudden or unexplained death, paramedics confirm the person has died and the police normally attend. That is standard procedure and doesn't mean anyone is under suspicion.
  2. The coroner may become involved.Sudden or unexpected deaths are usually referred to the coroner, and a funeral director can't collect the person until the coroner gives permission.
  3. The person is taken into the care of a funeral director or mortuary.
  4. The property is released.Only then can cleaning begin. The police and coroner have no duty to clean the property once they leave. That is arranged by the owner, the family or the landlord. The officer in charge, or your funeral director, can tell you where things stand.
  5. Call us at any stage.You don't have to wait for the property to be released. Talking to us early lets us plan and quote, so there's no avoidable delay when it is.

If it all feels like too much, Samaritans are available free, any time, on 116 123.

Who arranges the clean-up, and who pays?

  • If you're a family member or executor, the executor is responsible for the person's property and contents. The cost usually falls to the homeowner, the estate or the landlord, so speak to the executor or a solicitor before committing, and ask for a written, itemised quote.
  • If the home was rented, the landlord normally arranges the clean, and landlord insurance may contribute depending on what happened.
  • Insurance sometimes helps. Some home and landlord policies contribute, and an itemised quote and photos can support a claim. If the home is now empty, tell the insurer, as policies often have conditions about empty properties.
  • You shouldn't be rushed. Nobody should pressure you into agreeing to anything on the day.

What affects the price

Every property is different, so there's no honest flat rate. These are the things that move the price:

  • How long the person wasn't found. The longer it was, the further fluids and odour tend to spread
  • What has been affected. Carpet and underlay only is a smaller job than floorboards, subfloor or concrete
  • How many rooms are involved, and whether odour has spread through a flat or building
  • How much has to be removed, such as a mattress, sofa or flooring
  • Access and timing, including upper floors, parking and out-of-hours call-outs

The quote costs nothing and lists every item, so it can go straight to an executor, a landlord or an insurer. To see what to ask any cleaner before you hire one, read our biohazard cleaning page.

Why the smell doesn't just go away

The odour after a death isn't only in the air. Gases and fluids soak into porous materials such as carpet, underlay, floorboards, concrete and plasterboard. That's why a room can seem fine, then smell again when it warms up or has been closed for a while.

Things that don't fix it: air fresheners, opening windows alone, bleach on its own, painting over stains, candles or incense, and consumer ozone machines, which can irritate the lungs and don't deal with the source. What does work is removing the contaminated materials, cleaning and disinfecting what remains, then treating the odour and checking again once everything has dried.

If you've noticed flies or other insects, mention it when you call, as it affects the plan.

Belongings, valuables and sentimental items

Cleaning and clearance are different jobs. If you only need the contamination dealt with, say so. If you also need the property cleared before probate or a sale, tell us and we'll quote for that separately.

If you're an executor, you may want to go through valuables, cash, jewellery and important documents before anyone else has access. Tell us what you'd like set aside and who should be there. Hard surfaces and many belongings can be cleaned. Porous items that have been contaminated, such as carpet, mattresses and soft furnishings, generally can't be saved. Ask for a list of anything removed.

How it works

  1. Tell us where you are.Ring or fill in the enquiry form. We'll want the address, whether the property has been released, roughly how long the person went undiscovered, whether it's a house or a flat, who can approve the work and how the team gets in. Share only what you're comfortable with.
  2. See the price first.A few photos usually let us give you a figure quickly; bigger or unclear jobs may need a visit. The quote sets out the cost, what's covered and how long it should take, and nothing begins until you've agreed it.
  3. The clean itself.A trained team in protective equipment seals off the affected area, takes out what can't be saved, scrubs and disinfects the rest, then deals with the smell. Most families choose not to be there, and that's absolutely fine; keys can be left with someone you trust. Depending on the property, it can take from several hours to several days, and bigger jobs may be done in stages.
  4. Handover.You get a summary of the work and the waste documentation, a clear record to give an executor, landlord or insurer.

After Death Cleaning FAQs

How soon can cleaning start after a death?

Once the person has been taken into the care of a funeral director or mortuary and the property has been released. Acting quickly helps limit how far fluids and odour spread into floors and furnishings, so call as early as you can.

Will the smell definitely go?

Usually, yes, once the contaminated materials have been removed and the source treated. If fluids have soaked into floorboards or the subfloor, extra work may be needed. Your quote will explain what's likely to be involved.

Can I just air the property and use air fresheners?

It won't fix it. Odour sits in carpets, underlay and floorboards, and fresheners only mask it. Trying to clean up body fluids yourself can also spread contamination, and contaminated items can't go in a household bin.

Will my neighbours notice, or can the smell spread to other flats?

In flats and HMOs odour can travel through shared walls, floors and communal areas. Tell us about any affected neighbours or shared spaces, and if privacy is a concern, say so and we'll plan arrival and access around it.

Do I need to be there?

No. Many families would rather not see the property, and don't need to. Keys can be passed to the team by someone you trust.

Who pays for after death cleaning?

Usually the homeowner, the estate or the landlord. Some home insurance policies contribute, and an itemised quote can support a claim. This is general information, not legal or insurance advice.

What happens to belongings?

Hard surfaces and many items can be cleaned. Contaminated porous items generally have to be removed. Tell us what you'd like set aside, and ask for a list of anything taken away.

When can we go back into the property?

Not until the clean is finished and checked. Contamination and odour can linger where you can't see them, so wait for the team to confirm each area is ready.

I'm not sure whether I need this. Should I still call?

Yes. Describe what you can and we'll give you a straight answer about what's needed, even if the answer is that you don't need us.
