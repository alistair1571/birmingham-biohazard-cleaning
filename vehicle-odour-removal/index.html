Vehicle odour removal in Birmingham

Deep cleaning and odour treatment for cars, vans, taxis and fleet vehicles affected by smoke, vomit, spills, damp, pets and other smells. Serving Birmingham and the wider West Midlands.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

The smell isn't in the air freshener's league. It's in the seat foam, the carpet, or the vents. Tell us what you can smell, and we'll tell you where it probably is.

A car is a small, closed box with a lot of soft material in it. Seats, carpet, headlining, door cards and the foam underneath soak up whatever is spilled, smoked or sicked into them, and then they release it every time the sun warms the cabin or the heater comes on. The ventilation system makes it worse by pulling air through the dashboard and pushing it into your face.

That is why the usual fixes disappoint. Hanging a tree from the mirror, a bomb-style spray or a quick valet can cover a smell for a day or two. If the source is still there, the smell returns. The job is to find where it is, take it out or clean it out, treat what is left, and then deal with the vents and the cabin air filter.

This page is for people with a smell that has already beaten them. It covers what is causing it, which smells need a garage rather than a cleaner, what a proper treatment involves, and what taxi and fleet operators need to know. If you want a team on it, ring or send an enquiry. You'll know the cost up front.

What's included

  • A search for the real source, including under seats, in the boot and in the spare wheel well
  • Deep cleaning of seats, carpets, mats, headlining, door cards and trim
  • Targeted treatment of what has soaked in, such as spills, pet accidents and bodily fluids
  • Odour-neutralising treatment of the cabin once the source is dealt with
  • Advice on the cabin filter and ventilation, which often carry the smell
  • Removal of items that cannot be saved, and advice on replacement
  • A final check before the vehicle is returned

What kind of smell is it?

  • Cigarette, cigar and vape smoke held in the headlining, seats and vents
  • Vomit from a journey or a night out
  • Spilled milk, food and drink that has gone off under a seat or in a door pocket
  • Pet accidents, wet-dog smell and hair
  • Damp and mould, from a leak, wet carpets or a blocked drain
  • A musty or sour smell from the air conditioning
  • Bodily fluids such as blood, urine or faeces: see biohazard cleaning and human waste cleaning
  • A dead animal in the engine bay, vents or under the car
  • A second-hand vehicle that smelled fine at the viewing

If the smell is cigarette smoke in a home rather than a vehicle, our cigarette odour removal page is the right one.

When the smell isn't an odour problem

Some smells are warnings, and cleaning won't solve them.

  • Petrol or diesel. Could be a fuel leak or a faulty fuel system, which is a safety risk. Don't drive it, keep sources of ignition away, and have a garage look at it.
  • Burning plastic, burning oil or a hot electrical smell. Stop driving and have it checked.
  • Sweet, syrupy smell with fogged windows. Can be a coolant leak from the heater matrix.
  • Persistent damp smell and wet carpets. There's probably a leak, such as a failed door seal, a blocked sunroof drain or a leaking windscreen. Cleaning will fix the carpet, but not the leak.

If you aren't sure, tell us. We'd rather say "see a garage first" than clean a car that will smell again next week.

Why it keeps coming back

  • The source is still in there. Liquid spilled on a seat soaks into the foam and padding, well beyond the surface. Dried milk and vomit under a seat can sour for months.
  • Carpet has layers. Underneath the carpet are felt and sound-proofing that hold moisture.
  • The vents are a delivery system. Smoke, mould and damp collect in the cabin air filter and the air-conditioning evaporator, and are blown back into the cabin.
  • The headlining holds smoke. Rising smoke lodges in the roof lining, which can't be scrubbed hard without risking it sagging.
  • Masking. Fresheners add a scent, and the underlying smell soon breaks through.
  • A wet clean that leaves the car damp can make things worse. Fabric that stays wet grows mould.

What a proper treatment looks like

  1. Find it.A careful look and smell, working inwards from the surface to the places smell gathers.
  2. Remove what can't be saved.Loose mats, boot liners and heavily contaminated pieces come out, and the car is vacuumed thoroughly.
  3. Clean for the type of smell.Different methods suit smoke, spilled milk, vomit and pet waste. Enzyme-based products, for example, are commonly used on organic stains.
  4. Treat the cabin.An odour-neutralising treatment is used on the interior once the source has gone. Some professional methods use ozone. Ozone is not for use with people, pets or plants inside, and the car must be aired afterwards.
  5. Deal with the vents.Treat the air-conditioning system, and replace the cabin filter if it is part of the problem.
  6. Dry and check.The car is left to dry properly, then checked for any remaining smell.

Cabin filters and the air conditioning

If the smell comes out when you switch the fan on, or is worse with the air conditioning running, the ventilation system is a likely culprit. Moisture that doesn't drain properly can let mould and bacteria grow on the evaporator, which gives a musty or sour smell. A clogged cabin filter can hold smells and recirculate them. Replacing the filter is a routine job for a garage, and running the air conditioning regularly, even in winter, helps prevent the problem. A garage can also deep-clean and re-gas the system. We'll tell you whether this part of the job needs a specialist.

Taxis, private hire, vans and fleets

A smelly vehicle is lost income, and it costs reviews, as well as a sense of pride.

  • Vomit in a taxi or private hire car. Tell us where in the car it happened and how long ago. Fast action stops it soaking into the foam. Drivers also ask about contamination from blood or bodily fluids.
  • Work vans and fleet vehicles can pick up diesel, food, waste or animal smells. Tell us about the load, because it affects what we treat.
  • Downtime. If the vehicle is earning, ask how long it will be off the road, and whether the work can be done at a convenient time. We'll tell you what's realistic.
  • Hire and lease vehicles. Keep photos and a record of the work. Hire companies may charge for cleaning, and a dated record helps if there is a dispute.

Buying or selling a vehicle

  • Buyers. A smell that was masked at the viewing is a warning sign. It may be smoke, damp or a leak. Ask for a quote based on photographs before you decide, and have a trusted garage look for water ingress.
  • Sellers. A car that smells of smoke or pets can put buyers off, and can affect what you are offered. Cleaning the source properly beats fresheners. We don't make promises about sale prices.
  • Part-exchange. Dealers notice smells. Treat first if you can.

What can be saved?

  • Hard trim, plastics and rubber mats: generally cleanable.
  • Fabric seats and carpets: often cleanable, if treated before the smell has gone deep.
  • Leather: cleanable, but it can hold smoke, so we treat it carefully.
  • Seat foam and sound-proofing: if soaked with bodily fluids, they may not be restorable.
  • Headlining: handled carefully to avoid sagging.
  • Heavily contaminated or mouldy items: may need replacing.

We'll tell you what's realistic before we start, and we won't promise a car that never smells again if the foam is beyond saving.

What affects the price

  • The type of smell and its age
  • How far it has soaked, surface or deep
  • The size of the vehicle, from a hatchback to a minibus or van
  • Whether the vents and air conditioning need treatment
  • Whether any seats, carpets or trim need to be removed
  • Whether bodily-fluid contamination needs a biohazard clean
  • Where the work takes place, and how urgent it is

You'll have a written price before work starts. Photos of the affected areas and a note of when the smell began are enough for most quotes.

  2. Quote and plan.You'll get the price, the method and the time needed in writing. We'll say whether it can be done where the vehicle is or has to be brought to a workshop.

Vehicle Odour Removal FAQs

Can the smell be removed completely?

In most cases it can be removed or greatly reduced once the source has been dealt with. Foam or carpet that is badly soaked may need replacing.

Will an ozone treatment fix it?

Ozone can help with some smells once the source has been cleaned, but it isn't a cure on its own, and it must be used with nobody in the car. It doesn't remove the stain or the residue.

Can I clean it myself?

For light, recent smells, yes. Remove the source, clean it, let it dry fully, replace the cabin filter and air out the car. For vomit, urine, smoke or mould that has soaked in, a specialist gets further.

Why does the smell get worse when I use the heater?

Warm air releases what is trapped in the fabric and the vents. It's a sign that the source is still there, or that the ventilation system needs treating.

Do you clean taxis and private hire vehicles?

Yes. Tell us how soon you need the vehicle back on the road.

Will you come to me?

That depends on the team and the job. We'll tell you whether the work can be done where the vehicle is, or needs a workshop.

What about blood or other bodily fluids?

That needs biohazard cleaning, which is more than a valet. Tell us when you ring.

Will it harm my car's interior?

The right products and methods shouldn't. We'll avoid soaking the headlining, electronics and leather.

Will my insurance pay?

Only occasionally, usually after an incident or a theft. Check your policy.
