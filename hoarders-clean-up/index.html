Hoarders clean up in Birmingham

Respectful hoarding clearance and deep cleaning, done at your pace, with you deciding what stays. Covering Birmingham and the wider West Midlands.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

Whatever state it's in, and however you feel about it, asking for help is okay. Nothing you tell us will shock us.

Most people who search for this are in one of two places. Either it's your own home and things have reached a point where you can't cope, or it's someone you love, a tenant or a property you've inherited, and you don't know where to begin. Both are hard, and both are far more common than people admit.

Hoarding isn't laziness or dirtiness. It usually means someone is overwhelmed, and the NHS recognises hoarding disorder as a mental health condition: a strong attachment to possessions, real distress at the thought of letting them go, and rooms that can no longer be used for what they're meant for. Asking for help is a big step, and it deserves a kind, practical response.

Here's what that looks like with us. You decide what stays. The team sorts and clears at a pace you can manage, in stages if that's easier, takes waste away lawfully and finishes with a proper deep clean. No lectures, no judgement and no surprises on price.

What's included

  • A private assessment, and a price in writing before any work starts
  • Sorting room by room into things to keep, things to donate or recycle, and waste, with you deciding what stays
  • Valuables, documents, cash and sentimental items found along the way set aside and shown to you
  • Clearing of rubbish, food waste, soiled items and bulky furniture
  • Removal of what hoarding can hide: pest droppings, mould, spoiled food and other biohazards
  • Clear exits, stairs and routes through the home, and working kitchen and bathroom access restored
  • Deep cleaning and disinfection of every affected room, with odour treatment
  • All waste, general and contaminated, taken away lawfully, with records
  • Work in stages, if a single push would be too much

Who we help

  • A worried family member, unsure how to raise it or what to do next
  • Someone living in the home who is ready for help and wants it done sensitively
  • A landlord with a tenant whose home has become unsafe or unhygienic: Landlords and letting agents
  • Housing associations, councils and social workers concerned about fire risk, access or health
  • Families clearing a home before a repair, a sale or the end of a tenancy
  • Executors and relatives who have found a hoarded home after a death: After death cleaning

If you're worried about someone else

It's natural to want to fix it quickly. With hoarding, that tends to backfire.

  • Start with their safety, not the mess. "I'm worried you could get hurt" lands better than "this needs clearing".
  • Don't clear things without their agreement. Throwing belongings away against someone's wishes can cause real distress, and it often doesn't last.
  • Encourage a visit to their GP. Many people with hoarding disorder don't realise they have a problem, and talking therapies such as CBT are the usual treatment.
  • Think small. One path, one room or one corner is a realistic first step.
  • Get support yourself. Hoarding UK (hoardinguk.org) offers free, confidential help, including support groups for families.

You're welcome to call us for advice on what a gentle first step could look like. There's no obligation, and we won't push.

If you're finding it all overwhelming, Samaritans are free, any time, on 116 123.

Fire safety comes first

Hoarded belongings can block escape routes and let a fire spread much faster, particularly paper and cardboard. Common ignition risks include candles, portable heaters set too close to belongings, overloaded extension leads, cooking hobs and smoking.

West Midlands Fire Service offers free Safe and Well visits in the home, prioritising the most vulnerable people. You can request one here. In the meantime, keep exits as clear as you can, avoid candles, and keep heaters well away from anything that burns.

What we find, and what happens to it

A hoarded home can hide a lot: paperwork, cash, jewellery, medication, photographs and keepsakes, alongside spoiled food, pests, mould and damage nobody has been able to see.

Anything that looks valuable or personal is set aside rather than binned, and you can tell us in advance what you're looking for. If the home belongs to someone who has died, the executor is responsible for the contents, so tell us who that is and we'll work to their instructions.

If animals are living in the home, mention it when you call so it can be planned properly.

What affects the price

Every hoarded home is different, so a fixed price list wouldn't be fair to anyone. What drives the cost:

  • Volume. A single cluttered room is a very different job from a property that's full from floor to ceiling
  • Contamination. Pests, mould, spoiled food and human or animal waste take more time and more protective work
  • Access. Stairs, parking and how far items have to be carried all count
  • How many people and days the job needs
  • How much has to be disposed of, including anything that needs special handling
  • Whether it's done in one go or in stages, which can spread the cost

You'll have a written price before anything begins. A few photos help, but a visit is just as good, and it costs nothing.

If you're a landlord, housing officer or social worker

Keep dated photos or video of the property and a record of your contact with the tenant. A written report and before-and-after photos from the clean can support a council referral, an insurance claim or a deposit discussion.

Take care before removing a tenant's belongings. Landlords generally can't do that without following the proper process, so take legal advice first. Councils' environmental health and housing teams may be able to help, and may be able to refer the tenant to hoarding or mental health support. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

How it works

  1. A private conversation first.Ring or send an enquiry. Tell us about the property, who lives there, and what you're hoping for. Share whatever you're comfortable with.
  2. A plan and a price.We look at the home, through photos or a visit, and set out what's needed, in what order, how long it should take and what it will cost. Safety comes first: exits, the kitchen, the bathroom, anywhere that's a fire or health risk. Nothing starts until you're happy with it.
  3. Sort, clear, clean.The team works through the agreed plan at your pace, sorting with you or with someone you trust, then removing waste and deep cleaning. Staged work means it can be paused and picked up again.
  4. Afterwards.You receive the waste records and, where useful, a short written summary of the work. We'll also point you to ongoing support, because a clean on its own isn't a cure. Your GP and Hoarding UK can help it last.

Hoarders Clean Up FAQs

Will you judge the state of my home?

No. The team works in homes in every condition, and the aim is to help, not to comment.

Do I have to be there?

You don't have to be, but many people prefer to be involved, at least for the sorting. It's your choice, and you can stay in the home during staged work.

Can the work be done in stages?

Yes, and for many people it's the best way. We'll agree a plan with you or your family, and the work can be paused if it gets too much.

Will you throw things away without asking?

Not in someone's own home without their agreement. If the owner has died or moved out, we work to the instructions of the executor, landlord or whoever has the authority.

What if I want to keep something?

Then it stays. Keep, donate and waste piles are agreed with you, and anything valuable or sentimental found along the way is set aside for you to decide on.

How much does it cost?

It depends on volume, contamination, access and how many people and days are needed. You'll get a free quote before anything starts.

How long does it take?

A single room can take a day. A whole property can take several days, or longer if it's staged around you. Your quote will include an estimate.

Will my neighbours know?

If you're worried about this, tell us and we'll plan arrival times and access with that in mind.

My relative or tenant won't let anyone in. What can I do?

We can't enter or clear a home without proper authority. A GP, social services or the council may be able to help in the meantime, and Hoarding UK can support you as a family member. You're welcome to call us for advice on the options.

Will it come back?

It can. Clearing the home deals with the space, not the reasons behind it. Support from a GP, talking therapies and groups such as Hoarding UK can make a real difference to keeping it under control.
