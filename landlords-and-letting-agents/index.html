Biohazard cleaning for landlords and letting agents in Birmingham

Fast, documented clean-ups for rental properties that have been left contaminated, so you can deal with insurers, deposits and re-letting with evidence in hand. Single properties and portfolios, throughout Birmingham and the wider West Midlands.

Opened the door and wish you hadn't? Ring us from the doorstep. We'll tell you what to do before you touch anything.

Every day a property sits unlettable costs you rent, and every week it sits contaminated increases the repair bill. The problem is that the situations landlords and agents meet are rarely the ones in the tenancy guide: a tenant who has vanished and left a house full of rubbish and needles, a death nobody noticed, a cannabis farm in the loft, a flat that smells of years of smoke, a sewer backing up into the bathroom, or a hoarded home that a council officer has just called about.

This page is written for the person who has to deal with it. It is about the practical side: what to do first, who has to agree to the work, what evidence you'll want, who normally pays, and how to choose between the different kinds of clean-up. Each specific problem has its own page, linked below, with the detail.

Get in touch and a team can be arranged for the property, with an itemised price in writing before work starts. We coordinate the work, and the specialist team that attends carries it out.

You've just found it: the first hour

  1. Stop, and go no further than you must.Contaminated properties hide needles, broken glass, soft floors, exposed wiring and heavy fumes.
  2. Ring 999 if anyone could be dead or injured,or if it looks like a crime scene. Leave everything as it is until the police release the property.
  3. Keep people out.That includes tenants' relatives, contractors, prospective tenants and other staff.
  4. Photograph from the doorway,with dates. Record the condition before anything is moved. This protects you with insurers and in any deposit dispute.
  5. Don't clear, bin or change anythinguntil you're sure of your position. Removing a tenant's belongings or changing the locks without the proper process can create legal problems of its own.
  6. Tell the people who need to know:your insurer, the owner if you're an agent, the lender if there is one, and your own staff.
  7. Call us.We'll talk through what you've seen and what to do next.

Who has to agree to the work?

This is where agents lose days. Before a team is sent, it helps to know:

  • Who is the client? The landlord, the managing agent, a housing association, an executor, or a lender.
  • Who can authorise spending? An agent usually needs the landlord's written agreement beyond a set limit.
  • Does the insurer need to approve it first? Many insurers want to see the damage, or appoint their own contractor, before work starts. Ask before you commit.
  • Who has the keys, and who will let the team in?
  • Is the tenant still in occupation, or is their position unclear? If so, take legal advice before anything is removed.
  • Are the police, coroner or council still involved? Cleaning can't begin until they have finished.

If you tell us these things on the first call, the quote and the timing are much quicker.

A note on tenancy law

Rental law has been changing. From 1 May 2026, fixed-term assured shorthold tenancies convert to periodic tenancies and section 21 "no-fault" evictions have gone, so possession generally has to go through the courts. Other parts of the Renters' Rights Act are being phased in over the coming months and years, including changes to how homes must be maintained. Damp, mould and disrepair standards for private landlords are being tightened.

We aren't lawyers, and none of this is legal advice. What matters here is the practical point: a property that is unsafe or contaminated can slow down getting a tenant out, getting a new one in, and defending a dispute. Dated evidence and a documented clean help with all three. For anything about possession, abandonment or a tenant's belongings, speak to a solicitor or a landlord association.

Who pays: you, the tenant, or the insurer?

There is no single answer. In practice:

  • The landlord usually pays up front to get the property back on the market, and then looks for recovery.
  • The deposit may cover some of the cost, if the tenant caused the damage. You'd normally need the original inventory, check-out evidence, written quotes and a fair-wear-and-tear allowance. Tenant Fees Act limits mean professional cleaning charges can be hard to claim unless the property was documented as professionally cleaned at the start. Disagreements that can't be settled go to the deposit scheme's adjudication.
  • Landlord insurance sometimes covers specific events, such as malicious damage, escape of water, fire or a crime. Policy wording differs greatly, so check yours. Some policies exclude cannabis cultivation or have conditions for empty properties.
  • The tenant or their guarantor can be pursued for losses beyond the deposit, through the proper channels.
  • The estate, where the tenant has died. An executor or solicitor is usually the contact.
  • A council or housing association will have its own rules.

Ask for an itemised quote so you can use it for any of these. Save everything. Claims can drag on for months.

The paperwork you should expect

Whoever you use, you want a complete file at the end of the job:

  • The final invoice, broken down the same way as the quote
  • Waste documentation, including who carried it away and where it went
  • A record of the rooms and areas treated
  • Dated photos from before and after
  • The job's risk assessment, if one was prepared

File it against the property. Insurers, lenders, councils and later letting agents have a habit of asking months afterwards. Our biohazard cleaning page has a longer list of questions worth putting to any provider, us included.

Clean is not the same as relettable

A clean-up is one step. Before a property goes back on the market you may still need:

  • an electrician's report and any rewiring, especially after a cannabis farm or fire
  • a plumber or drainage specialist after sewage or leaks
  • a pest controller, if rodents or pigeons are still active
  • a surveyor or builder for structural, damp or mould problems
  • redecoration, flooring and replacement furniture
  • the usual safety certificates and checks required for letting
  • a fresh inventory with photos

We'll tell you what we see that needs another trade. Sorting the clean first lets the others see what they are dealing with.

Landlords & Letting Agents FAQs

What documentation do I get?

The invoice, dated photos, a report of the work and waste paperwork. They are there to back up an insurance claim or a deposit conversation, though we can't say how any insurer or scheme will respond.

Do you work with managing agents and portfolios?

Yes. Tell us how many properties and how often, and we'll set out how it can work.

Can you clear the tenant's belongings?

Not without proper authority. If a tenant may still be in occupation, or their position is unclear, take legal advice first. Once you have clear authority, the team can clear and clean.

Will you work with my insurer?

Yes, we can provide the quotes and records they ask for. Many insurers also want to approve the work or appoint a contractor, so ask them first.

How quickly can you attend?

The phone line is open at all hours. Tell us it's urgent and what you've seen, and we'll say how soon a team can attend. We don't promise specific times without knowing the job.

What if I don't know what the problem is?

Send photos of what you can see, from a safe place, and we'll tell you what we think it is and what it is likely to need.

Do you deal with tenants directly?

We work for whoever is instructing us, usually the landlord or agent, and we don't discuss the property with tenants or neighbours.

Is the clean enough to re-let the property?

It makes the property clean and safe from contamination. Electrical, structural and legal checks are separate, and we'll point out what else is needed.

Will you take part in a deposit dispute?

We supply the quote, photos and report. Making the decision is for the scheme's adjudicator, and legal advice is for a solicitor.
