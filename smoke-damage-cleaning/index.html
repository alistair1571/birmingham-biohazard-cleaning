Smoke damage cleaning in Birmingham

Removal of smoke residue, soot film and smoke smell from walls, ceilings, soft furnishings and the hidden spaces smoke gets into. For homes, landlords and businesses around Birmingham and the wider West Midlands.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

No flames reached your home? The smoke may still have. Tell us what happened and we'll tell you what it is likely to have done.

Smoke damage doesn't need a big fire. A pan left on the hob, a fire next door, a boiler that puffed back, a wood burner flue that failed, or a bin fire outside a window can leave a whole home smelling of smoke and coated in an invisible film. You may have aired it for days, washed the curtains and painted a wall, and still smell it every time the heating comes on.

That is because smoke is not just a smell. It is a mix of fine particles and gases that settle on every surface, soak into anything porous and travel into places you can't see: behind kitchen units, under floors, into wall cavities, along ducts and through the loft. While the residue stays, the smell comes back. Cleaning it away is the only thing that makes it stop.

This page is for the many people whose home was hit by smoke without much burning. If there was real fire damage as well, read our fire damage cleaning page first, because the order of work is different. Otherwise, you're in the right place. Get in touch and the cost will be agreed in writing before anyone is sent.

What's included

  • An inspection to find how far the smoke has gone, including the rooms you wouldn't think to check
  • Cleaning of walls, ceilings, floors, windows, light fittings, vents and fixtures, using methods suited to the residue
  • Cleaning or specialist laundering of curtains, soft furnishings and clothing that can be saved
  • Treatment of voids and cavities where smoke smell is trapped
  • Deodorising methods aimed at the source of the smell, not covering it up
  • Honest advice on which items can be restored and which are better replaced
  • A final smell check once the work is done, and waste taken away lawfully
  • Photos and a written summary for your insurer, landlord or managing agent

What caused it?

  • A kitchen fire or burnt pan that has filled the home with smoke
  • A fire in a neighbouring flat or house, with smoke entering through shared walls, vents or the loft
  • An electrical or appliance fault that smouldered rather than burned
  • A chimney, flue or wood burner problem that sent smoke back into the room
  • A boiler or heater fault that left a sooty film
  • A small fire put out quickly, such as a bin, shed or car fire close to the house
  • A fire elsewhere in the building or on a neighbouring site that has pushed smoke through the whole block
  • A rental property that has smoke damage from a previous occupant: Landlords and letting agents

If the smell is cigarette smoke rather than fire smoke, our cigarette odour removal page covers that.

Why the smell keeps coming back

If you've tried to deal with it and failed, here is why.

  • Air fresheners and candles just add another smell on top.
  • Opening windows clears the air, but not the residue on surfaces.
  • Washing walls with household cleaner can smear the film, particularly if it's greasy.
  • Painting over it seals some of the residue in, and the smell often returns through the paint, especially in warm or humid weather.
  • Machines that release ozone are sometimes sold for the job. They can irritate the lungs, don't remove the residue and are not suitable in a home you are living in.
  • Cleaning only the room that was hit leaves smoke in the next one, in the cupboards and in the roof space.

What works is removing the residue from the surfaces, cleaning or replacing what has absorbed it, treating the spaces behind and around, and then checking for the smell again once everything is dry.

Not all smoke is the same

The way a fire burned decides what is left behind, and so how it has to be cleaned. As a rough guide:

  • Dry, powdery soot from a fast, hot fire sits on top of surfaces and can often be lifted with careful dry cleaning. Wiping it wet can smear it.
  • Sticky, smeary residue from slow, smouldering fires, such as electrical faults, is harder to remove and clings to porous materials.
  • Greasy residue from kitchen fires coats cupboards, extractor hoods and walls, and carries a strong smell.
  • Oily soot from heating equipment leaves dark staining and a persistent smell.

You don't need to know which one you have. A look at the room and a test on a small area usually tells the team what to use.

What to do now

  1. Ventilateif it's safe, but don't rely on that to solve it.
  2. Don't wipe soot or residue off walls with a damp cloth.It tends to spread it.
  3. Don't switch on heating or fansthat could carry residue around until the source has been checked.
  4. Get any boiler, flue or appliance involved checkedby a Gas Safe engineer, HETAS-registered installer or qualified electrician before using it again.
  5. Photograph the damageand write down what has been affected.
  6. Tell your insurer, or your landlord or agent,before you throw anything away.
  7. Wash soft items only if it's agreed.Heavily smoke-affected clothing sometimes needs specialist cleaning instead.
  8. Call us.We can explain what to expect and what's worth doing before the team arrives.

If anyone in the home has asthma or another breathing condition and is struggling, stay somewhere else until it has been cleaned, and get medical advice if you need it.

What can be saved?

  • Hard surfaces such as tiles, glass, metal, painted walls and ceilings can usually be cleaned. Heavily affected walls may still need repainting after cleaning.
  • Curtains, bedding and clothing can often be rescued by laundering or specialist cleaning.
  • Carpets, rugs and upholstery can sometimes be cleaned. Where smoke has gone deep into the padding, replacement may be the better option.
  • Mattresses, pillows and soft toys are often difficult to restore.
  • Food, open packets, cosmetics and medicines that were exposed to smoke should generally be thrown away.
  • Electronics should be checked before use, because soot can corrode contacts.

Claiming on insurance

Check your policy, because smoke damage from an insured event is often covered, but the details differ.

  • Report it early, and give the fire service's incident number if there was one.
  • Don't bin anything until the insurer or loss adjuster has agreed to it. Sometimes they will want to see the item.
  • Ask whether they will cover cleaning, replacement or redecoration, or a mix, and get the answer in writing. Disputes tend to be about whether something is cleaned or replaced.
  • Keep a list of what was affected, with photos.
  • Chimneys and wood burners. Many policies expect a yearly sweep with a certificate, so keep it. The condition can matter if the claim came from a flue problem.
  • If you and a neighbour, or the building owner, have different insurers, ask whether more than one claim is needed.
  • Keep receipts for emergency costs. Insurers sometimes help with these.

This is general guidance, not insurance advice. If you aren't happy with the insurer's decision, there are free complaint routes, and consumer services can point you to them.

If you rent, lease a flat or run a business

Tenants. Let the landlord or agent know, plus your contents insurer if you have one. Leave the landlord's walls, carpets and fittings alone until you've spoken.

Landlords and agents. Smoke damage can stop a property being let. Keep the photos, reports and the record of the clean. See the landlords page.

Flats and shared buildings. Smoke from a neighbouring flat reaches you through shared walls, vents, ceiling voids and corridors. Report it to whoever manages the building, so corridors, voids and the homes next door get looked at in one go.

Businesses. Smoke can taint stock, packaging and food. Give us your opening times and a list of stock to save, and the work can fit around trading. A food business will want a dated cleaning record before it reopens.

Smoke Damage Cleaning FAQs

Will the smoke smell go completely?

Usually, yes, or nearly so, when the residue has been removed and the source treated. Heavily affected soft furnishings may have to be replaced, and deep smoke in plaster or insulation can need more than one visit.

Why can I still smell smoke after repainting?

Because the residue was left under the paint. Cleaning first, and then painting if needed, is the right order.

Can I clean smoke damage myself?

Light, dry soot on a hard surface can sometimes be handled with care. A greasy or widespread problem, anything involving soft furnishings or hidden spaces, and anything an insurer is paying for is better left to a specialist.

Is smoke damage dangerous to health?

Residue and fumes can irritate the nose, throat and lungs, particularly for people with asthma or other breathing conditions. Keep vulnerable people out until it has been cleaned. Take that as a general guide rather than medical advice.

How long does it take?

A room or two can take a day. A whole house, or one with smoke in the voids, can take several days. The quote includes an estimate.

Do you clean my belongings too?

Many items can be cleaned or sent for specialist cleaning. We'll tell you what is realistic.

Will my insurance cover it?

Often, for an insured event, but not always. Ask your insurer before you start, and keep the paperwork.

Is it different from fire damage cleaning?

Yes. Smoke damage cleaning is for when smoke is the main problem. If there is fire, heat or water damage as well, see fire damage cleaning.

Do you work in flats and shared buildings?

Yes. Tell us who else is affected and who can authorise the work.
