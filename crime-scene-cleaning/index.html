Crime scene cleaning in Birmingham

Discreet, thorough clean-up of blood, bodily fluids and everything else an incident leaves behind, as soon as the police hand the property back to you. Covering Birmingham and the West Midlands around the clock.

After an assault, a serious accident or a sudden death at a property, the police and emergency services do their work and leave. What they leave behind is blood and other fluids, in places you can see and places you can't, and a home or business that no longer feels safe or normal. Nobody is ready for that, and most people have no idea where to start.

Once a property is released, cleaning it isn't the police's job, or the coroner's. Arranging it usually falls to the owner, to the landlord in a rented home, or to the business operator. That can feel like one more burden, so this page covers what you most need to know: what to do while you wait, what the police leave behind, who pays, and what the clean involves.

You may also see this work called trauma cleaning or crime scene clean-up. It's the same job: removing blood, bodily fluids and contaminated materials, disinfecting every affected surface and dealing with the odour. Ring us, and we'll line up the right team and give you the price in writing before anyone sets foot inside.

What's included

  • Removal of blood and other contaminated material, including from porous surfaces such as carpet, underlay and upholstery
  • Cleaning and disinfection of walls, floors, furniture and fittings, including edges and gaps where fluids run
  • Cleaning of marks and residue left by the investigation, such as fingerprint powder
  • Honest advice on what can be saved and what can't
  • Odour treatment once the source has gone
  • Contaminated waste collected and disposed of lawfully, with documentation
  • Quiet, private working throughout

When this service applies

  • After an assault or other violent incident at a home or business
  • After a serious accident that has left blood or bodily fluids
  • After a sudden or traumatic death, including after death cleaning
  • After a break-in or disturbance in which someone was hurt
  • In a rented home or a commercial property, where the landlord, agent or business owner needs it dealt with: Landlords and letting agents
  • Any time the police have finished and the property has been released to you

While you wait: before you clean anything

Cleaning too early can interfere with an investigation, so hold back until the police say it's fine.

  1. Don't clean, move or throw anything away until the officer in charge confirms the scene has been released.If you're not sure, ask them directly.
  2. Follow any police adviceabout what to leave untouched. They may ask you not to disturb certain areas.
  3. Keep children and pets awayfrom the affected area.
  4. Don't try to clean it yourselfonce it's released. Household cleaners and vacuums can push contamination into places you can't reach, and contaminated items shouldn't go out with your normal rubbish.
  5. Keep the police crime reference number to hand.Insurers and landlords may ask for it.
  6. Call us early.We can talk it through, plan and give you a quote before the property is released, so nothing waits on us afterwards.

If you've been affected by what happened, Victim Support is a free, independent charity for people affected by crime and traumatic incidents in England and Wales. Its Supportline is open 24 hours on 08 08 16 89 111. Samaritans are also free, any time, on 116 123.

What the police leave behind

Police investigate and secure a scene, but they don't clean it. Once the property is released, you may be left with:

  • Blood and other fluids on visible surfaces
  • Fluids you can't see, which run into carpet edges, floor gaps and the back of skirting boards, and soak through into underlay and floorboards
  • Residue from the investigation, such as fingerprint powder and marks
  • Odour, which can build in a closed, warm room even when the surface looks clean

That last point is why wiping over a stain rarely finishes the job. A surface that looks clean can still be contaminated underneath, and the smell and the reminder stay until the source is removed.

Who pays for crime scene cleaning?

There's no single answer, and it depends on who owns the property and what's covered.

  • Homeowners. Your home insurance may help, depending on what happened and what your policy covers. Check with your insurer first, and keep the crime reference number and an itemised quote.
  • Tenants. Tell your landlord straight away. Don't arrange or pay for a clean before you've spoken to them.
  • Landlords and agents. The property usually needs to be properly cleaned before it's re-let, and landlord insurance may help with the cost.
  • Businesses. Some commercial policies contribute. Check your wording, and keep photos, the quote and the paperwork for the insurer.

Take your time. Ask for the price in writing, itemised, and speak to your insurer or landlord before you agree. This is general guidance rather than legal or insurance advice.

If it happened at your business

For a shop, pub, office, takeaway or workplace, the pressure is to reopen quickly without customers or staff seeing anything. Tell us your opening hours and who needs access, and where possible the work can be planned around them. You'll also want a written record for your insurer, and we can provide the quote and the waste documentation you need for that.

What affects the price

Crime scene cleans vary enormously, from a single room to a whole property, so there's no honest flat rate. The main things that move the price are:

  • What's there. Blood and bodily fluids need more work than residue from the investigation
  • How far it has spread, and whether it has soaked into flooring, underlay or walls
  • How much has to come out, for example flooring or soft furnishings
  • How many rooms are affected
  • How long it has been there
  • Access and timing, including out-of-hours work

The quote is free and broken down line by line, so it can be shown to an insurer or landlord. Our biohazard cleaning page has a checklist of questions worth asking any provider.

How it works

  1. Call and tell us where things stand.We need the address, whether the police have released the property, what type of premises it is, who has authority to say yes, and how the team will get in. Say as much or as little as you like.
  2. Agree the price.Photos usually let us quote quickly; bigger jobs may need a visit first. You'll see the cost, the scope and an expected timescale in writing, and nothing starts until you've said yes.

  4. You get the records.You're handed a written account of what was done plus the waste records, ready for your insurer, landlord or the police if they ask.

Crime Scene & Trauma Cleaning FAQs

Can cleaning start before the police have finished?

No. Work can only start once the scene has been released, and you can confirm that with the officer in charge. We're happy to plan and quote beforehand so you're ready to go as soon as it is.

Is it discreet?

Yes. We don't discuss what happened, and the team is there to clean, not to ask questions. If you'd like extra care over how and when the team arrives, tell us and we'll build that into the plan.

Do you clean businesses and rented homes as well as private houses?

Yes. That includes shops, offices, pubs, takeaways and rented homes. Tell us who owns and operates the property when you call, so we know who can approve the work.

Is it safe to stay in the property while it's cleaned?

We'd advise staying somewhere else until the affected area has been cleaned and checked. Contamination and odour can spread to rooms you're not using.

Will it look and feel normal again afterwards?

Cleaning removes contamination and marks from hard surfaces. Where carpet, underlay or flooring has been soaked, it usually has to be taken out. You'll be told what can be saved and what can't, and you can ask for a record of what was taken out.

How long does a crime scene clean take?

A single contained room can take hours. Larger or more affected properties can take days. You'll get an estimated timescale with your quote.

Is this the same as trauma cleaning?

Yes. Crime scene cleaning and trauma cleaning describe the same specialist work, and the same teams do both.

Will insurance cover it?

Possibly. Home, landlord and business policies vary, so check yours first and keep the quote and records.
