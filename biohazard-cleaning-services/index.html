Biohazard cleaning in Birmingham

Calm, discreet clean-up of blood, bodily fluids, human waste and contaminated materials, across Birmingham and the West Midlands. Call any time, day or night.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

You don't need to know exactly what to call it. Tell us what has happened and we'll tell you what happens next.

If you're searching for biohazard cleaning, something has happened at a property that can't simply be wiped up and forgotten. It might be a death at home, an incident the police have now left, a flat or house that has been neglected for years, or a rental handed back in a state no one should have to walk into. Whatever the reason, you're not the first person to be in this position, and you don't have to work it out alone.

Biohazard cleaning is the specialist removal and disinfection of anything that could carry infection or cause harm: blood and other bodily fluids, human waste, sharps, contaminated waste and the residue left after traumatic events. A mop, a household cleaner or a bottle of bleach can't make these areas safe, and the waste has to be disposed of properly.

Call us, tell us what you can, and we'll arrange the right team for your property, with a clear price before any work begins. No judgement, no pressure, and no need to go into detail you'd rather not.

What's included in a biohazard clean

  • A clear, no-obligation quote, itemised so you can see exactly what it covers
  • Removal and bagging of contaminated items that can't be saved, such as soft furnishings, mattresses, carpet and underlay
  • Cleaning and disinfection of every affected surface, including the less obvious places fluids travel to, like skirting boards and the edges of flooring
  • Odour treatment once the source has been removed
  • Collection and disposal of contaminated waste by a registered waste carrier, with the paperwork to prove it
  • A final check, and confirmation of what was done, so you know the area is safe to use again

When you might need biohazard cleaning

  • After a death at home, particularly where the person wasn't found for some time: After death cleaning
  • After an accident, assault or other traumatic incident, once the police have released the property: Crime scene and trauma cleaning
  • Where there is human waste, vomit or sewage: Human waste cleaning and sewage clean-up
  • After hoarding or severe neglect, handled at your pace and without judgement: Hoarders clean up
  • After a cannabis farm, squat or drug use has left a property contaminated: Cannabis farm clean up
  • Where needles or sharps have been left in a home, garden or communal area: Needle sweep service
  • At the end of a tenancy, when a property has been left contaminated: Landlords and letting agents

If you've just discovered it: what to do in the first hour

It's hard to think clearly in the first few hours. Here's what helps, in order.

  1. If someone has died or been hurt, or you think a crime has taken place, call 999 first.Don't disturb anything. Biohazard cleaning can only start once the police and coroner have released the property.
  2. Keep people and pets out of the affected area, and close the door if you can.
  3. Don't try to clean it yourself.Avoid vacuuming, mopping or using household sprays, which can spread contamination. Don't put contaminated items in a household bin or skip. If you do have to go in, wear disposable gloves and wash your hands thoroughly afterwards.
  4. Once the property has been released, take photos of the affected areasif you feel able to. They help us quote quickly and can support an insurance claim.
  5. Call us.We can talk you through what's needed and what happens next, even if you're not sure yet whether it's a biohazard job.

After the police or coroner have left: who arranges the clean-up?

A lot of people assume the police or the council will deal with the cleaning once they've finished. They won't. Police and coroners have no duty to clean a property when they release it, so arranging it usually falls to the property owner or, in a rented home, the landlord. When you're already grieving or in shock, that can feel like one more blow.

A few practical points:

  • If you're a landlord or letting agent, you'll normally need the property properly cleaned before anyone can re-enter or re-let it. Your landlord or buildings insurance may help with the cost, depending on what happened.
  • If you're family, don't feel pressured to agree to anything quickly. Ask for a written, itemised quote, and speak to the landlord, the person dealing with the estate or your home insurer before you commit.
  • If you're a housing provider or council officer, we can work to your timescales and provide the documentation you need for your records.

How it works

  1. Get in touch.Call or send an enquiry. We'll ask where the property is, what has happened (in as much or as little detail as you're comfortable with), whether the police or coroner have released it, and whether any children, pets or vulnerable people are involved.
  2. Get a clear quote.Photos often let us quote quickly; for larger jobs a team may visit first. You'll get the price, the scope and a realistic timescale in writing before anything starts.
  3. The clean.A trained team arrives in protective equipment, contains the area, removes contaminated materials, cleans and disinfects every affected surface and treats odours. In most cases you don't need to be there; access can be arranged through a family member, landlord or agent.
  4. Paperwork and sign-off.You receive confirmation of what was done and the waste documentation, so you have a clear record.

What you should receive at the end

Whoever you use, you should come away with:

  • An itemised invoice that matches the quote
  • The waste carrier's registration details and a copy of the waste paperwork. Contaminated waste from a biohazard clean is usually classed as hazardous, and hazardous waste must travel with a consignment note to an authorised site
  • A written summary of the areas treated and the work carried out
  • Before and after photos, where appropriate

Keep these together. Landlords, letting agents and insurers sometimes ask for them months later, and a claim can be delayed without evidence that the work was done properly. If a company can't produce them, ask why.

What affects the price

Every job is different, so every job is quoted individually. The things that move the price most are:

  • The type of contamination, and how far it has spread, including whether it has soaked through flooring or into walls
  • How long it has been there. The longer a job waits, the further it tends to spread and the harder odours become to remove
  • How much has to be removed and disposed of, such as carpets, mattresses and furniture
  • The size of the area and how many rooms are affected
  • Access, such as upper floors, limited parking or restricted entry
  • Timing, as urgent and out-of-hours call-outs can cost more

Our quotes are free and itemised, so you can compare them fairly and pass them to an insurer or landlord. Ask what happens if the job turns out bigger than expected, so there are no surprises later.

How to check any biohazard cleaner before you hire

It's worth asking these questions of anyone you speak to, including us:

  • Are they a registered waste carrier, and can they give you the registration number? (Carriers can be checked on the Environment Agency's public register.)
  • Do they have public liability insurance, and can they show you the certificate?
  • Will they provide a written risk assessment or method statement for the job?
  • Is the quote itemised, in writing, and clear about what's included?
  • What waste documentation will you receive afterwards?
  • What training does the team hold in biohazard and infection control?

Biohazard Cleaning FAQs

Can I clean up a biohazard myself?

We strongly advise against it. Blood and bodily fluids can carry viruses and bacteria, and household products don't disinfect to the standard needed. Contamination can also sit unseen under flooring or behind skirting boards, and contaminated waste can't go in your household bin, so it has to be removed by a registered carrier.

How much does biohazard cleaning cost in Birmingham?

It depends on what has happened, how large the affected area is, how much has to be removed and how urgent the job is. A small, contained clean costs far less than a whole-property clean after a long-undiscovered death. We give a free, itemised quote, and photographs help us do it quickly.

How long does biohazard cleaning take?

A small, contained job can be dealt with in a few hours. Larger or more complex jobs, such as hoarding or a death where the person wasn't found for some time, can take several days. You'll be given an estimated timescale with your quote.

Who is responsible for cleaning after a death or incident at a property?

Not the police or the coroner. Once they release the property, arranging the clean usually falls to the owner or, in a rented home, the landlord. If you're not sure where you stand, call us and we'll talk through your situation, though we can't give legal advice.

Is biohazard cleaning covered by insurance?

Sometimes. Some home and landlord policies contribute, depending on the cause and the policy wording. Speak to your insurer before you commit, ask for an itemised quote and keep the paperwork, as insurers often want evidence that a qualified provider carried out the work. This is general information, not insurance advice.

When is it safe to go back into the property?

Not until it has been cleaned and checked. Areas that look clean can still hold contamination. The team will tell you when each area is safe to use again.

Will my neighbours know?

Tell us if privacy matters and access and arrival can be arranged with that in mind.

Do I need to be there?

Usually not. If you'd rather not be at the property, we can arrange access through a family member, landlord or agent.

Can carpets, furniture and belongings be saved?

Hard surfaces can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Porous items such as carpet, underlay, mattresses and soft furnishings that have been contaminated generally have to be removed. The team will tell you what can and can't be saved, so you can set aside anything of sentimental value.
