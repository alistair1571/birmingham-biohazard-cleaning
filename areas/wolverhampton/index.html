Wolverhampton is a city of about 264,000 people, one of the three cities in the West Midlands county alongside Birmingham and Coventry. It has been a working town since Lady Wulfrun was granted the land in 985, and it still holds almost every kind of home: tall blocks, 1920s council estates, student terraces, leafy suburbs, canalside streets and industrial units.

When something goes wrong in a property, most people don't know which of several organisations to speak to, and the order matters. A cleaner cannot start while the police or coroner still hold a property, a housing provider may need to approve work in a block, and a landlord has paperwork to keep. This page puts those contacts first, then explains what happens when you ring, and then sorts the city by the types of home and situation we are most often asked about.

What happens after you ring: five stages

We can't promise a fixed arrival time, because it depends on the job and the access, but the process is the same every time.

  1. The conversation.We ask what has happened, where, whose property it is, who can approve the work and what you've seen. You don't need technical terms; "a strong smell upstairs and flies at the window" is plenty.
  2. Photos or a visit.Photographs from the doorway are usually enough for a first view. If the job is large, a team may look in person.
  3. A written, itemised quote.Each task is a separate line: removal, cleaning, disinfection, odour treatment, waste disposal. Nothing starts until you've approved it.
  4. Booking and access.The start date, who holds the keys, parking, lifts and anything to be set aside, such as papers, jewellery or keepsakes, are agreed in advance.
  5. The work and the records.The team attends, and afterwards you receive dated before-and-after photographs and a short report, with the waste paperwork where it applies.

If the scope changes on site, work pauses until you've agreed a revised price. An estate, landlord, insurer or housing provider may have to approve the quote first, so it helps to ask them early.

Student houses, HMOs and tenancy turnover

The University of Wolverhampton has its main campus in the city centre, and a large share of the terraced housing near it is let to students and shared households. That creates a pattern.

  • Late summer is the crunch. Tenancies often end together, and landlords want properties ready for September. Book early and give us a date.
  • Typical finds. Food waste, overflowing bins, blocked drains, mould in bathrooms, bodily fluids on mattresses, and sometimes needles, damage or an abandoned grow.
  • Licensing. The council's guidance from 2018 says a house shared by five or more people from two or more households, sharing amenities, needs a mandatory HMO licence, with civil penalties of up to £30,000 for unlicensed properties. Check the council's current rules for your property.
  • Evidence matters. Take dated photos before anything is touched, hold on to the check-in report, and ask for before-and-after pictures with an itemised bill. A deposit scheme or insurer will look for exactly these.
  • Belongings. Check your legal position before disposing of anything a tenant leaves behind.
  • Agents. If you manage several houses, tell us up front so the work can be scheduled together.

High-rise, maisonettes and estate living

Wolverhampton built a great deal of council housing. The Low Hill estate had more than 2,000 council houses by 1927, Heath Town was largely redeveloped in the 1960s, and the Blakenhall tower blocks came down between 2002 and 2011. Some Heath Town maisonette blocks were demolished in 2017. Blocks and estates bring shared problems.

  • Stairwells and lifts. A specialist team removes needles and human waste from shared areas and disinfects afterwards. Say which floor you're on and whether the lift runs, because a high carry adds time.
  • Odour and pests. Both travel along ducts and corridors, so involve the housing provider quickly.
  • Who to tell. Council tenants begin with their housing officer.
  • Who pays. Normally the landlord or housing provider for shared areas, and the tenant, owner or estate inside the home.

A death in a flat raises further questions, answered in after death cleaning and in our post on who cleans up after a death in a property.

Needles, towpaths and waste ground

The council's own figures from 2017 recorded 1,467 syringes and other drug paraphernalia collected in a year, down from more than 3,800 two years earlier, and said that reported needles were cleared within one working day. The numbers will have changed since, but the advice has not: don't pick one up, report it, and keep children and pets away.

The Birmingham Canal Main Line and other canals pass through the city, and towpaths, derelict yards and overgrown corners collect fly-tipping, rats and sharps. If it is on your own land, the needle sweep service page explains how a sweep works, and after rodent clean up covers what follows pest control.

Tettenhall, Penn and larger homes

On the western side of the city, Tettenhall and Penn have larger detached houses and bungalows, and many are lived in by the same owners for decades. The calls are different in kind: the volume of belongings, the number of rooms and outbuildings, and families who live a long way off.

  • Tell us about every floor, the loft, garage and garden buildings. A large house takes more time even when only one room is badly affected.
  • Work in stages. Many families clear the urgent rooms first and decide about the rest later.
  • Authority first. Executors, attorneys and owners can approve work. A relative without it cannot.
  • Sort keepsakes beforehand. Papers, jewellery and photographs can be set aside or left in place.

Rail, racing and commercial work

Wolverhampton station is on the West Coast Main Line, the Metro has its western terminus here, and Dunstall Park racecourse draws crowds. Those flows, together with offices, retail, depots and industrial land, mean commercial calls are common: sewage backups, needles and human waste in service yards, pigeon droppings on roofs, and vacant units. See sewage clean-up, human waste cleaning and pigeon guano cleaning. Give us keyholder details, alarm and gate information and any stored materials. For event days, tell us the date so the van isn't caught in traffic.

Wolverhampton biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover the whole of Wolverhampton?

Yes, including Tettenhall, Penn, Bushbury, Whitmore Reans, Blakenhall and Heath Town, plus Wednesfield, Bilston and Willenhall.

Who do I ring when someone dies at home in Wolverhampton?

Emergency services first. For a sudden or unexplained death, the Black Country Coroner's Court in Oldbury (0121 569 7200) is involved, and the police can tell you when the property will be released.

Is the Wolverhampton coroner the same as Birmingham's?

No. Wolverhampton deaths go to the Black Country Coroner, based in Oldbury.

Who removes a discarded needle from a Wolverhampton street?

The council. Don't handle it yourself; report it to the council's customer services.

Can you clean a rented student house or HMO?

Yes, with an itemised quote and dated photographs for landlords and agents. Book early for the summer changeover.

Can you work in a high-rise block?

Yes, if the team knows about lifts, stairs and parking before it arrives.

How quickly can someone come?

It depends on the job, the access and when the property can be entered. Ring and we'll say what is realistic, without promising a time we can't keep.

Who pays for a clean-up after a death?

Usually the estate or the family, unless insurance or the landlord covers it. Our post on who cleans up after a death in a property explains.

I live abroad. Can you manage without me?

Yes. Leave the keys with a person you trust and we send the photos and report when it's done.
