Biohazard cleaning in Bilston

Specialist clean-up for homes, rentals, flats and empty buildings across Bilston and WV14, including Ettingshall, Spring Vale, Bradley, Stowlawn and The Lunt. After a death, hoarding, needles, sewage, rodents or a tenant who has left a property in a state.

Whoever you are, a landlord with a set of keys, a relative clearing a flat or a neighbour who has noticed something wrong, tell us what you can see. We'll tell you what it probably is, what should happen first and who needs to agree.

Bilston has changed more than most Black Country towns. It was an iron, steel, coal and enamelling centre; its steelworks closed in 1979 and 1989; and between the 1920s and 1960s most of the Victorian terraces were replaced with council housing and flats. Today the town is a mix of former council estates, owner-occupied homes, private lets, shops around the market and a lot of land that has been, or is being, redeveloped.

That history shapes the calls we hear. They tend to come from landlords and agents dealing with tenancies that have ended badly, from families who have inherited a home with years of belongings in it, from housing providers, and from people responsible for empty buildings and sites where needles, waste and vermin have accumulated. This page explains how those jobs work in Bilston, which agencies are involved, and what to have ready when you ring. Call or send an enquiry and we'll find a specialist team for the property, with the price itemised in writing before work begins. We coordinate; the attending team carries out the clean.

What's included

  • A first conversation about what's happened, who holds the keys and what should be left alone until a team arrives
  • A view of the property from your photos or a visit, followed by a written quote split into clear tasks
  • Removal of items that are beyond saving, then cleaning and full disinfection of what stays
  • Odour treated at its source
  • Needle and sharps clearance from rooms, gardens, yards and communal areas
  • Rubbish and contaminated waste moved out through a registered carrier, with proof of where it went
  • A dated photo record and a short report on what was done
  • Notes on which other trades the property needs before it is let or sold again

From Victorian terraces to council estates

Much of Bilston's housing falls into a few types, and each raises different questions.

  • Former council housing. The estates built from the 1920s onward, including those at Stowlawn, The Lunt and Bunker's Hill, are now a mix of social rent, owner-occupiers and private lets. If you're a tenant of a council or housing association, contact your landlord first. They have their own procedures, and many charge for work the tenant is responsible for. Our guide to who cleans up after a death in a property explains what happens to a social tenancy after a death.
  • Right-to-buy and private lets. A lot of ex-council homes are now owned by landlords who live elsewhere. Remote ownership means condition problems can go unseen for a long time, which is why a full inventory and regular inspections matter. See our landlords and letting agents page.
  • Older properties. Where Victorian and Edwardian buildings survive, timber floors, cellars and shared walls can carry smells and moisture further than you'd expect.
  • Flats and maisonettes. Shared stairwells, bin stores and neighbours all add a layer of care to the work, and odour often reaches communal areas before anyone has been told.

Landlords, agents and tenancy clear-outs

A good share of calls from WV14 are from rental properties that a tenant has left, or been removed from.

  • What you find on the doorstep. Rubbish piled in every room, human waste, needles in a bedroom or the garden, a smell that stops you at the door.
  • Before you clear anything. Get legal advice about the tenant's belongings and whether the tenancy has actually ended. Throwing out possessions or changing locks without the proper process can create problems of its own.
  • Evidence. Dated photographs from the doorway, your inventory and check-in report, and an itemised quote all matter for a deposit dispute or insurance claim. Our blog guide on whether insurance covers biohazard cleaning explains how insurers tend to treat these claims.
  • Clean is not the same as relettable. The electrics, drains, pests and decoration may need other trades. We'll flag what we see.
  • Cannabis. If you suspect or have found a grow, don't go in until the police say you can. Cannabis farm clean up and the blog guide on finding a cannabis farm in a rental set out the order of things.

Inherited homes, hoarding and neglect

When someone dies or goes into care, relatives often find a house packed with decades of belongings, or one where self-neglect has gone on for some time.

  • Talk about what matters before anything is moved, and don't clear anything without the owner's agreement unless you hold the legal authority.
  • Work in stages: safety first, then the kitchen and bathroom, then the rest. Hoarders clean up explains the approach and the blog post who pays for a hoarder clean up goes through who normally covers the cost.
  • Ask your GP, adult social care or Hoarding UK for help with the person. We help with the property.
  • If the home has fluids or odour from a death, see after death cleaning.

Empty buildings, redevelopment sites and open ground

Bilston has had a lot of redevelopment, from the old steelworks land to the urban village planned around the school and leisure centre, and with that come vacant buildings, boarded-up units and open ground waiting for work. Places like these attract fly-tipping, needles, nesting birds and vermin.

  • Needles and sharps. Don't pick them up by hand. Keep people clear and read the needle sweep service page for safe handling and how a sweep is run.
  • Fly-tipped waste and rubbish. Contaminated or mixed waste needs sorting and removal by a registered carrier. Keep the records.
  • Pigeons. Disused buildings and warehouse roofs are ideal roosts. Dry droppings shouldn't be swept. See pigeon guano cleaning.
  • Rodents. Rubbish and open ground mean rats. See after rodent clean up. Cleaning follows pest control, it doesn't replace it.
  • Telling us in advance. Tell us if a site is empty, fenced, derelict or due for demolition so access and safety can be planned.

If you're a landlord, developer or managing agent with several sites, say so when you ring.

Who to contact in Bilston

  • 999 for danger to life or a suspected crime
  • 101 for West Midlands Police on anything non-urgent, including when a property will be released
  • City of Wolverhampton Council for housing, environmental health and rubbish on public or private land. Bilston is part of the City of Wolverhampton, though it was a separate borough until 1966
  • The Black Country Coroner Service for sudden or unexplained deaths. It serves Wolverhampton, Sandwell, Dudley and Walsall, and sits at the Coroner's Court in Oldbury
  • Severn Trent for public sewers, while a blocked private drain is generally the owner's responsibility
  • Your landlord or housing provider, if you rent

A practical note on the coroner: its office hours are weekdays only, with shorter days on Thursday and Friday. Outside those hours the coroner's own page says to call West Midlands Police on 101. If a death has just been discovered, the officer in charge or the funeral director will tell you what is happening, and nothing can be cleaned until the property is released. You can still ring us in the meantime to plan.

Water, drains and the canal

Bilston sits on the Birmingham Canal route and on older drainage. Sewage backing up, burst pipes and flooding after heavy rain are all part of the picture. If water has got into the house, see sewage clean-up, flood damage clean up and flooded basement clean up. Don't go into standing water where sockets or appliances are in reach, and don't use electrics in wet rooms until they've been checked.

Getting to the property

Bilston has Metro stops at Bilston Central, Loxdale and The Crescent, bus links and the Black Country Route, though the railway station closed in 1972. What matters on the day is the street. When you ring, mention:

  • Whether there's a drive, a bay or only on-street parking
  • Stairs, floors and lifts, if it's a flat
  • Who holds the keys and when they're available
  • Whether gas, electricity and water are connected
  • Any neighbours who need to know, or who have already noticed

How it works

  1. Ring with the basics.Give the address, say what you've found and who owns or looks after the property, and mention any police, coroner or council involvement.

Bilston biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Spring Vale and Ettingshall?

Yes, along with Bradley, Stowlawn and the neighbouring towns. Give us your postcode and we'll confirm.

Can a landlord book a clear-out and deep clean together?

Yes. Say what the property needs and ask for each part priced separately.

Who covers Bilston?

City of Wolverhampton Council is the local authority, with West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service. The Black Country Coroner Service deals with sudden deaths.

Can empty buildings be cleaned before redevelopment?

Yes. Mention it early so access and safety can be planned.

I'm a council or housing association tenant. What do I do?

Tell your landlord first. They have their own processes and may charge for work.

Do I have to be there?

No. Keys can be passed to someone you trust.

What if a neighbour's property is the problem?

Contact the council's environmental health team, who can act in some cases where waste or pests are a risk to health. You're welcome to ring us for advice too.
