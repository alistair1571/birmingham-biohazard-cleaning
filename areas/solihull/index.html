Biohazard cleaning across Solihull borough

Solihull is a Metropolitan Borough south-east of Birmingham, with a town centre and a spread of suburbs and villages, including Shirley, Olton, Knowle, Dorridge, Hampton-in-Arden, Meriden, Balsall Common, Castle Bromwich and Chelmsley Wood. Postcodes include B90 to B94, B91 to B93 for the town and B37 and B36 on the northern side.

The borough is more varied than its reputation suggests. Prosperous commuter villages and the town centre sit alongside the large estate at Chelmsley Wood and the industrial and commercial sites around the airport and the NEC. The specialist teams cover the entire borough.

Large houses, older owners and probate clean-ups

Many Solihull homes are large detached and semi-detached houses, often lived in by the same owner for decades. When an elderly owner dies, becomes ill or moves into care, families can find a house full of belongings, or one that has deteriorated badly. The work may combine a clear-out, a deep clean, odour treatment and specialist cleaning in a single visit.

The hoarder clean-up page explains how clearing is done respectfully. Solicitors and executors can ask for photographs and a written summary for probate files. The cost guide shows what affects the price of larger properties.

Chelmsley Wood and the post-war estates

Chelmsley Wood was built in the 1960s and 1970s as an overspill estate for Birmingham, and Smith's Wood and Kingshurst belong to the same era. The estates mix houses, maisonettes and flats, managed by a combination of owners, housing providers and private landlords. Typical jobs include sharps and human waste in communal areas, tenancy clear-outs and neglected properties.

Housing providers and managing agents can ask for out-of-hours attendance, a written risk assessment and documentation for each address. See our guide for landlords and letting agents.

The airport, the NEC and commercial work

Solihull's commercial footprint includes Birmingham Airport, the NEC, the business parks around Blythe Valley and Solihull's offices and hotels. Sewage backups, pigeon droppings on large roofs and sharps in car parks are the usual commercial problems, and large organisations often want a single contact and reports for multiple sites.

Solihull Council is the local authority, and West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the borough. The River Blythe and several brooks can flood after heavy rain, so check your postcode on the government's flood risk service if you live near a watercourse.

Large properties and staged work

A large house in Solihull may have many rooms, outbuildings, a loft and a garage. Rather than treating the job as a single mass, a good team will survey each area, agree priorities and break the work into phases. This allows you to see progress, adjust the plan and control the cost.

If you are dealing with an estate, ask the team to provide a short written report after each phase, with photographs, so executors, family members and solicitors can see what has been done. For housing providers, ask for a consistent report format across all addresses.

A common situation in Solihull

Executors are asked to prepare a large family house for sale after the owner's death. The solicitor wants a valuation, the estate agent wants the house presentable and the family wants time to sort belongings. Meanwhile, the property is deteriorating, with damp, rodent activity and odours.

A specialist can step in at the stage that suits the family: a survey, then cleaning of kitchen, bathrooms and bedrooms, then a deeper clean and odour treatment. Written reports and photographs help executors show that the property has been looked after.

Who to contact in Solihull

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Solihull is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Solihull Metropolitan Borough Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes B90 to B94 and B37, and nearby places include Shirley, Knowle, Dorridge, Olton, Chelmsley Wood, Castle Bromwich.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
