Specialist clean-up for houses, flats, outbuildings and commercial premises across Solihull and its villages, including Shirley, Olton, Knowle, Dorridge, Balsall Common, Meriden, Castle Bromwich and Chelmsley Wood. Is the house a long way from the main road, or has it been empty for months? Both are common here, and both are covered below.

Solihull is a town of about 127,000 people at the centre of a borough of 216,000, and about three-quarters of that borough is green belt. So when someone searches for a cleaner here, they could be in a 1930s semi in Shirley, a flat near the Touchwood centre, a farmhouse near Meriden or a large detached house in Dorridge that has been standing empty since its owner went into care.

Those homes don't share the same problems. The suburban ones are close to mains drainage, neighbours and bus routes. The outlying ones often aren't, which changes what a sewage problem means, who is responsible for the drains, how long it takes to reach the property, and what happens to a house that nobody visits through the winter. This page is organised around those differences, and around the questions people most often ask us when they ring from the borough. Get in touch and we arrange a team for your address, with each cost itemised and agreed in writing before work starts. Coordination is our part; the attending team does the cleaning.

A sudden death means no cleaner can enter until the police or coroner's officer releases the house. Emergencies: 999. Questions about release: 101. The work itself is described under after death cleaning, and responsibility for the bill is covered in who cleans up after a death in a property.

Beyond the mains: septic tanks, cesspits and private drains

Much of the borough is rural or semi-rural, and homes on its edge often aren't connected to a public sewer. That matters, because the water company is responsible only for public sewers. A septic tank, cesspit or private drain belongs to the property, and so does the mess when it fails.

Signs of trouble

  • Slow drains and gurgling toilets across the house, not just in one room
  • Sewage smell in the garden or around an inspection cover
  • Wet, foul-smelling patches over the drainage field, or sewage surfacing in the garden
  • A tank that hasn't been emptied for years

What to do

  1. Stop using water as far as you can, and keep children and pets away from affected ground.
  2. Don't open covers or try to clear a tank yourself; the gas and the contamination are dangerous.
  3. Call a tank emptier or drainage specialist to stop the source. Cleaning follows once the overflow has stopped.
  4. Photograph the damage and tell your insurer. Cover for sewage varies between policies.
  5. Ring us to plan the clean-up of any building, outbuilding or ground that was affected. Our sewage clean-up page explains the work.

If you're buying or inheriting a rural property, ask for the last emptying record and any drainage survey. It is easier to arrange before a problem than after.

Barns, stables and outbuildings

Outlying properties tend to come with extra buildings: garages, workshops, stables, barns and sheds. Those need to be included when you ring.

  • Rodents. Stored feed, bedding and clutter attract rats and mice. Pest control comes first; see after rodent clean up.
  • Bats and nesting birds. They are protected, so get advice before blocking up or clearing a roof space.
  • Unknown stored items. Chemicals, fuel, asbestos sheeting and old equipment are separate problems. We'll say what is beyond a biohazard clean and who you need.
  • Access. Farm tracks, gates and long drives affect how a van reaches the property. Tell us the distance from the road and any gate codes.

A big house standing empty through the winter

Solihull has many large houses lived in by one person for decades, and they often go empty after a death, a move into care or a long hospital stay. Winter makes an empty house riskier.

  1. Tell the insurer it is unoccupied.Many policies restrict cover after a set period, so ask what the conditions are.
  2. Keep the heating on low,or ask a heating engineer about draining the system down if it can't be heated. Frozen pipes burst, and a leak in an empty house can run for days.
  3. Know where the stopcock is,and turn the water off if the house will stay empty.
  4. Arrange regular visitsby a relative, neighbour or agent, and keep a log.
  5. Don't leave valuables, papers or medication behind.Take them to a safe place.
  6. If you find water damage or a smell,photograph it before moving anything, then ring. See flood damage clean up and hoarders clean up if the house is also full of belongings.

If you're an executor, remember that authority comes first; nothing should be cleared without it. The insurance guide explains why the cause of damage affects a claim.

The airport, the NEC and business parks

Birmingham Airport, the NEC and the business parks make for a steady stream of commercial work: sewage backups in toilet blocks, vacant units, and pigeons. Out-of-hours work avoids disruption, though it costs more. Tell us who holds keys and alarm codes, and what is stored on site.

Housing providers and landlords

If you manage several properties, tell us at the start; we can provide itemised quotes, dated photos and waste records for each address. Landlords should photograph first and hold on to the inventory and tenancy paperwork; more on records is on the landlords and letting agents page.
