Biohazard cleaning in Bloxwich, WS3

Bloxwich is a large suburb in the Walsall borough, lying between Walsall, Cannock, Willenhall and Brownhills. Roughly 25,400 people were counted in the ward in 2011. The A34 forms the High Street, there are two railway stations, Bloxwich and Bloxwich North, and the M6 between junctions 10 and 11 is around four miles away.

Anyone searching for help is likely to describe the property as Bloxwich, Walsall or Blakenall. All three are covered, so give the postcode and say briefly what has happened. The team can then arrange an assessment and a clear quote.

Estates, homes and who manages them

Bloxwich was built up in layers. Between-the-wars council housing grew around Blakenall Heath, Harden and Goscote. After the Second World War came estates such as Lower Farm, Beechdale and Mossley, and Turnberry is also council-built. Privately owned houses are concentrated in the Little Bloxwich area, and further housing was built in the 1990s and 2000s.

That mix means a lot of properties managed by landlords, housing providers and agents, as well as owner-occupiers. Typical jobs include tenancy clear-outs, neglected or hoarded homes, sharps in stairwells and communal areas, human waste in abandoned properties, and clean-ups after a death. Landlords can find the records they should ask for on our landlords and letting agents page.

A Victorian high street and flats above shops

The town centre is mainly Victorian and Edwardian, with flats above many shops. Older mixed-use buildings can have shared entrances, tight stairs and old drainage, which makes sewage backups, pigeon droppings on ledges and leaks more disruptive. Shop owners and landlords should say at the start that the building is mixed-use, so access, noise and discretion can be planned.

See our pigeon guano and sewage clean-up pages for how these jobs are done.

Coal, canals and a long industrial past

Bloxwich grew in the eighteenth century on the back of coal mining, iron smelting and manufacturing, including the making of awl blades, and canals are part of the town's heritage. The legacy is a stock of older buildings and some former industrial land, which is why surveys of older properties and empty sites are worthwhile before any clearance.

Walsall Council is the local authority for Bloxwich, so report a neglected or empty property to them. West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the area, and anyone concerned about a hoarded home can ask the fire service for a Safe and Well visit.

Typical timings and what affects them

A small room contaminated by a single incident can be cleaned in a few hours. A whole house after a long-term hoarding situation or an unattended death can take several days, with clearing first, then cleaning, disinfecting and odour treatment. In Bloxwich's terraced and semi-detached homes, the biggest variables are the volume of waste, the condition of the floors and whether furniture can be saved.

Tell the team about any pets, medication, children or vulnerable neighbours, since these affect how work is done and what products are used. If the property is rented, ask the landlord or agent to confirm the work in writing so there is no confusion later about who authorised it.

A common situation in Bloxwich

A shop owner rents the flat above the unit to a tenant. After the tenant leaves, the owner finds sewage backing up from a blocked stack, damp walls and a smell in the shop below. Customers have noticed.

A specialist can deal with both the flat and the shop, extract sewage, clean and disinfect, dry the walls and treat odours. A drainage contractor should clear and survey the stack, so the problem does not return.

Who to contact in Bloxwich

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Bloxwich is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Walsall Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes WS3, and nearby places include Blakenall, Beechdale, Leamore, Goscote, Birchills, Pelsall.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
