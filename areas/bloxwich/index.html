Biohazard cleaning in Bloxwich

Specialist clean-up for houses, flats, rentals and shops across Bloxwich and WS3, including Blakenall, Beechdale, Little Bloxwich, Goscote and Mossley. Hoarding, a death at home, needles, human waste, sewage, rodents or a tenancy that has ended badly.

Bloxwich is one of the largest suburbs in the Walsall borough, and it has the mix of housing you'd expect from a place that grew in stages. It began as a village that lived off coal mining and cottage trades such as awl, nail and saddle-blade making, spread along the canals in the nineteenth century, and was filled out in the 1950s and 1960s with three large housing estates and a ring of schools. Today there are estates of former council housing, privately rented houses, housing association properties, Victorian parades with flats above the shops, and old industrial and pub sites being turned into new homes.

Different properties mean different problems, and different people who have to say yes before anyone can start. This page explains how clean-ups work across Bloxwich's main property types, which agencies are involved, and what to prepare before you ring. Ring or use the enquiry form and we'll arrange a team for the address, with every cost set out in writing first. Our part is coordination; the people who turn up do the cleaning itself.

What's included

  • The property assessed from your photos or on a visit, then a written quote that separates each task
  • Removal of anything contaminated beyond saving, and cleaning and disinfection of what remains
  • Odour treated where it comes from

  • Dated before-and-after photographs and a short summary of the work

Estates, homes and who manages them

Most of Bloxwich's housing was built in the post-war decades, and a large share started as council housing. Ownership is now much more mixed, which changes who you need to talk to.

  • Housing association and council tenants. If you rent from a social landlord, tell them first. They have their own procedures for hoarding, repairs and tenancies, and may expect the tenant to meet some costs. After a death, the tenancy needs to be ended formally and the home returned empty. Our blog guide on who cleans up after a death in a property goes through what social landlords commonly require.
  • Former council homes now privately owned or let. Many are owned by landlords elsewhere, so problems can build for months before anyone sees them. A regular inspection and a dated inventory help when a tenancy ends. See our landlords and letting agents page.
  • Owner-occupiers. Often long-term residents. When one dies or goes into care, relatives are left with a house full of belongings. Our hoarders clean up page explains how to approach it, and the blog post on who pays for a hoarder clean up covers the cost.
  • Housing providers with several properties. Tell us how many, and we can set up consistent quotes and paperwork for each address.

A Victorian high street and flats above shops

Bloxwich High Street still has older parades with flats and storage above shops. Those buildings bring their own difficulties.

  • Shared entrances and stairs. The shop, the flat and any neighbours often share a door, a yard or a stairwell. Tell us who has keys and how waste can leave the building.
  • Smell and fluids travel. Between floors, along timber joists and through old party walls. A problem in an upstairs flat can show up as a smell in the shop below.
  • Trading hours. If a shop is affected, tell us when you open and when you need to be back. Work outside trading hours is sometimes possible, usually at a higher price.
  • Rodents and pigeons. Yards, bin stores and roof voids behind commercial premises attract both. See after rodent clean up and pigeon guano cleaning. Cleaning follows pest control, it doesn't replace it.
  • Landlord and tenant. Check the lease, and tell the landlord and your insurer before agreeing to anything you may later want to claim for. Our guide to whether insurance covers biohazard cleaning explains how claims usually turn on the cause.

Empty pubs, old factories and redevelopment land

Bloxwich has had its share of closed pubs, disused factories and cleared industrial sites, and some of them have been redeveloped for housing. For years before that happens, they can be a problem.

  • Needles and sharps. Left in sheltered corners, yards and boarded-up entrances. Don't pick them up by hand. Keep people away and see our needle sweep service page for safe handling.
  • Fly-tipping and mixed waste. Contaminated rubbish should be separated and removed by a registered carrier. Keep the records.
  • Squatters' or trespassers' waste. Human waste and bedding in empty buildings are a biohazard. See human waste cleaning.
  • Rats and pigeons. Empty buildings are ideal for both.
  • Before demolition or conversion. Tell us if the building is empty, fenced or due for work, so we can plan safe access.

If you manage or own a vacant property in Bloxwich, say so when you ring, and tell us about any security or access conditions.

Coal, canals and old ground

Bloxwich grew on coal and canals, and the legacy is still around: old waterways, former industrial land and a lot of ground that has been built on more than once. For householders, the practical effects are damp, occasional flooding after heavy rain and older drains. If water has got into your home, see sewage clean-up, flood damage clean up and flooded basement clean up. Sewage from a public sewer is a matter for the water company; a blocked private drain is usually the owner's responsibility.

Who to contact in Bloxwich

  • 999 for danger to life or a suspected crime
  • 101 for West Midlands Police on anything not urgent, including when a property will be released
  • Walsall Council for housing, environmental health and rubbish on public or private land. Walsall Council says it has withdrawn its own pest control service and suggests hiring a professional firm, so check its current page. It also says it can act on build-ups of rubbish on private land that attract rats or mice, and you can report these to it
  • The Black Country Coroner Service if a death was sudden or unexplained. Based at the Coroner's Court in Oldbury, it covers Walsall, Sandwell, Dudley and Wolverhampton, and is separate from the service for Birmingham
  • Your landlord or housing provider, if you rent
  • Your insurer, before you book work you may want to claim for

You can't begin cleaning after a death or crime until the police or coroner have released the property. You can still ring us beforehand, so a plan and a quote are ready when it is.

How long does it take?

Every property is different, so we won't quote a standard timescale, but as a rough guide:

  • A single room with a specific problem, such as a spill, a small amount of waste or a few needles, can be a matter of hours.
  • A whole house after long-term hoarding, a long-undiscovered death or a tenancy abandoned in a state can take several days, sometimes in stages.
  • Flats and shop units can be slower because of stairs, shared access and restricted hours.

What speeds a job up is a clear brief: who holds the keys, who can approve the work, whether the utilities are on, and what has to be kept. What slows it down is waiting for permission from a landlord, an estate or an insurer, so it helps to ask those questions early.

Bloxwich biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Blakenall, Beechdale and Little Bloxwich?

Yes, along with Goscote, Mossley and nearby parts of Walsall, Willenhall and Brownhills. If you're not sure, give us your postcode.

Can you help a housing provider or landlord with several properties?

Yes. Each address gets its own itemised quote, photographs and waste records.

What about a flat above a shop?

Tell us about shared entrances, stairs, access times and who needs to be told, so removal and disruption can be planned around them.

Which council covers Bloxwich?

Walsall Council. West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover policing and fire, and the Black Country Coroner Service deals with sudden deaths.

I rent from a housing association. Who do I call first?

Your landlord. They have their own rules and will usually want to know before work starts.

Can you clean an empty pub or industrial building?

Yes, with notice. Tell us about the state of the building, access and security.

Do I need to be there?

No. Keys can be passed to someone you trust.
