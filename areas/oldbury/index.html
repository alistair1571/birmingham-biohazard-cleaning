Biohazard cleaning in Oldbury, B68 and B69

Oldbury is a Black Country town in the Metropolitan Borough of Sandwell, between Birmingham and Dudley, close to junction 2 of the M5. Its postcodes are B68 and B69, with B66 and B67 to the east in Smethwick and B65 to the south in Rowley Regis. Sandwell Council is the local authority, and callers sometimes describe an address as Smethwick, Langley or Tividale. All are covered.

The specialist teams cover the whole of Oldbury, from the Rood End and Langley estates to the commercial areas by the motorway. Give the postcode when you call and describe what has happened.

Chemical works and canal-side living

Oldbury's history is industrial. It was a centre for chemical manufacture, with the phosphorus works of Albright and Wilson a major employer, and it also had steel and engineering works and a network of canals. The Birmingham Canal Navigations runs through the area, including the Titford Canal and Oldbury Locks. Many industrial buildings have been cleared and replaced by housing and retail parks.

Former industrial land and canal-side housing can both mean damp and awkward drainage. When a property has flooded or sewage has backed up, ask for drying and a moisture reading as well as the clean, as described on our flood damage page.

Rented homes and tenancy turnover

The area has a lot of terraced and semi-detached housing, with a significant private rented sector, along with housing association properties and flats. Landlords and agents regularly face tenancy endings where rubbish has accumulated, sharps have been left or human waste is present. Clear, itemised quotes and photographs help landlords protect themselves in a dispute over the deposit.

Our landlord and letting agent page explains what to ask for. Hoarded flats can also be cleared in stages: see hoarder clean-up.

Business parks and who to contact

Oldbury has a large amount of commercial space, including the Wolverhampton Road area and the units around the M5. Out-of-hours attendance, written quotes and waste paperwork are all worth requesting. Pigeon droppings in warehouse roofs and sharps in yards are the usual commercial problems.

West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover Oldbury. Sandwell Council can advise on neglected and empty properties, and is the right body to contact if you have concerns about someone else's home.

Commercial, canal and housing jobs together

Oldbury combines large commercial areas, canal-side housing and dense terraces, so a team here needs to handle quite different jobs. Ask whether the team has done work like yours before, and whether they can provide photographs of similar jobs with the customer's identity removed.

For landlords, the main risk is not the clean itself but the paperwork. Insist on a dated quote, a dated invoice, and before and after photographs. For commercial customers, ask for method statements and waste transfer notes. These documents matter if there is a dispute with a tenant, a landlord or an insurer.

A common situation in Oldbury

A business owner arrives on Monday morning to find that a drain has backed up over the weekend, leaving sewage in a toilet block and a corridor. Staff cannot work, and customers are due. They need the area cleaned, disinfected and safe within hours.

A specialist can attend at short notice, extract the sewage, clean and disinfect surfaces and dry the area. Ask for a written note of what was done, which you may need for your insurer, and a follow-up with a drainage specialist to fix the cause.

Who to contact in Oldbury

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Oldbury is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Sandwell Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes B68 and B69, and nearby places include Langley, Rounds Green, Brandhall, Warley, Tividale, Rood End.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
