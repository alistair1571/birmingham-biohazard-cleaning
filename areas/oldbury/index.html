Oldbury is the town most people in the Black Country picture when they think of Sandwell Council: its headquarters is in the town centre, the Black Country Coroner's Court is on Halesowen Street, and the M5 runs overhead on its raised section. It is also an old industrial place that was rebuilt in layers, so one postcode can contain Victorian terraces, 1930s council estates, 1960s tower blocks and canal-edge units within a few streets of each other.

That layering is what this page is built around. The right first call in Oldbury depends less on what has happened than on what kind of building it happened in, and who is responsible for it. You'll find a street-by-street guide to the housing, a plain account of what the council's own pest service does and doesn't cover, and a note on the small number of situations where Oldbury's coroner arrangements affect timing. Tower-block and flat jobs: what changes

Many biohazard calls in Oldbury involve a flat, and flats are harder than houses for a reason that has nothing to do with the contamination: other people.

  1. Find out who the landlord is.A council or housing association tenant should tell their housing officer before anything else. The landlord has its own steps for hoarding, repairs, access and tenancies after a death, and may arrange or charge for work.
  2. Work out how everything gets out.Lift size, stairs, bin stores and the distance to a van decide the number of carries. Say which floor, and whether the lift works.
  3. Expect neighbours to be aware.Odour travels through ducts and corridors. A discreet team, an unmarked approach where possible and a short word with the managing agent keep it low-key.
  4. Treat the neighbouring flats as part of the problem.If the smell or pests came from next door, the flat above and below may need checking.
  5. Keep the record.Dated photos and waste paperwork help the landlord, the executor or an insurer later.

For a death in a flat, see after death cleaning and the guide on who cleans up after a death in a property, which sets out who is responsible by tenancy type.

Rats and mice: what the council offers, and where we fit

Sandwell Council runs its own pest control team, and it publishes its prices. At the time of writing it treats rats, mice, wasps, cockroaches and bedbugs, among a short list of others. Rats in gardens are free for everyone, while rats or mice inside a property are charged, with a reduced rate for people who receive Council Tax benefit on the property. Wasp treatment runs only from 1 July to 31 October. Bookings go through a MySandwell account or the contact centre on 0121 569 7474, and payment is taken before the booking is confirmed. Check the council's page for current figures, as they change.

What the service does not do is clean up afterwards. That is where our work starts:

  • Treatment first. Bait, proofing and removal of the animals come from the pest controller. Cleaning before that is wasted effort.
  • Then the mess. Droppings, nests, urine-soaked insulation and contaminated stored items are removed and the area disinfected; see after rodent clean up.
  • Don't sweep or vacuum droppings yourself. Dust from dry droppings is a health risk, particularly in lofts and garages.
  • Canal-edge gardens. Properties near the Titford and the other canals tend to attract rats; if the neighbour's rubbish is the cause, tell Sandwell Council's environmental health team.

When there's a death

The Black Country Coroner's Court is in Oldbury, at Jack Judge House on Halesowen Street. That fact matters in only one way for a householder: the coroner's service covers the Black Country boroughs, so a sudden or unexplained death at an Oldbury address is dealt with locally, and no cleaning can begin until the police or coroner's officer has released the property.

  • The funeral director or the officer in charge will tell you when the property is released. You can ring us before then to plan, but nothing is cleaned until it is.
  • For emergencies, call 999. To ask police about the release of a property, call 101.
  • Starting soon after release limits how far fluids and odour spread through carpets, underlay and floorboards.

Old industry, canals and the ground under the house

Oldbury has been an industrial town for a long time. The chemical works founded by Arthur Albright and John Wilson was important enough that Lloyds opened a branch in 1864 to serve it, and Albright & Wilson's works at Rood End and Langley Green remain among the town's best-known industries. There were also blast furnaces and steelworks, and brick-making and coal mining at Whiteheath between about 1850 and 1920. Canals came earlier still; the Birmingham Canal was cut south of the centre in 1768.

None of that means your house is contaminated, and we make no such claim. It does mean two practical things for people with a damaged or flooded property:

  • Cleaning isn't a survey. If you see new cracks, floors that slope, or doors that stop closing, a surveyor and your insurer should look at it, and the team will flag it if they notice the signs.
  • Drains and water are separate trades. A repeated backup or persistent damp usually needs a drainage or building fix. See sewage clean-up, flood damage clean up and flooded basement clean up.

Landlords: tenancy turnover in B68 and B69

A lot of Oldbury's older terraces and flats are rented, and the same few problems recur at the end of tenancies.

  • Needles, human waste, rubbish and food left in a property
  • Cannabis growing, with damp, mould and electrical interference; see cannabis farm clean up
  • Smoke and cigarette smell; see cigarette odour removal
  • Hoarding, or a tenant who has stopped coping

Before anything is touched, photograph the property, keep the inventory and check-in report, and take advice on what you may do with belongings. Ask for an itemised quote and dated before-and-after photos, which are what a deposit scheme or insurer will want. The landlords and letting agents page explains the process, and the insurance guide covers when cover applies.

Units, yards and the edge land

Small industrial units and yards around the canals and the M5 corridor bring their own problems: needles and human waste in loading areas, pigeons nesting in roof spaces, and drains that back up. See needle sweep service, human waste cleaning and pigeon guano cleaning. Tell us about gates, alarms and keyholders, and whether any stored chemicals are on site, so a biohazard job isn't confused with an industrial one.

What it costs and what changes it

Price follows the volume of material to remove, the type of contamination, the floor and the carry, the time needed and any out-of-hours work. Photos and a short description normally give a first figure, and any change on site pauses the work until you've agreed it.

Oldbury biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Oldbury, Langley and Tividale?

Yes, along with Rood End, Brandhall, Titford, Warley and Lion Farm, plus nearby Smethwick, West Bromwich, Rowley Regis and Tipton.

Is Oldbury in Birmingham?

No. It is in Sandwell, although the B68 and B69 postcodes belong to the Birmingham postal area.

Does the council deal with rats in Oldbury?

Sandwell Council has a pest control team that treats rats and mice, and charges for some visits. It doesn't clean up afterwards, so droppings and nests are a separate job.

Can you clean a flat in a tower block?

Yes. Tell us the floor, whether the lift works and where a van can stop, so the carry can be planned and priced.

I'm the executor and live far away. Can you manage without me?

Yes. A neighbour, solicitor or agent can hold the keys, and we send dated photos and a short report afterwards.

Is the clean-up the same as a clearance?

No. Cleaning treats contamination, while a clearance empties the property. They can be done together, but should be priced on separate lines.
