A very big place to cover

Birmingham is the largest local authority district in England, with around 1.18 million residents in 2024. It is a young city: roughly two in five residents are under 25, and five universities, including the University of Birmingham at Edgbaston, make it the biggest centre of higher education outside London. That mix of students, young renters and long-term homeowners shapes the kind of property problems that come up.

Districts named across the city include Edgbaston, Selly Oak, Moseley, King's Heath, Hall Green, Yardley, Erdington, Handsworth, Aston, Saltley, Stirchley, Hockley and Bournville, as well as Sutton Coldfield to the north-east. Biohazard cleaning is available throughout, and you can call or send an enquiry whichever postcode the property has.

Shared houses, rentals and tenancy turnover

Large numbers of students and renters mean a busy lettings market, and with it, a steady need for end-of-tenancy and landlord clean-ups that go beyond a standard clean: hoarded rooms, human waste, sewage backing up from shared bathrooms, sharps and cannabis-related contamination. Landlords and managing agents should ask for an itemised quote, photographs and waste paperwork to support deposit claims and insurance.

Our guide for landlords and letting agents sets out what to ask for, and the cost guide on the homepage explains how jobs are priced.

Tower blocks, back-to-backs and Victorian streets

The city's housing is varied. Heavy wartime damage and 1950s and 1960s redevelopment produced tower-block estates such as Castle Vale. Much older housing survives too, including the Birmingham Back to Backs, the last surviving court of back-to-back houses in the city. That variety means very different access and logistics: lifts and stairwells in high-rises, narrow courts and terraces in older streets, and parking limits almost everywhere.

A good team will ask about access, parking, floor level and the age and construction of the property before they quote, because those details affect how waste is carried out and how long the job takes.

Water, canals and drainage

Birmingham has only minor rivers, mainly the Tame, Rea and Cole, but it has about 250 miles of urban brooks and streams and a large network of canals, the Birmingham Canal Navigations. Flooding and sewage problems tend to be localised, so it is worth checking your postcode on the government's long-term flood risk service if you live near a watercourse.

For sewage backing up inside a property, see our sewage clean-up and flood damage pages. Birmingham City Council covers the city and Sutton Coldfield, and West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the area.

Choosing a team for a city this size

In a city of more than a million people, a team's travel time matters, as does the postcode. Ask where the team will be coming from, whether they can attend quickly, and whether the quote covers waste disposal, parking and congestion. Ask for the waste carrier registration number and check it on the Environment Agency's public register. A reputable team will give it without hesitation.

Be wary of quotes that are only a single figure with no breakdown. A clean-up in a high-rise flat in Ladywood and one in a Victorian house in Moseley can look similar on a phone call and be very different on the day. Good teams ask about the floor, the access, the type of contamination and how long it has been there before they commit to a price.

A common situation in Birmingham

A managing agent responsible for many flats receives a report from a neighbour about a smell in a high-rise block. They gain entry with the police and find that a tenant has died. The block is busy, with limited lift access and neighbours who are anxious.

A specialist can assess the flat, plan the route for waste, work discreetly and treat odours. The agent receives a report for the landlord and the insurer. Clear communication with neighbours, handled by the agent, reduces worry.

Who to contact in Birmingham

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Birmingham is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Birmingham City Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes B1 to B38 and surrounding postcodes, and nearby places include Edgbaston, Selly Oak, Harborne, Moseley, Kings Heath, Hall Green.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment. Birmingham biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover the whole of Birmingham?

Yes. All Birmingham postcodes are covered, including the city centre, Sutton Coldfield, Selly Oak, Edgbaston, Handsworth, Erdington and Hall Green. If the property is just outside the city boundary, call and give the postcode.

Can you help a landlord or agent who manages many Birmingham properties?

Yes. Landlords and managing agents often need a dependable contact across several addresses. Ask for itemised quotes and before and after photographs for each property.

What if the property is a high-rise flat?

High-rise flats are common in Birmingham. Mention the floor, lift access and parking when you call, as these affect how waste is removed and how long the job takes.

Which council do I contact in Birmingham?

Birmingham City Council covers the city, including Sutton Coldfield. Properties in Smethwick, Oldbury, Solihull and Wolverhampton fall under other councils, so check the address before you report a problem.
