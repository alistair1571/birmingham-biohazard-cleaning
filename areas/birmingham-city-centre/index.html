Biohazard cleaning in Birmingham city centre

Specialist clean-up for apartments, offices, bars, shops and shared buildings inside the Middleway, from the Jewellery Quarter to Digbeth and from Brindleyplace to Eastside. Planned around lifts, loading bays, managing agents and opening hours.

Call 0121 000 0000 Request a free quote

In a city-centre building, the clean is only half the problem. The other half is getting a team in and the waste out without upsetting the building. Tell us the address and the access, and we'll plan around it.

Most biohazard clean-ups in the city centre don't happen in a house with a front door and a driveway. They happen on the ninth floor of a managed block, in the basement of a bar with a delivery window, in a Jewellery Quarter workshop with a listed façade, or in a serviced apartment between guests. The contamination is the same as anywhere else. The constraints around it are not.

A lift that has to be booked, a concierge who needs notice, a car park with a height barrier, a neighbour on the other side of a thin wall, a landlord three steps removed from the person on the phone: these decide how long a job takes and whether it goes smoothly. This page is about getting those things right. It covers who has to approve the work, what changes in a shared building, what bars and offices need to think about, and what to tell us in the first call so the plan fits your site. Get in touch and a specialist team can be arranged, with a written, itemised price before any work begins. We coordinate, and the attending team does the work.

What's included

  • A first call to establish what has happened, how the building is laid out and what is safe to do in the meantime
  • Planning around access: lifts, loading, parking, concierge rules, quiet hours and waste routes
  • Removal of contaminated materials and cleaning and disinfection of everything affected
  • Odour treatment at the source, with attention to where air moves between flats or floors
  • Waste leaving the building in sealed containers through a registered carrier, with the records to prove it
  • Before-and-after photos and a brief write-up for the managing agent, landlord, lender or insurer
  • Advice on other trades the premises may need, such as drainage, electrical or pest control

Who has to say yes in a city-centre building

Before a team can arrive, someone with authority has to approve the work. In city-centre property that is rarely one person.

  • A flat you own. You can instruct the work inside your own flat, but the building's rules still apply to lifts, corridors and bin stores. Tell the managing agent early.
  • A flat you rent. The landlord or their agent approves and arranges the clean. Tenants should tell them straight away and not wait.
  • A flat where the owner has died. The executor or administrator is the contact, and our blog guide on responsibility after a death at home sets out each situation.
  • Communal areas. Corridors, stairwells, bin stores and car parks normally sit with the managing agent or freeholder, not individual residents.
  • Commercial premises. The tenant, the landlord and the insurer may each have a say, depending on the lease. Check who is responsible for what before you commit.

If your building has a managing agent, ask them first what the building rules are. They often have a standard procedure for contractors, and following it avoids delays at the door.

Apartments, shared walls and discretion

City-centre apartments sit close together, share ventilation routes and are watched by neighbours. That changes how a clean-up is done.

  • Odour travels. Smell can pass along corridors, through service risers and under doors. If neighbours have noticed something, tell us, because we'll want to know which flats are affected and where the source probably is.
  • Discretion matters. Corridors and lifts are shared, so how the team arrives, where waste is brought out, and who sees it, are worth discussing. Tell us what the building requires and what you would prefer.
  • Neighbours. A short, factual note from the managing agent, without any detail about the people involved, usually settles worries better than silence.
  • Fire doors and communal safety. The team should not prop open fire doors or block escape routes. Tell us about any building restrictions.
  • Student blocks and short lets. These turn over quickly. A flat left in a poor state between tenants or guests needs work before the next arrival, and the operator often needs photographs and a written record for a deposit claim or an insurer. See our landlords and letting agents page.

For death-related work in flats, our after death cleaning page explains the sequence, and our guide to crime scene cleaning covers what happens when police have been involved.

Bars, restaurants, clubs and venues

Nightlife businesses in places like Broad Street, Digbeth and the Gas Street area face a different pressure: they have to open, and the clock is running.

  • Out-of-hours work. Much of the work for venues is done in the gap between closing and opening. Tell us your deadline when you ring and we'll say what is realistic. Out-of-hours work usually costs more.
  • Sewage and blocked drains. A backed-up toilet block or kitchen drain is a common reason for a call. If the premises prepares food, check with your local environmental health team before you reopen after a sewage incident. Our sewage clean-up page goes through what is involved.
  • Vomit and bodily fluids. After a busy night, a small amount of vomit is routine, but a larger spill, or blood, needs proper disinfection. See human waste cleaning.
  • Needles in toilets and doorways. Where sharps are found, don't pick them up by hand. Our needle sweep service explains what to do and how a sweep works.
  • Insurers and licences. Keep dated photographs, quotes and records. Your insurer may want to approve the work first, and a written report helps if you need to show the premises is safe.

Offices, shops and commercial units

For offices and shops, the question is who pays and who must be told.

  • Check the lease for who is responsible for repairs and cleaning, and whether the landlord needs to be told.
  • Tell your insurer before work starts if you may claim. Our guide on whether home and landlord insurance covers biohazard cleaning shows how insurers tend to look at the cause.
  • Protect staff and customers by keeping people out of affected areas until the team confirms it is safe.
  • Ask for a written record you can show a landlord, auditor or inspector.

Pigeons, rodents and the hidden city

Older and disused buildings, ledges, loading bays and railway arches attract pigeons and vermin. Droppings build up where people don't look: roof voids, plant rooms, window ledges and dark corners of car parks. Dry droppings can carry health risks when disturbed, so don't sweep them. Working at height is a job for a team that has the right equipment. See pigeon guano cleaning and after rodent clean up. Cleaning doesn't stop birds or rodents returning, and proofing is a separate job for a pest specialist.

Listed buildings, the Jewellery Quarter and the canals

Parts of the city centre are conservation areas, and many buildings are listed. Original floors, plaster and joinery need careful treatment, and harsh methods can do lasting damage. Tell us if the building is listed or in a conservation area so the team can choose suitable materials and methods.

Canal-side and low-lying sites around Gas Street, Digbeth and Eastside can be prone to damp and water ingress. A leak or flood in an old building often reaches the floors and walls below, so see flood damage clean up and flooded basement clean up.

Getting in and out of the city centre

Logistics decide how long a city-centre job takes, so tell us as much as you can in the first call.

  • Where will the team park? Say whether there is a loading bay, a bay you can reserve, a car park with a height restriction, or none. Some streets have waiting restrictions.
  • The Clean Air Zone. Birmingham's Clean Air Zone covers the roads inside the Middleway ring road. Older vehicles that don't meet the emissions standard can be charged daily, so ask who is covering that cost when comparing quotes, and check the current rules on the council's site.
  • Lifts and stairs. Give the floor number, whether there is a goods lift, and whether it can be reserved.
  • Concierge and security. Tell us who has to be notified and whether the team needs ID or a pass.
  • Waste route. Say where waste can leave the building and whether there are restricted times.
  • Quiet hours. Many residential buildings limit noisy work. Say if yours does.

Who to contact

  • 999 if anyone is in danger or you think a crime has been committed
  • West Midlands Police on 101 for non-emergencies, and to find out when a property will be released
  • Your managing agent, landlord or facilities team for building access and rules
  • Birmingham City Council for public land, housing and environmental health
  • Your insurer, before you book work you hope to claim for

Cleaning can't begin until the police or coroner have released the property.

What affects the price

  • What the contamination is and how far it has spread
  • The number of rooms, floors or units affected
  • Access: lifts, distance to the waste route, parking and loading
  • Timing: out-of-hours, weekend or bank holiday work
  • Building rules: notice periods, escorts and restricted working hours
  • How much has to be removed, and how it must be disposed of

A handful of photos, the floor and the access notes normally get you a first figure, and the final price is written down before anyone starts.

How it works

  1. Call or send the details.Address, what happened, who manages the building, who signs off spending, plus lift, parking, quiet-hours and deadline information.
  2. Quote and access plan.A written quote sets out scope, cost, timing and how the team will get in and out. Where a landlord or insurer must agree first, the job pauses until they do.
  3. On site.Protective kit on, building rules followed, affected areas cleaned and disinfected, odour treated, waste carried out. Nobody has to wait there; the building's keyholder can let the team in.
  4. Handover.The photos, write-up and waste records follow, along with a note of any other trade the unit still needs.

Birmingham City Centre FAQs

Do you cover the whole city centre?

Yes, including the Jewellery Quarter, Digbeth, Eastside, Southside, Westside, Brindleyplace, Ladywood, the Gun Quarter and the area around the Middleway. If you're just outside, give us your postcode.

Can a clean-up be done in a managed apartment building?

Yes. Tell us about the lift, loading, parking, concierge and quiet hours so the team can plan routes and containers around the building.

Can you work out of hours for a bar or venue?

Usually, at extra cost. Give us your opening time and we'll tell you what's realistic.

Can you clean a listed or conservation-area building?

Yes, but tell us beforehand so suitable methods and materials are chosen.

Will neighbours know?

We plan the work to be low-key, but we can't control what neighbours notice, especially if odour has spread. Your managing agent can decide what to tell residents.

Who do I ask about the building's rules?

The managing agent, concierge or facilities team. Ask what they require of contractors before you book anyone.

Does my insurer need to know first?

If you might claim, yes. Many insurers want to approve the work or appoint a contractor first.

What if I don't know who is responsible for a communal area?

Ask the managing agent or freeholder. If nobody answers, tell us and we'll help you work out where to start.
