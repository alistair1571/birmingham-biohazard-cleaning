Wednesfield made its name making traps, from mousetraps to mantraps, and locks. Many of those factories have been cleared, and the town has become a place of 1950s estates, terraced streets, a long high street with flats above the shops, and tower-block and low-rise estates in Heath Town. It lies in the City of Wolverhampton, a few miles from the city centre, but its roots and feel are Black Country.

Searching for a cleaner is rarely the first thing people do. Most callers from Wednesfield have just been through something, whether a death, a long illness, a bad tenancy or a leak, and they want three things: to know what happens next, who has to be involved, and what it will cost. This page leads with the situation we are asked about most here, which is a death at home, and then covers estates, flats, shops and the canal. A death at home: expected or sudden?

Whether a cleaning team can start soon or has to wait depends mostly on one question: was the death expected?

Expected death (for example, someone receiving end-of-life care at home)Sudden or unexplained death
Who is involved firstA doctor, usually the GP, who can say why the person died, and the funeral directorPolice and paramedics, then the coroner
Is the property released quickly?Usually yes, once the funeral director has taken the person into careNot until the police or coroner's officer hands the property back
Who can tell you?The GP or funeral directorThe officer in charge or the coroner's officer
Coroner for WolverhamptonNot usually involvedBlack Country Coroner Service, Oldbury

This is general information, not legal advice, and every case is different, so ask the funeral director or the GP what applies. In an emergency dial 999; for questions about when police will hand a property back, ring 101.

The cleaning itself differs as well. After an expected death the main tasks are usually bedding, mattresses, soft furnishings and the odour of a long illness. If a death went unnoticed for days, fluids soak into carpets, underlay and floorboards, so an early start after release limits the spread. After death cleaning describes the work, and our post on who cleans up after a death in a property explains who pays under each kind of tenancy.

Wolverhampton Council, not Sandwell or Walsall

Wednesfield borders both Walsall and Willenhall, and it feels like part of the Black Country, but it falls under City of Wolverhampton Council. That affects who you contact.

  • Council: City of Wolverhampton Council handles housing, environmental health, waste and empty properties. Council-owned homes are managed by Wolverhampton Homes, so council tenants should start with their housing officer.
  • Rats and mice: check the council's current pest control offer directly, because services and charges differ from borough to borough. Our Walsall and Oldbury pages show two very different approaches. Either way, treatment comes first and cleaning follows; see after rodent clean up.
  • Police and fire: West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service, as in the rest of the area.
  • Your boundary: A council tax bill confirms the authority for your road.

The 1950s overspill estates and the people who stayed

In the early 1950s Wolverhampton built the Ashmore Park and Long Knowle estates to rehouse families from slum clearance. Many of those households stayed for decades, and their homes are now sold, rented or passed down. Some have the characteristics of any long tenure: lofts, sheds and garages full of belongings, and rooms that have been closed off for years.

  • A parent's house. After a death, a hospital admission or a move into care, confirm who holds legal authority before anything is cleared. The staged approach is in hoarders clean up, with the money side in who pays for a hoarder clean up.
  • A former council house. Where the property was bought, it belongs to the owner or their estate. Where it is rented, the landlord's rules apply.
  • A first visit. Photograph from the doorway, avoid disturbing droppings, sharps or fluids, and make a note of anything that must not be lost.

Heath Town and living in a block

Heath Town has had tower blocks and low-rise flats, and in recent years some blocks have been refurbished, low-rise blocks demolished and new homes planned. Flats mean shared space, and shared space means shared problems.

  • Stairwells and entrances. Needles and human waste are cleared by a specialist team, and the area is disinfected. Don't pick up sharps by hand. See needle sweep service and human waste cleaning.
  • Lifts. Tell us if the lift works and which floor. A carry from a high floor is a different job from a ground-floor one.
  • Neighbours. Smell travels through corridors and ducts. Speak to the housing provider or managing agent early.
  • Who pays. Usually the landlord or housing provider for communal areas, and the owner or tenant for inside a flat, but check your tenancy or lease.

The High Street, shops and the canal

Wednesfield's long high street has shops with flats above, which means shared doors, trading hours and tight access. Out of hours work avoids lost trade but costs more. For sewage backups, see sewage clean-up.

The Wyrley and Essington Canal, known locally as the Curly Wyrley, opened in 1797 and ran around the old town centre on three sides. Homes near the water see more damp, and gardens near the towpath see more rats. See flood damage clean up for water in the home, and tell us when you ring if the property is near the canal.

Nearby, the Bentley Bridge retail and leisure area and the units around it face a different set of problems: fly-tipping, needles and human waste around loading areas. Tell us who holds the keys and alarm codes, and where a van can stand.

Landlords

Rented homes across Wednesfield produce the usual end-of-tenancy problems. Photograph the property with dates before anything is moved, hold on to the inventory, and ask for advice before disposing of belongings. Useful records are listed on the landlords and letting agents page, and the insurance guide covers claims.

What changes the price

Price is driven by volume, the type of contamination, time since it happened, the stairs or lift involved and urgency. Photos are normally enough for a ballpark figure, and any extra work gets a new quote first.

Wednesfield biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Wednesfield and Heath Town?

Yes, along with Ashmore Park, Low Hill, Fallings Park and Wood End, plus nearby Bilston, Willenhall and Wolverhampton.

Which council covers Wednesfield?

City of Wolverhampton Council.

How soon after a death can the property be cleaned?

It depends on whether the police or coroner are involved. After an expected death it can often be soon; after a sudden one, not until the property is released. Ask the funeral director or the GP.

Can you clean a flat above a shop?

Yes. Tell us about shared access, opening hours and parking.

Can you deal with needles in a block of flats?

Yes. The sharps are collected into approved containers and communal areas are disinfected.

I live too far away. Can you manage without me?

Yes. A relative, neighbour, agent or solicitor can hold the keys, and the paperwork is emailed afterwards.
