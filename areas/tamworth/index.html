Biohazard cleaning in Tamworth, B77 to B79

Tamworth is a market town in Staffordshire, about 15 miles north-east of Birmingham, with postcodes B77, B78 and B79. A small part of the borough lies in Warwickshire. It is known as the ancient capital of Mercia, and Tamworth Castle overlooks the meeting of the rivers Tame and Anker. Tamworth Borough Council is the local authority.

The specialist teams cover Tamworth and its neighbours, including Glascote, Amington, Wilnecote, Fazeley, Kettlebrook and Dosthill. Give the postcode when you call and describe what has happened.

A town expanded in the 1960s

Tamworth grew rapidly in the 1960s as an overspill town for Birmingham, and estates such as Belgrave, Stonydelph, Glascote Heath and Wilnecote date from that period. Many of those homes are now fifty or sixty years old, and some are on their second or third generation of ownership.

A death, a hospital stay or a house sale commonly reveals a property that needs specialist cleaning. See our after-death cleaning and hoarder clean-up pages, and the first-hour advice on our homepage.

Rivers, canals and flooding

The River Tame, the River Anker, the Birmingham and Fazeley Canal and the Coventry Canal all pass through or near Tamworth. Low-lying land by the rivers can flood after heavy rain, and flood water is contaminated with sewage. Check your postcode on the government's flood risk service if you live near a watercourse.

See our flood damage clean-up and sewage clean-up pages for what the work involves.

Landlords, business and local contacts

Landlords and agents with rented property in Tamworth regularly need tenancy clear-outs and deep cleans. The town also has retail parks, a large business area and the Snowdome, and commercial managers should ask for out-of-hours attendance and waste paperwork. See our landlord and letting agent guide.

Tamworth railway station is on the main line between Birmingham and Derby. Staffordshire Police and Staffordshire Fire and Rescue Service cover most of the town, and Tamworth Borough Council can advise on neglected and empty properties.

Estates built for families who stayed

Tamworth's estates from the 1960s onwards were often bought by families who stayed for decades. Today those houses are in their second or third generation of ownership, and clearing them can be an emotional job as well as a practical one. Ask the team to work sensitively and to set aside items of sentimental value.

If several family members are involved, appoint one person to give instructions and receive reports, so that the team is not given conflicting directions. A single written list of items to keep, items to discard and items to donate saves arguments and mistakes.

A common situation in Tamworth

In a town where many homes were built in the same decade, similar problems arise at similar times. Roof spaces and cavity walls age, drains collapse and loft insulation attracts rodents. A homeowner finds droppings in the loft or a bad smell in a kitchen, and wants to know whether it is serious.

Rodent droppings and nests can carry disease, so they should not be swept or vacuumed. A specialist wears protective equipment, removes the contaminated material, disinfects and, where needed, replaces insulation. A pest controller should deal with the cause first, so the problem does not return.

Who to contact in Tamworth

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Tamworth is Staffordshire Police, and the fire and rescue service is Staffordshire Fire and Rescue Service. The local authority is Tamworth Borough Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes B77 to B79, and nearby places include Wilnecote, Glascote, Fazeley, Amington, Kettlebrook, Bolehall.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
