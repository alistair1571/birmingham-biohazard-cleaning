Tamworth sits at the point where the River Tame and the River Anker meet, about 14 miles north-east of Birmingham. It was the capital of the Kingdom of Mercia in the eighth and ninth centuries, but most of the town you see today dates from the last sixty years: its population grew from about 25,000 in 1961 to 64,000 in 1981, as families moved out of the West Midlands conurbation into new estates.

Two things follow from that, and they shape this page. First, the town has a history of flooding, and the Environment Agency is still at the early stage of planning a flood-risk scheme, so floods remain a live question. Second, a large share of Tamworth's homes were built or let in the same twenty years, and the people who moved in are now at the same stage of life. This page is about what that means for a family or landlord who needs a specialist. A Birmingham postcode in a Staffordshire town

Tamworth's postcodes begin with B, the Birmingham area, but the town is in Staffordshire. It moved entirely out of Warwickshire in 1889, and a few nearby places are still across the line. That catches people out, particularly when they search for a council, a coroner or a pest service.

Where two rivers meet: flooding

Tamworth has a long history of flooding, and river levels were very high in both 2024 and 2025. The Environment Agency has begun early work on a flood risk management scheme for the Tame and Anker, including ground investigation, but it is at an early stage, so nothing about the scheme will protect a home this winter. If you live or let property near either river, or near the canal, plan as if it could happen.

What to do
Before the wet seasonSign up for Environment Agency flood warnings; check whether your insurance covers flooding; know where the stopcock and fuse board are; keep documents and valuables upstairs
While the water is risingMove people and pets to safety, don't walk or drive through flood water, and call 999 if anyone is in danger
Once it recedesPhotograph the damage at every level before moving anything; ring the insurer; keep receipts; don't switch on electrics that have been wet until they've been checked

Estates built in the boom years

Tamworth grew in steps: about 7,000 people in 1931, roughly 13,000 just after the war, 25,000 in 1961, 40,000 in 1971, 64,000 in 1981 and 72,000 by 2001. The expansion brought estates in places such as Glascote, Belgrave, Stonydelph, Amington and Wilnecote, and the people who arrived in those years are now elderly or have died.

That changes the kind of call we get.

  • A house lived in since the 1960s, 70s or 80s. Decades of belongings, a loft and garage that haven't been opened for years, and sometimes a home that has become unsafe through illness or self-neglect.
  • Families scattered across the Midlands. The children moved away, or the original move was from Birmingham or the Black Country, so relatives often manage from another city.
  • A death at home. The work is described under after death cleaning, and the question of who pays is answered in who cleans up after a death in a property.
  • Hoarding and neglect. Go in stages; hoarders clean up shows how, and who pays for a hoarder clean up covers the cost.

Managing it all from another city? Settle who has authority first, start with photographs, give the keys to a person you trust, write down anything that must be kept, and ask for before-and-after pictures afterwards.

Canals, towpaths and the edges of town

The Coventry Canal runs through Tamworth and joins the Birmingham and Fazeley Canal at Fazeley Junction. Waterside gardens, sheds and garages attract rats, and towpaths and wasteland can collect fly-tipping and needles. Pest control comes first, then cleaning; see after rodent clean up. Don't touch sharps; our needle sweep service page explains how to handle them.

Landlords, logistics and industrial units

Tamworth has a working economy of logistics, engineering and manufacturing, including the Wilnecote industrial area, and a large private rental sector.

  • Rented homes. Take dated photos first, hold the inventory and check-in report, and get advice before disposing of belongings. More on records is on the landlords and letting agents page.
  • Industrial units and yards. Empty units draw needles, human waste and pigeons; the relevant pages are human waste cleaning and pigeon guano cleaning. Mention gates, alarms and stored materials when you ring.
  • Out-of-hours work avoids disruption, though it costs more.

What changes the price

The bill reflects volume, contamination type, time left untreated, distance to the van and urgency. A first figure can usually come from photos, and a revised quote precedes any additional work.
