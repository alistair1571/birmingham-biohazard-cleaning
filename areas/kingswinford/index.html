Biohazard cleaning in Kingswinford

Specialist clean-up for homes, bungalows, gardens and outbuildings across Kingswinford and DY6, including Wall Heath, Wordsley and Pensnett. Whether it follows a death, long-term hoarding, rodents in the loft or shed, sewage, or a tenancy that ended badly.

Ringing about a relative's bungalow, a neighbour's overgrown garden or a rental you manage from afar? Begin with the address and whatever is visible from the pavement.

Kingswinford is a big place that still thinks of itself as a village. Most of it went up in a rush in the 1950s and 1960s, and the later estates, such as Charterfields in the 1970s, filled in around it. That left a town of semis, bungalows and quiet roads, with allotment-sized gardens, garages, sheds and lofts behind almost every house, and open ground and canal on the western edge where Dudley borough ends and Staffordshire begins.

The result is that the problems here rarely sit in one room. A death or a long illness might have left a bedroom in need of specialist cleaning, while the garage hides years of stored belongings and the shed has a rat run under it. This page follows the whole property, from the front door to the bottom of the garden, and explains what we do at each stage, what the council and the boundary mean for you, and what to expect when you ring. Get in touch and a team is lined up for your address, with the price laid out and agreed on paper beforehand. The organising is ours; the cleaning is done by the team that attends.

What a bungalow job looks like, room by room

Bungalows are a big part of the DY6 housing stock, and they are often the homes of long-term owners. A clean after a death or a period of neglect tends to go in a sequence, and knowing it helps you judge a quote.

  1. The approach and the doorway.The team checks access, parking, doorways and anything that might block removal. Tell us if the front door sticks, if a ramp has been fitted, or if the hall is narrow.
  2. The room affected.In a bedroom or living room after a death, the focus is on bedding, mattress, carpet, underlay and sometimes the floorboards beneath. These are the porous materials that hold fluids and odour. See after death cleaning.
  3. The kitchen and bathroom.If someone has been unable to look after the home, these rooms are usually the most urgent. Food, waste and human waste come first. See human waste cleaning.
  4. The loft.Single-storey homes often have a big, easily reached loft. It may hold droppings, nests, stored boxes or contaminated insulation. Don't sweep or vacuum droppings; leave them for protective cleaning.
  5. The garage and shed.Often the biggest surprise. Years of stored items, damp, food for birds and mice, and rubbish all collect here.
  6. The garden.Fly-tipped items, compost heaps, dog waste and sharps can be hidden in overgrown borders.
  7. The walk-through.Before the team leaves, each area is checked and photographed with dates.

Each step is priced separately, so you can see where the cost is and choose what is done first. A property can be cleaned in stages, which suits families who need to decide about belongings as they go.

Garages, sheds, lofts and gardens

These are the spaces people forget when they ask about a clean-up. In Kingswinford they are also where many of the real problems are.

  • Rats and mice. Garages, sheds and lofts beside gardens and open ground attract them. Get them treated before anything is cleaned; after rodent clean up covers the work. The council's own rodent service, and what it leaves to private firms, is set out on our Brierley Hill page.
  • Squirrels and birds. Nesting birds and bats are protected, so ask for advice before blocking anything up.
  • Stored belongings. A garage packed with decades of belongings is a clearance job, not a biohazard job, unless it is contaminated. Ask for the two to be priced separately.
  • Dumped waste. Overgrown plots and garden edges collect fly-tipping that can hide broken glass and needles. Keep children and pets out, and read the needle sweep service page before touching anything sharp.
  • Compost, dog waste and standing water. These breed pests and smell. A garden clear-out is often the first practical step before anything else is done.

The Staffordshire side of the line

Kingswinford sits inside Dudley borough, yet Staffordshire begins a short drive to the west. A house in Swindon, Himley or Wombourne can feel like Kingswinford and be in a different council area, covered by a different police force and sometimes by a different coroner. The rural part of the old Kingswinford parish went to Kinver in 1933 and to South Staffordshire in 1974, so the edges are not tidy.

  • In Kingswinford, Wall Heath, Wordsley or Pensnett: Dudley Council is your council (0300 555 2345 covers housing, environmental health and rubbish) and a sudden death is handled by the Black Country Coroner Service at Oldbury.
  • In South Staffordshire: a different council applies, and your council tax bill will confirm which. Whoever is dealing with a death can tell you which force and coroner have the case.
  • Emergencies: 999. For the release date of a property, 101.

When the problem is next door

Not every call is about your own house. Sometimes a neighbouring property is the problem: an overgrown garden, rubbish piling up, a smell, rats. You can report it, but the right route depends on the issue.

  • Rubbish and rats on private land: Dudley Council can serve notices on owners whose waste draws rats, and has taken people to court for ignoring them. Dudley Council Plus is the front door.
  • Smell or concern for the person living there: If you think a neighbour is unwell or hasn't been seen, call the police on 101, or 999 if you think they are in danger. Don't force your way in.
  • A rented property: The landlord or agent is responsible. Tell them in writing.
  • Cleaning it yourself: Don't. It isn't your property, and it may not be safe.

Once the owner or landlord decides the place needs cleaning, they are the one to contact us; the landlords and letting agents page covers the process.

Glass, canals and what's underneath

Kingswinford grew on mining, glass and canals, and the glass trade still defines the area, with the Stourbridge Glass Quarter and the glass museum at Wordsley nearby. Old industry means old drainage, culverts and ground that has been dug before. For householders, that matters only when something goes wrong.

  • Sewage and blocked drains. Public sewers belong to the water company; a blocked drain within your boundary is usually yours. See sewage clean-up.
  • Flooding after heavy rain. See flood damage clean up and flooded basement clean up.
  • Damp and cracks. Persistent damp, new cracks or doors that suddenly stick need a surveyor, not a cleaner. If the team spots signs of any of these, you'll be told.

For families, executors and landlords

  • Executors and relatives. Confirm who holds legal authority before a single item leaves the house; the blog on who cleans up after a death in a property sets out how that differs by tenure. Cleaning and clearing are separate jobs, and invoices should show them as separate lines.
  • Hoarding and neglect. Without the owner's consent or legal authority, leave belongings where they are. Support for the person themselves comes from their GP, adult social care or the charity Hoarding UK; the property side is covered in hoarders clean up and who pays for a hoarder clean up.
  • Landlords. Photograph first, hold on to the inventory and confirm the tenancy position before moving belongings. The cause of damage can decide a claim, as the insurance guide explains.
  • Smells. Cigarette smoke, damp and years of closed windows are all treatable at source. See cigarette odour removal.

Kingswinford biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Kingswinford and DY6?

Yes, including Wall Heath, Wordsley and Pensnett, plus nearby Brierley Hill, Stourbridge, Sedgley and Himley.

Is Kingswinford in Dudley or Staffordshire?

Dudley. The Staffordshire boundary is just to the west, so check your council tax bill if you're near it.

Can you clean a bungalow after a death?

Yes: assess, remove contaminated materials, disinfect and treat the odour at its source. Garages and sheds can be included or quoted separately.

Do you remove rodent droppings from a loft or shed?

Yes, nests and soiled insulation included. Getting rid of the animals themselves is for a pest controller, ideally before we start.

Can you work in stages?

Yes. Many families prefer to start with the urgent rooms and decide about the rest later.

What if I live too far away?

We can work from photographs, with a neighbour or solicitor holding the keys, and send you dated photos and a report afterwards.
