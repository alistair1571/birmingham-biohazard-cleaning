Stourbridge is a market town of nearly 57,000 people, and it holds a wide range of homes: Victorian terraces near the centre, dense streets around Lye and Wollaston, large detached houses around Pedmore and Oldswinford, and flats above shops along the ring road. The town was historically in Worcestershire, joined the Dudley borough in 1974, and still feels like a separate place from the Black Country towns around it.

That variety shapes the work. A large house lived in by one elderly owner is a different job from a rented terrace or a unit above a shop, and this year the town has also had to deal with something none of us expected: a grass fire in August that spread into homes in the Norton area. This page covers both the everyday calls and the unusual one, explains who does what in Dudley borough, and sets out what to expect from us. Ring or use the enquiry form and a team is found for the address, with the price broken down and signed off by you before work begins. The Norton fire: smoke, ash and getting back to normal

On Thursday 13 August 2026 a grass fire near Stourbridge Golf Club spread to nearby homes. According to the fire service, 19 homes were destroyed and 18 more were damaged, and around 130 properties were affected in total. Residents were evacuated, and Dudley Council took over leadership of the recovery from the emergency services on 17 August. The cause was still being investigated at the last report we saw. We are not the right place for official news, so for the latest on support, funds and access, use Dudley Council's own channels and be careful of unofficial appeals.

If your home was affected but still stands, these are the practical points we're most often asked about:

  1. Wait for permission to go back.Don't enter a damaged property until the fire service or the council says it is safe.
  2. Tell your insurer first.Photograph everything before it is moved or cleaned, and ask whether they have preferred contractors. Our insurance guide explains why the cause of damage matters to a claim.
  3. Treat ash and soot with care.Ash from burned buildings can contain harmful material. Avoid sweeping or vacuuming it dry, wear gloves and a mask if you must go in, and keep children and pets away.
  4. Don't wash it into drains.Hosing ash into gutters and drains spreads it. Ask the council or your insurer how to dispose of it.
  5. Smell is a sign, not the whole problem.Smoke gets into plasterboard, carpets, soft furnishings and loft insulation, and covering it up doesn't remove it. See smoke damage cleaning and fire damage cleaning.
  6. Look after yourselves.The council has offered free mental health support to those affected. It's reasonable to take it.

If you've found rodent activity, sharps or sewage in a damaged property, those are separate jobs; see after rodent clean up and sewage clean-up. We won't contact households unprompted, and if your insurer already has a contractor, use them.

When an elderly owner can no longer cope

Many calls from the leafier parts of Stourbridge involve a relative who has lived alone for years and has become unwell, fallen or died. The pattern is familiar:

  • Rooms shut off, with post piling up behind the door
  • Food, bedding or clothing that can't be dealt with any longer
  • A loft or garage full of old belongings, sometimes with rodents
  • Family living a long way off, unsure what they're allowed to do

Authority comes first: executors, attorneys and owners can approve work, and a relative without that status can't. Responsibility by tenancy is set out in our post on who cleans up after a death in a property; for a packed house, read hoarders clean up alongside who pays for a hoarder clean up. For a death, see after death cleaning. Anyone who dies suddenly at home in this part of the borough comes under the Black Country Coroner Service in Oldbury, and the house remains closed until it's released. For emergencies call 999, and use 101 for questions about release.

The Stour, the canal and wet ground

The River Stour runs through the town, and low-lying streets, particularly near the centre and Amblecote, have had to deal with flood warnings. The Stourbridge Canal and its Fens Pool arm add waterside land where rats and damp are common.

  • Flood water carries sewage and run-off, so treat it as contaminated and stay out of it. Drying and sanitising are covered under flood damage clean up, with cellars on a separate page.
  • Blocked or failing drains are the owner's problem within the boundary and the water company's beyond it.
  • Damp after the water has gone needs drying and checking before redecoration, not just cleaning.

Getting here: two stations, one half-mile branch

Relatives often arrive by train. Stourbridge Junction is the main station, with services to Birmingham and Worcester. Stourbridge Town is at the end of a short branch, just over half a mile, served by a shuttle from the Junction, and is the one for the town centre. If someone is meeting the team with keys, say which station and whether they'll need a lift to the property. Parking near the ring road and the older streets is limited, so tell us where a van can stop.
