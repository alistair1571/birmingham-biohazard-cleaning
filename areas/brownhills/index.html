Biohazard cleaning in Brownhills, WS8

Brownhills is a former mining town in the Walsall borough, about six miles north-east of Walsall and 13 miles north-north-west of Birmingham. It lies to the south of Cannock Chase and neighbours Pelsall and Walsall Wood. Roughly 13,400 people lived in the Brownhills ward at the 2021 census.

Because it sits on the edge of the conurbation, people calling about a Brownhills property often also describe it by its neighbours, such as Aldridge, Burntwood or Lichfield. All are covered. Give the postcode (WS8 for most of Brownhills) and say what has happened, and the team will take it from there.

Homes in Brownhills

At the 2011 census Brownhills had around 5,170 dwellings, nearly half of them semi-detached. Many are former council houses, now managed by organisations including Walsall Housing Group and WATMOS, alongside privately owned and rented homes. That mix means jobs for landlords and housing providers as well as families.

Typical requests include clearing and cleaning after a death, hoarded or neglected properties, tenancy handovers where a property has been left contaminated, and sharps or human waste found in communal areas. If you are a landlord or agent, see our guide for landlords and letting agents.

Coal, canal and common land

Brownhills grew from a hamlet of about 300 people to more than 13,000 by the end of the nineteenth century on the strength of coal mining. The last pit closed in the 1950s. The Wyrley and Essington Canal runs through the town and meets the Daw End Branch at Catshill Junction, Chasewater lies to the north, and Brownhills Common and Clayhanger Common are close by.

Boats, canal-side homes and properties near open land can have their own difficulties, including damp, pest droppings and abandoned items. Subsidence linked to old mine workings and the Vigo Fault has been a long-running issue for buildings in the area, so it is worth asking a surveyor about the ground if you are buying or repairing after a clean-up.

Roads, transport and who to contact

Brownhills is served by the A5 and is close to the M6 Toll. It has no operating railway station, and bus routes link it to Birmingham, Walsall, Burntwood and Lichfield. Walsall Council is the local authority, and West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the town, while Lichfield and Burntwood sit across the border under Staffordshire Police.

If you are concerned about a neighbour's property, Walsall Council can advise about neglected or empty homes, and anyone can refer a household for a West Midlands Fire Service Safe and Well visit, which can include a conversation about hoarding.

Rural edge, urban problems

Brownhills sits on the edge of the conurbation, close to canal paths, commons and open land. That mix means two sets of problems: ordinary urban ones such as neglected flats and tenancy clear-outs, and rural-edge ones such as rodents in outbuildings, abandoned items near footpaths and fly-tipping on lanes. A job might involve a house, a garage and a garden shed.

When you call, list every building and outbuilding that needs attention, and tell the team about any access issues such as narrow lanes or locked gates. If land is not yours, for instance fly-tipping on council or canal land, report it to Walsall Council or the Canal and River Trust instead of tackling it yourself.

A common situation in Brownhills

A householder finds that rats have taken over a garage and a shed, with droppings and chewed material everywhere. The smell has started to reach the house. They want to know whether it is safe to go in and who should clear it.

Stay out until it has been assessed. A specialist will wear protective equipment, remove droppings and nests with wet methods, dispose of the waste lawfully and disinfect. Use a pest controller to close entry points, so the problem is not repeated.

Who to contact in Brownhills

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Brownhills is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Walsall Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes WS8, and nearby places include Walsall Wood, Clayhanger, Shire Oak, Pelsall, Aldridge, Chasetown.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
