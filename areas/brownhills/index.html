Brownhills sits at the edge of the Black Country, with the canal on one side, Chasewater and the commons on the other, and Staffordshire a short walk away. It was a mining town: the population jumped from a few hundred in 1801 to over 13,000 by 1891 as pits opened, and the last colliery closed in the 1950s. What's left is a town of mostly semi-detached homes, former council streets now owned or let in all sorts of ways, a high street that has struggled, and plenty of green and waterside land close to people's back gardens.

That edge-of-town character is why calls from Brownhills often differ from city ones. They involve sheds and garages as much as bedrooms, towpaths and common land as much as stairwells, and a mix of tenures where nobody is quite sure who is responsible. This page deals with those things. Phone or use the enquiry form and a specialist team is arranged for the address, with every cost agreed in writing beforehand. Our role is organising the job; the people on site carry it out.

Rural edge, urban problems

Plenty of Brownhills homes back onto open ground, and that changes what turns up.

  • Rats and mice in garages, sheds and lean-tos. Stored food, bird feed, old furniture and rubbish give rodents everything they need. Stay out of an outbuilding until it has been looked at, and don't sweep droppings dry. Pest control comes first and cleaning second; see after rodent clean up.
  • Fly-tipping on plots and tracks. Dumped rubbish can hide needles, nappies, food waste and sharp objects. Keep children and dogs away.
  • Human waste in sheltered spots. Bushes, garages and unused outbuildings are sometimes used as toilets or sleeping places. See human waste cleaning.
  • Squirrels, birds and bats in roofs. Cleaning a loft is straightforward only once the animals are out; nesting birds and bats are protected, and that needs proper advice before anything is blocked up.

Towpaths, commons and canalside land

The Wyrley and Essington Canal, known locally as the Curly Wyrley, runs through the town, and Chasewater and the commons are close by. These places are public and attract all sorts of use.

  • Needles and sharps on a towpath or common. Don't touch them. Keep people and animals away and report them. Our needle sweep service page explains how to handle sharps safely and how a sweep works.
  • Who owns the land? Canal land is generally the Canal and River Trust's, and commons are usually the council's. If the land isn't yours, report the problem to whoever owns it first. If it's on your own property or your tenant's, that's where we come in.
  • Water. After heavy rain, low ground, old drainage and neglected culverts can flood gardens and cellars. See flood damage clean up and flooded basement clean up.

Former council streets and who is responsible

A large share of the town's housing began as council homes, and ownership has since spread across social landlords, owner-occupiers and private landlords. So the first question is always whose property it is.

  • If you rent from a housing association or the council, tell them before you do anything. They have their own rules on hoarding, repairs and what happens after a death.
  • If you own a home that was once council-owned, it is yours to deal with, but many owners live elsewhere and discover problems late. A dated inventory and regular inspections help.
  • If you are a landlord, see our landlords and letting agents page for who needs to approve work and what paperwork to keep.
  • If a tenant has died, the home usually has to be cleaned, emptied and handed back. The blog post on who cleans up after a death in a property goes through each case.
  • If a relative has left a packed house, approach it patiently. Hoarders clean up explains the staged approach and who pays for a hoarder clean up covers the money.

The empty shopping centre and other vacant buildings

Brownhills High Street has had a long-empty site: the former Ravenscourt shopping centre, which Walsall Council says has been empty and largely derelict for years and has attracted vandalism and anti-social behaviour. Aldi owns most of it and has planned a new store, but it was still trying to buy some remaining units at the council's last published update, and the council was looking at its options.

Vacant buildings in any town centre bring a predictable set of risks: broken windows, forced doors, pigeons, rats, human waste and discarded needles. If you own or manage a unit, flat or shop that has stood empty:

  • Tell your insurer that the building is unoccupied, because policies often change.
  • Secure it before you clean it. There's no point disinfecting a building people can walk back into.
  • Clean for the hazard. See pigeon guano cleaning for droppings and biohazard cleaning services for mixed contamination.
  • Keep records. Dated photos and waste paperwork matter if there's a dispute with a landlord or insurer.

Who to call in Brownhills

Walsall Council handles housing, environmental health and rubbish complaints for the town. It has said it no longer runs its own pest control and points people to private firms, though it can act where rubbish on private land attracts rats. If there has been a sudden death, the Black Country Coroner Service, based in Oldbury, covers Walsall. Police can differ near the edge: West Midlands Police cover most of Brownhills, but Staffordshire Police cover places across the border such as Burntwood and Chasetown, so check which force is dealing with your case. For emergencies call 999, and use 101 to ask when a property will be handed back.

Getting there

Brownhills has no railway station, and the A5 and M6 Toll are the main routes in. That makes little difference to us, but it's worth telling us about parking, narrow access or rear-only entry to a property, as many of the older streets and rear garages have limited space.

Costs, in plain terms

Prices depend on what has happened and how far it has spread, the size of the property, how much has to be removed, whether sharps or rodent contamination are involved, access to the site and how soon you need it done. Photos and a short description usually give us enough for a first figure, and you'll see the final price in writing before anyone starts. If an estate, landlord or insurer must approve the quote, it helps to ask them early.

Brownhills biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Brownhills, Walsall Wood and Clayhanger?

Yes, along with Pelsall, Aldridge, Burntwood and Lichfield. Give us your postcode if you're near an edge.

Can landlords with former council houses get help?

Yes. Tenancy clear-outs, disinfection and odour treatment come with an itemised quote and dated photos.

Is Brownhills in Walsall or Staffordshire?

Walsall. It was historically in Staffordshire and borders it, which is why the police force can differ near the boundary.

Can you clear a garage or shed with rodent droppings?

Yes, after pest control. Stay out until it's been assessed and tell us what is stored inside.

Who do I report needles on the canal towpath to?

The landowner, which is usually the Canal and River Trust for canal land, or the council for commons. Don't pick them up yourself.

Is there a railway station?

No. The team travels by road. Mention parking and access when you ring.

Does someone have to wait at the property?

No, a relative or neighbour can hold the keys.
