Biohazard cleaning in Smethwick

Specialist clean-up for terraces, flats, shared houses and small commercial premises across Smethwick, B66 and B67, including Bearwood, Cape Hill, Uplands, Londonderry and Windmill Lane. Whether it's a death, hoarding, needles, sewage or a let that went wrong.

Smethwick packs a lot into a small area. About 50,000 people live in the built-up area, and the streets are a patchwork: red-brick terraces from the turn of the twentieth century, 1930s semis, flats that replaced earlier high-rise estates, and newer homes built on old industrial land, such as the Cape Hill brewery site. The town sits four miles west of Birmingham city centre, but its boundary with Birmingham runs right through Bearwood.

That density shapes the work. Neighbours are close, buildings are shared, and in many homes several adults live under one roof, whether as a family, as tenants or as housemates. This page covers the questions that come with it: which council and coroner apply, how a shared house is handled, what to do when a family needs the property released quickly, and how a team gets in and out of a tight street. Call or send an enquiry and we'll find a team to attend, with each item priced and agreed in writing beforehand. We coordinate; the attending team does the cleaning.

Bearwood and the two-council street

Bearwood Road is where Smethwick and Birmingham meet, and the boundary can fall between two houses. Which side an address is on decides who you contact.

AddressCouncilCoroner for a sudden or unexplained death
On the Smethwick side of Bearwood, and elsewhere in B66 and B67Sandwell CouncilBlack Country Coroner Service, based at Jack Judge House in Oldbury
On the Birmingham side (B17 and B21 addresses nearby)Birmingham City CouncilBirmingham and Solihull Coroner Service

A council tax bill will tell you which side you're on. It's worth knowing before a death, because the coroner controls when the body is released and the house stays off limits until then. Emergencies go to 999; 101 is the number for asking police about the property's release. Our Birmingham page covers the Birmingham side in more detail.

When timing matters to the family

Some families need a funeral to happen quickly, for faith or personal reasons, and a death at home can mean waiting for the coroner. If that's your situation:

  1. Tell the coroner's officer and the funeral director earlyabout any timing that matters to your family. They decide the release, and we can't speed it.
  2. Plan the clean for the day of release.Ring us in advance with the address and a rough description, and we can have a team ready to start as soon as the property is handed back.
  3. Say what must be handled with care.Religious items, documents and keepsakes can be set aside or left in place if you tell us beforehand.
  4. Choose who attends.A relative, a neighbour or a solicitor can meet the team; some families prefer not to be present at all.
  5. Ask about privacy.The team can park and work as quietly as the street allows, which matters in terraces where neighbours know each other.

See after death cleaning for what the work involves, and the guide on who cleans up after a death in a property for who is responsible by tenancy type.

Shared houses and multiple occupancy

A large share of Smethwick's older terraces is rented, and many are let to several people at once. That changes how a clean-up works.

  • Several tenancies, one problem. One person's room may be contaminated while the rest of the house is fine. Tell us which rooms are affected and which tenants are still living there.
  • Shared kitchens and bathrooms are the usual source of complaints: waste, food, human waste and blocked drains.
  • Who gives instructions. Usually the landlord or managing agent, though a tenant with the lead name may be the contact. Say who is authorised to approve spending.
  • Check the rules. Larger shared houses can be subject to licensing and safety conditions, and councils inspect them. Ask Sandwell Council or your agent if you aren't sure what applies to yours.
  • Evidence for deposits and claims. Photograph before touching anything, keep the inventory and check-in report, and ask for dated before-and-after photos. The landlords and letting agents page explains what paperwork to keep, and the insurance guide covers when cover applies.

Flats, stairwells and replaced estates

Smethwick has rebuilt itself more than once. The Windmill Lane high-rises were demolished in 1988, the West Smethwick estate was redeveloped as Galton Village in the 1990s, and the Cape Hill brewery site has become housing. The result is a mix of older terraces and newer blocks, often side by side.

  • Communal areas. Needles, human waste and rubbish in stairwells and entrances are for the managing agent or landlord to commission. See needle sweep service and human waste cleaning. Don't pick up sharps by hand.
  • Odour between floors. Smell can travel through ducts and shared walls, so neighbours notice quickly. Tell the managing agent early.
  • Lifts and bin stores. Say whether the lift works, how many floors the team has to carry from, and where waste can be stored.
  • Service charges. Leaseholders may be asked to contribute to communal clean-ups. Check the lease and the agent's notice.

Canals, cuttings and rats

The Birmingham Canal runs through Smethwick, with Brindley's original line from 1769 and Telford's deep cutting of 1829 crossing under the Galton Bridge. Waterside land, old culverts and overgrown banks attract rats, and gardens and yards close to the canal are the usual places to find droppings. Pest control comes first and cleaning second; Sandwell Council's own pest service is described on our Oldbury page, and the work that follows treatment is in after rodent clean up. For drains and sewage, see sewage clean-up.

Getting a team in and out

Smethwick is well connected by rail, with Smethwick Rolfe Street, Smethwick Galton Bridge and The Hawthorns stations, and by the West Midlands Metro, which opened in 1999. For relatives travelling in, that makes it easy to meet the team. For the team, what matters is the street: on-street parking, terraces with no rear access and shared entrances all affect the carry and the quote. Tell us:

  • how far the van will be from the door
  • whether the property has a rear entry or only a front door
  • the floor and whether there is a lift

What changes the price

Volume to remove, the kind of contamination, how long it has been left and the carry from floor to van drive the figure. Shared properties can need extra planning if tenants remain in residence, and a change of scope on site means a pause and a revised quote.

Smethwick biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Smethwick and Bearwood?

Yes, including Cape Hill, Uplands, Londonderry and Windmill Lane, plus nearby Oldbury, West Bromwich, Handsworth and Harborne.

Which council covers Bearwood?

It is split: the Smethwick side is Sandwell Council and the Birmingham side is Birmingham City Council. Your council tax bill will say.

Can you clean one room in a shared house?

Yes. The affected rooms are treated and the rest left alone, and the quote lists each area separately.

Can you work around a quick funeral?

We can't change when the coroner releases a property, but we can have a team ready to start on the day it does.

Can you clear needles from a communal stairwell?

Yes, with the sharps collected into approved containers and the area disinfected. Keep residents away until it's done.

I'm abroad or out of the area. How does this work?

Keys can be left with a relative, agent or solicitor, and the records are emailed once the job is done.
