Biohazard cleaning in Smethwick, B66 and B67

Smethwick lies immediately west of Birmingham in the Metropolitan Borough of Sandwell, and many streets run straight into Edgbaston, Harborne and Bearwood. Its postcodes are B66 and B67, with B21 and B17 on the Birmingham side of the boundary. Which council covers an address depends on which side of the line it sits, so check your council tax bill if you are unsure.

The specialist teams cover both sides of the boundary. Give the postcode when you call and describe what has happened.

Dense terraces, flats and shared houses

Smethwick is densely built. Victorian and Edwardian terraces line many streets, with post-war council estates and tower blocks mixed in. The Bearwood area has a mix of shared houses, flats and family homes, and a large private rented sector. That means regular tenancy clear-outs, shared house clean-ups where rooms have been left in a poor state, and sharps or human waste in communal areas.

Ask landlords and agents for an itemised quote, dated photographs and waste transfer paperwork. Our guide for landlords and letting agents shows what to request.

Industry, canals and Galton Bridge

Smethwick was a centre of the Industrial Revolution. The Soho Foundry, where Boulton and Watt built steam engines, is here, as are the Smethwick Pumping Station and the Galton Bridge, a cast-iron canal bridge designed by Thomas Telford and completed in 1829 over the deep Galton Valley cutting. Cape Hill was the home of the Mitchells and Butlers brewery.

Older industrial buildings, many now converted or empty, and the surrounding canals bring their own problems: pigeon droppings, abandoned items, damp and fly-tipping. See our pages on pigeon guano and needle sweeps.

Trains, trams and local contacts

Smethwick Galton Bridge and Smethwick Rolfe Street stations are on the lines into Birmingham, and the West Midlands Metro runs nearby. Sandwell Council is the local authority for the Smethwick side of the boundary, and Birmingham City Council covers Bearwood's Birmingham side. West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the area.

If a property has been the scene of a crime or an unexplained death, cleaning can only begin once the police have released it. Once it is safe to enter, ring for advice about the next steps.

Shared houses and multiple occupancy

Smethwick has many shared houses and houses in multiple occupation. These raise particular issues: several tenants, different rooms in different states and sometimes disputes about who is responsible. Photograph each room before work starts and keep a written record of who authorised what.

For landlords, the clean-up should be quoted room by room so that costs can be allocated fairly between tenants and the deposit scheme. Ask for photographs of each room before and after, and keep the waste transfer note with the tenancy file.

A common situation in Smethwick

A letting agent is asked to inspect a shared house after several complaints from neighbours. Inside, they find overflowing bins, food waste in bedrooms and a toilet that has not been working for some time. Some tenants have left; others are still living there.

The first step is to protect people: restrict access to the worst areas, arrange alternative accommodation if needed and involve the landlord. Specialists can then clear and clean in phases while people remain on site, though in some cases it is better to wait until the house is empty.

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Smethwick is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Sandwell Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste.
