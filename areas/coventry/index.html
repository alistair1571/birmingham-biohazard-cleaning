Coventry has its own coroner and its own council services. If you're used to the Black Country pages on this site, the next section explains what's different.

Coventry is the tenth largest city in England, with about 345,000 people, and it is not part of the Black Country. It sits roughly 19 miles east of Birmingham, has been its own county in the past, and still runs its own council, its own coroner and its own pest service. For anyone who needs a specialist cleaner, that makes a practical difference to who you contact and in what order.

The city is also unusual in other ways. It was rebuilt after the November 1940 Blitz, so its centre and many suburbs date from the 1950s and 1960s, alongside older terraces that survived. It has two big universities, Coventry and Warwick, and tens of thousands of students and overseas residents who live in rented homes. And its council pest service has a waiting list long enough to affect cleaning. This page deals with those things first, because they are what people in Coventry tell us they didn't know. After a sudden death the house stays closed to cleaners until the police or coroner's officer hands it back. The officer dealing with the case, or the funeral director, can tell you which coroner is involved. Dial 999 in an emergency; 101 is the number for asking police about release. Rats and mice: the council service, the queue and what to do while you wait

Coventry City Council has its own pest team. As we write, it deals with rats, mice, fleas, wasps, bed bugs, German cockroaches, black ants, moles and grey squirrels inside the home. Rats and bed bugs in a home are treated free, mice carry a charge, and fleas, wasps and ants cost more, with concessions on some. Mice in sheds, garages and gardens are excluded. Charges change, so confirm them on the council's own page.

The point that matters for cleaning is the wait. The council says that at busy times appointments can be four to six weeks away, and you book online or by phone. Cleaning shouldn't begin until the animals have been dealt with, so a long queue can leave a contaminated kitchen or loft sitting for weeks. If that's you:

  • Consider a private pest controller if you can't wait. Ask for a written report on what was found and what was done.
  • Protect food and surfaces. Seal food in hard containers, keep children and pets out of affected rooms, and wash your hands after contact.
  • Leave droppings and loft insulation alone. Sweeping or vacuuming them dry puts harmful dust in the air.
  • Photograph what you find and note the dates, for a landlord or insurer.
  • Ring us in the meantime. We can plan the clean-up for the day treatment is finished; see after rodent clean up.

If you rent, the council's own page says owners and occupiers are responsible for controlling pests on their premises. Tell your landlord or agent in writing and keep a copy.

Students, overseas tenants and the sudden departure

Coventry University is in the city centre, the University of Warwick is on the southern edge, and Arden University is based nearby. Between them the two big universities have had around 60,000 students, and about 15,000 of them are non-UK nationals who stay for a year or more. The result is a large rented sector in places such as Earlsdon, Cheylesmore, Hillfields, Foleshill and around the centre.

That produces a particular kind of call. A tenant leaves, sometimes abroad, sometimes at short notice, and the landlord opens the door to a kitchen full of food waste, bins that haven't been emptied, a bathroom with mould, mattresses stained with bodily fluids, and belongings left behind.

  1. Photograph before touching anything,with dates.
  2. Check the tenancy positionbefore disposing of belongings. If a tenant has gone abroad, your legal position matters, so take advice from your agent, the deposit scheme or a solicitor.
  3. Gather the paperwork:the tenancy agreement, inventory, check-in report and any messages with the tenant.
  4. Ask for an itemised quotethat separates cleaning from clearance, and for dated before-and-after photographs.
  5. Plan around the calendar.Tenancies tend to end together, so book early for the late-summer changeover.

The landlords and letting agents page lists the paperwork worth keeping, and the insurance guide explains when an insurer may contribute. A house that was used for growing is a different job again: cannabis farm clean up.

A rebuilt city: terraces, estates and tall narrow houses

Coventry's housing runs from tall three-storey terraces to 1950s and 1960s rebuilds. The first council houses were let in 1917, large developments followed in the 1920s and 1930s, and post-war suburbs such as Tile Hill, Wood End and Stoke Aldermoor were added after the war. For a cleaner, the housing type decides the practicalities:

  • Tall terraces. Waste has to be carried down narrow stairs, which takes longer and affects the quote. Tell us how many floors and where the van can stop.
  • Estates and suburbs. Many have long-term owners and plenty of belongings. See hoarders clean up and who pays for a hoarder clean up.
  • Flats. Shared stairs and bin stores mean neighbours notice problems quickly. Needles and human waste in communal areas are handled by a specialist team; see needle sweep service and human waste cleaning.

Rivers, the canal and wet ground

The River Sherbourne runs through the centre and the Coventry Canal starts at the city basin. If you live near either, check the Environment Agency's flood-risk service for your address. If water has got into a home, it carries sewage and run-off and should be treated as contaminated; see flood damage clean up and sewage clean-up.

What changes the price

Cost tracks how much has to go, what it is, how long it has sat, how many stairs are involved and how urgent it is. A few photos usually give a first figure, and any extra work is quoted afresh before it happens.

Coventry biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Coventry and the CV postcodes?

Yes, including Foleshill, Earlsdon, Cheylesmore, Tile Hill, Canley, Binley and Allesley, plus Kenilworth and Nuneaton if you give us a postcode.

Which coroner covers Coventry?

Coventry City Council has its own Coroner's Service. It is separate from the Black Country coroner.

Can I get the council to deal with rats?

Yes. The council treats rats in homes free of charge, but there can be a wait of several weeks. We clean up afterwards.

Can a landlord get a student house cleaned after a tenancy?

Yes, with an itemised quote, dated photographs and written confirmation of the waste removed.

Can you clean a narrow three-storey terrace?

Yes. Tall terraces take longer because everything is carried down the stairs.

I'm abroad. Can you manage without me?

Yes. Keys can be held by a relative, agent or solicitor, and the paperwork is sent once the job is done.
