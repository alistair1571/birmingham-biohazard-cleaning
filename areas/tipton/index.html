Tipton grew from a handful of hamlets into a crowded industrial town in a little over a century. Its population went from about 4,000 in 1800 to more than 30,000 by 1900, driven by coal, ironstone and the canals. Most of the Victorian housing was cleared by the end of the 1970s, and what stands now is largely the council estates built from the late 1920s onwards, newer private homes, and pockets of older property near the canal and the centre.

So when someone rings from Tipton, the sensible first question is rarely "what's gone wrong?" It's "whose home is this?" The answer decides who can approve work, who pays and who needs to be told. This page is arranged around the people who tend to call: tenants, relatives, landlords and neighbours. It also explains the estates, the canal and the council boundary, so you can see how they affect a job. Phone or send the form and we'll source a team for the property; the quote lists each item and you approve it on paper before a bag is filled. Our part is coordination, and the crew who attend do the hands-on work.

Which of these sounds like you?

"I'm a tenant and something has happened in my home." Tell your landlord or housing officer first. Council and housing association tenancies have their own rules for repairs, hoarding, access and what happens after a death, and the landlord may arrange or charge for work. If you're a private tenant, write to the landlord or agent, keep copies, and don't remove damaged items before photographing them. If it's a health risk, such as sewage or sharps, say so in the message.

"I'm a relative and a family member has died, or gone into hospital or care." Check who holds authority before anything is cleared: an executor, an attorney or the owner. After a sudden death the house is off limits to cleaners until it has been handed back. The work itself is described under after death cleaning, and the question of who pays is answered in who cleans up after a death in a property. If the house is full of belongings, hoarders clean up is the better starting point.

"I'm a landlord or agent and the tenants have gone." Take dated photos before anything is moved, keep hold of your inventory, and get advice on what you may do with abandoned belongings. Tipton has plenty of private lets, and the typical discoveries are rubbish, needles, human waste, rodents and evidence of a grow; cannabis farm clean up and the landlords and letting agents page cover the next steps.

"I live next door and something is wrong." If you think someone is in danger, call 999 and don't force entry. For a welfare concern that isn't an emergency, call 101. For rubbish, rats or a smell from a neighbouring property, report it to the council that covers the address; the next section explains which one that is. Don't try to clean it yourself.

Sandwell or Dudley? The boundary through the town

Tipton has been split between two boroughs since 1974, so the council depends on the road. Most of the town is in Sandwell, but a fragment went to Dudley in 1966, and the Dudley Port and Coseley edges sit close to the line.

The estates: eras and what they leave behind

Tipton's council housing arrived in waves, starting with estates such as Shrubbery in Tipton Green, Tibbington, Moat Farm at Ocker Hill and Cotterill's Farm in the late 1920s and 1930s. The last big council development, Glebefields at Ocker Hill, came in the early 1960s, and the Foxyards estate in the mid-1960s was the last big private one.

The first tenants and buyers of the oldest estates are often the ones now ageing in place. Cleaning calls from those streets frequently follow a fall, an illness or a death. Costs in those cases are covered in who pays for a hoarder clean up.

The canal side: rats, damp and water

Tipton is known as the "Venice of the Black Country", and the Old and New Main Lines of the Birmingham Canal Navigations still run through it. The towpaths are now popular for walking and cycling. For a householder, water brings predictable problems:

  • Rats. Plots that run down to the water suit them. A pest controller deals with the animals; the aftermath is covered in after rodent clean up.
  • Damp after a leak or flood. Persistent damp needs the cause fixed before it is cleaned or redecorated. See flood damage clean up.
  • Needles and fly-tipping on towpaths. Don't touch them. Report them to the landowner, usually the Canal and River Trust, and read our needle sweep service page for safe handling.
  • Sewage backups. See sewage clean-up. A blocked public sewer is the water company's problem; a private drain is generally the owner's.

Old ground, new homes

Tipton was once nicknamed the "town of a hundred tips" because of its spoil heaps, and much of the housing sits on land that was mined, quarried or used by factories. Several estates, including Standbridge Park, were built on old industrial sites. None of this means your house is unsafe, and we don't make that claim. But new cracks, sloping floors or sticking doors belong with a surveyor and your insurer, not a cleaner. How an insurer treats the cause of a loss is explained in the insurance guide.

What changes the price

Tipton biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Tipton, Princes End and Great Bridge?

Yes, along with Ocker Hill, Tipton Green, Horseley Heath and Dudley Port, plus nearby Dudley, Wednesbury, West Bromwich and Oldbury.

Which council covers Tipton?

Mostly Sandwell Council, with a part in Dudley borough. Your council tax bill will say which.

Can you clean a house after a cannabis grow?

Yes, including damp, mould and contaminated materials. Electrics need an electrician's check before use.

Can you help if the property isn't mine?

Only with the owner's or landlord's approval, or that of someone with legal authority. We'll tell you who needs to be involved.

Can a neighbour or relative hold the keys?

Yes. A relative, neighbour, agent or solicitor can meet the team, and the records are sent afterwards.

Is a clean the same as a clearance?

No. One treats contamination, the other empties rooms, and a fair quote shows each as its own line.
