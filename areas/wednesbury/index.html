Biohazard cleaning in Wednesbury, WS10

Wednesbury is a Black Country town in the Metropolitan Borough of Sandwell, on the River Tame, between West Bromwich, Darlaston, Walsall and Tipton. Its postcode is WS10, shared with Darlaston, and junction 9 of the M6 is on its doorstep. It is known for tube-making and heavy industry, and for the Wednesbury Riots of the 1740s, which followed the visit of John Wesley.

Sandwell Council is the local authority, and the specialist teams cover Wednesbury and the neighbouring areas of Darlaston, Tipton and West Bromwich. Give the postcode when you call and describe what has happened.

A town of terraces, estates and tram stops

The housing is a mix of Victorian terraces, inter-war council houses and post-war estates such as Friar Park, with some newer developments. West Midlands Metro stops at Wednesbury Parkway and Great Western Street, making the town easy to reach from Birmingham and Wolverhampton.

A large part of the older housing is privately rented. When a tenancy ends badly, landlords and agents commonly need rubbish cleared, sharps and human waste removed and the property cleaned and deodorised. Our landlord and letting agent page explains what records to ask for.

The River Tame and flooding

The River Tame runs through Wednesbury and the area has seen flooding from surface water and blocked drains in heavy rain. If you live close to the river or a low-lying street, it is worth checking your postcode on the government's flood risk service. Flood water in a home is contaminated with sewage and needs more than a mop and bucket.

See our flood damage clean-up and sewage clean-up pages for what the work involves, and the first-hour advice for what to do before anyone arrives.

Industrial units and local contacts

Wednesbury has a large number of industrial units, many of them close to the M6. Empty and neglected units attract fly-tipping, rough sleeping and pigeons, leaving needles, human waste and droppings. Managers should ask for a written quote, waste paperwork and, if needed, out-of-hours attendance.

West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the town. If a property has been the scene of a crime, cleaning can only start once the police have released it.

After the flood water goes

When water has gone down, the work is far from over. Flood water carries sewage, and anything it has touched may need to be cleaned or removed. Porous materials such as carpets, underlay, plasterboard and upholstered furniture usually cannot be saved. Hard surfaces can be washed, disinfected and dried.

Take photographs before anything is moved and keep receipts. Contact your insurer early, as they may want to inspect first or appoint their own contractor. If you rent, tell your landlord straight away and keep a copy of your message.

A common situation in Wednesbury

A house on a low-lying street floods after heavy rain, and the water is brown and smells of sewage. The family moves upstairs or to relatives, unsure whether the downstairs can be saved. The insurer wants to inspect, the landlord wants photographs, and nobody is sure who should start.

Take photographs, keep receipts and call your insurer. A specialist can then extract water, remove ruined materials, clean and disinfect, and start drying. Do not switch on electrics in a wet room, and do not use a domestic vacuum on flood water.

Who to contact in Wednesbury

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Wednesbury is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Sandwell Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes WS10, and nearby places include Great Bridge, Moxley, Darlaston, Tipton, West Bromwich, Friar Park.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
