Wednesbury is small enough to cross on foot and busy enough to have its own motorway junction, its own tram stops and a retail park beside the M6. It has been a coal, iron and tube town for centuries, and it is now a mix of council-built estates, older terraces, rented flats, retail units and depots, all clustered close to the River Tame.

What makes it tricky for someone searching for help is a quirk of the postcode. WS10 is shared with Darlaston, which is in a different borough with a different set of council services. So a household one road away may have an entirely different answer to "who do I ring about rats?" This page starts there, then covers the places Wednesbury people tell us they actually need help: after floods and drain failures, in rented homes, on industrial and retail sites, and when a family needs to know what to do after a death. The River Tame, heavy rain and the first 48 hours

Wednesbury sits near the source of the River Tame, and in heavy rain low-lying streets and blocked drains are the usual trouble spots. Flood water is contaminated: it carries sewage and run-off, and shouldn't be treated as simple water damage. If you're facing it, this is the order that usually works:

First 24 hours

  1. Keep everyone out of the water, and don't touch electrics or appliances that water has reached. If the power is affected, call your electricity network operator on 105 or the fire service rather than trying to switch anything off yourself.
  2. Photograph the water level and the damage before you move anything.
  3. Call your insurer and ask what they cover and whether they have preferred contractors.

Days two to five

  1. Get the water out and the building drying. Porous materials that have soaked up contaminated water, such as carpet, underlay, mattresses and plasterboard, often can't be saved.
  2. Keep receipts and a list of what is thrown away.

Before you redecorate

  1. Make sure the building is dry and the source is fixed. A repeat backup means a drainage fault that cleaning won't cure.

Estates, terraces and rented homes

Council housing in Wednesbury began in the early 1920s, and by 1959 the council had more than 5,000 properties. Estates such as Friar Park, Wood Green and Hateley Heath, which straddles the West Bromwich border, grew from that. Many of those homes are now privately owned or let, and the rest are with housing providers.

  • Long-term owners often hold a great many belongings and may live alone. Calls follow a fall, an illness or a death; see after death cleaning and hoarders clean up.
  • Private tenants and landlords need to treat damage in the tenancy order: photograph, notify, then clean. The landlords and letting agents page lists the paperwork that matters.
  • Council and housing association tenants should talk to their housing officer first, because the landlord may arrange or recharge the work.

Who is responsible for a home after a death depends on the tenancy; our post on who cleans up after a death in a property works through each case.

Retail parks, depots and empty units

Wednesbury has a motorway junction, a large retail site on land once occupied by a steelworks, a town-centre supermarket and distribution buildings. That adds a type of customer that residential pages don't serve: site managers, facilities teams and property agents.

  • Empty units and yards attract fly-tipping, needles, human waste and pigeons. See needle sweep service, human waste cleaning and pigeon guano cleaning.
  • Out-of-hours work avoids lost trade, though it costs more.
  • What to have ready: who holds keys and alarm codes, gate and height limits, where a van can stand, and any stored chemicals, so a biohazard clean isn't confused with an industrial one.
  • Records. Dated photographs, a short report and waste transfer paperwork protect you with landlords, insurers and tenants.

The ground under the town

Coal pits in Wednesbury were first recorded in 1315, and mineshafts had to be filled in before parts of the town could be redeveloped. This isn't a reason to worry about your home, and we don't suggest it is. It's a reason to take new cracks, sloping floors or sticking doors seriously, and to call a surveyor and your insurer rather than a cleaner.

Arriving by tram or motorway

The West Midlands Metro stops at Wednesbury Great Western Street and Wednesbury Parkway, and junction 9 of the M6 is close. Relatives travelling in can meet the team or a keyholder easily. For the team, what matters is the street: terraces with no off-street parking, shared entrances and the floor. Tell us how far a van will be from the door and whether there is a lift.

What changes the price

Cost follows the volume to remove, the type of contamination, how long it has been left and how far everything has to be carried. Urgent and out-of-hours calls cost more. A first figure can often come from photos, and extra work needs a revised price you've agreed.
