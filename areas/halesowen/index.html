Biohazard cleaning in Halesowen, B62 and B63

Halesowen sits on the south-western edge of the Black Country, in the Metropolitan Borough of Dudley, with Birmingham to the north-east and the countryside of the Clent and Lickey Hills to the south. Its postcodes, B62 and B63, belong to the Birmingham postal area even though the council is Dudley, which regularly confuses people trying to work out who covers what.

Historically Halesowen was part of Worcestershire and was a detached part of Shropshire until the nineteenth century, and the ruins of Halesowen Abbey still stand in the town. Today the specialist teams cover Halesowen, Hasbury, Hayley Green, Mucklow Hill, Cradley and the neighbouring villages, as well as the Birmingham suburbs a short drive away.

Family homes, bungalows and long-term owners

Halesowen is a commuter town with a good deal of owner-occupied housing: 1930s semis, post-war estates, and bungalows popular with older residents. In such homes, hoarding or neglect often develops quietly and only comes to light after a fall, a hospital stay or a bereavement. Families clearing a house for probate or sale are often shocked by the state of it.

If this is your situation, you do not need to deal with the whole house in one go. A team can work room by room, set aside paperwork and valuables, and leave the property clean, disinfected and ready for sale or let. Our after-death cleaning and hoarder clean-up pages explain what to expect.

Retail, offices and the town centre

The Cornbow Shopping Centre and the town centre, the retail parks along the A456 and the Mucklow Hill business areas all have commercial premises where sewage backups, pigeon droppings, vandalism and needles in car parks can need urgent attention. Managers should ask for out-of-hours attendance, a written quote and waste paperwork.

Properties along the Halesowen Road and the A456 are also exposed to road traffic and to flooding from blocked drains in heavy rain. If surface water or sewage has entered a building, see our flood damage clean-up page.

Green belt, brooks and local contacts

Halesowen is close to green belt land, the Leasowes park and the Illey and Clent area, and a number of small brooks run through it. Rural-edge homes and outbuildings can attract rats and mice, and rodent contamination in lofts and kitchens is a regular reason for a deep clean. See after-rodent clean-up.

Dudley Council is the local authority, and West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the town. The nearest mainline stations are not in Halesowen itself, so travel is by road and bus, and the team arrive by van.

Probate, sales and tight deadlines

Many Halesowen jobs are driven by a deadline: a house sale, a probate valuation or an end of tenancy. If you have a date to meet, say so when you call. A good team will plan the work backwards from it, tell you honestly whether it is realistic and, if needed, split the job so that clearing is done first and cleaning and deodorising follow.

Executors and solicitors often need a written summary and photographs for the estate file. Ask for them at the outset. If valuable or sentimental items are found, ask that they are placed in a labelled box and listed, so nothing is lost or disputed later.

A common situation in Halesowen

An elderly resident is admitted to hospital and then to a care home. Family members visit the bungalow to collect belongings and find the kitchen in an unsanitary state and the loft showing signs of rodents. They need the home cleaned before it can be let or sold.

A phased plan works well: remove food waste and perishables, deal with the loft, deep clean the kitchen and bathroom, then treat odours. Keep a record of the work for the estate or the sale, and ask the team to set aside documents and valuables.

Who to contact in Halesowen

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Halesowen is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Dudley Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes B62 and B63, and nearby places include Hasbury, Cradley Heath, Blackheath, Quinton, Hawne, Rowley Regis.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
