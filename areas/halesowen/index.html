Biohazard cleaning in Halesowen

Specialist clean-up for family homes, bungalows, flats and shops across Halesowen, Hasbury, Hayley Green, Lutley, Mucklow Hill and the B62 and B63 postcodes. After a death, a fall, a hospital stay, years of hoarding, rats in the loft or a property that has to be sale-ready by a date.

If you're dealing with a house that isn't yours, as executor, attorney, landlord or relative, tell us who you are and what you need it ready for. That's often more useful than the description of the mess.

Halesowen is mostly a town of private homes, many of them built in the thirty years after the Second World War, with Victorian and Edwardian terraces nearer the centre and the 1930s and 1940s suburbs around it. People stay in these houses for a long time. So the biohazard calls from here are rarely about a short let gone wrong. They are about a house that has been lived in for decades by one person or one family, and now has to be cleaned, cleared, valued or sold because something has happened: a death, a fall, a move into care.

That changes what you need from a cleaning company. You want someone who understands that there's a solicitor, a probate timetable and an estate agent in the background, and who can give you the written records they will ask for. This page is organised around that reality. It also deals with something Halesowen is unusual for: it has Birmingham postcodes, Dudley Council, and a boundary with Worcestershire a few minutes away. Ring or send an enquiry and we'll arrange a team to suit the property, with the price broken down and signed off in writing first. Organising it is our part; the cleaning belongs to whoever attends.

Which side of the line? Councils, coroners and postcodes

Halesowen's B62 and B63 postcodes are Birmingham's, but the town is in the Metropolitan Borough of Dudley, and the edges are close. Which council, and sometimes which coroner, applies depends on where exactly the house stands.

Your council tax bill settles the council question. For a death, the funeral director or the officer dealing with it can say which coroner has the case. The distinction matters because the coroner decides when a body can be released, and no cleaning can begin until the property is handed back. Dial 999 for an emergency, and 101 to ask police when a house will be released. Dudley Council's number for housing, environmental health and rubbish is 0300 555 2345.

A sale or probate on a deadline: what fits when

When a Halesowen house is going on the market, or has to be handed back, the cleaning is one item in a longer timetable. Here is how it usually fits, so you can see what has to happen before what.

In the first days

  • Establish who holds legal authority: executor, administrator, attorney or owner. Responsibility varies with the kind of home, as the post on who cleans up after a death in a property shows.
  • Wait for the police or coroner to release the property after a death. You can ring us in the meantime to plan, but nothing can be cleaned yet.
  • Photograph each room with dates, and secure the house. Tell the insurer if it is now empty, because policies often impose conditions.

In the first weeks

  • Agree what is to be kept: papers, jewellery, photographs, keepsakes. Tell us what to set aside and who should be present.
  • Get an itemised quote and send it to the solicitor or executor, who may need to approve it before the estate pays.
  • Book pest control first if there are rodents. Cleaning comes after.

Before valuation and viewings

  • Clean, disinfect and treat odour. Smell is what viewers notice first and surveyors note. Whether it is cigarette smoke, a death, damp, pets or years of closed windows, it needs treating at the source, not covering. See after death cleaning, cigarette odour removal and smoke damage cleaning.
  • Keep the report, photographs and waste paperwork for the file. A buyer's solicitor or surveyor may ask what was done.

After the clean

  • Clearance, repairs and decoration follow. A clean is not the same as a clearance, so ask for them to be priced separately.
  • Electrics, drains and damp may need their own trades. We'll say if we see something that does.

Executors sometimes ask whether they can claim the cost from the estate. Usually yes, but ask the solicitor before committing, and keep the invoices. Our guide on whether insurance covers biohazard cleaning covers when an insurer might contribute.

Family homes that outlast their owners

The calls we hear from the suburbs follow a pattern, and none of it is a judgement on the person who lived there.

  • A fall or a hospital stay. A parent goes in, and the family walks into a house nobody has entered for months. The kitchen or bathroom may be unusable, and there may be far more belongings than anyone realised.
  • Self-neglect and hoarding. Usually discovered rather than planned. Without the owner's consent, or legal authority, nothing should be cleared. Their GP, social services or Hoarding UK can support them; we deal with the property. See hoarders clean up and who pays for a hoarder clean up.
  • Bungalows and older houses. Single-storey homes where someone has lived alone can have an unnoticed problem for a long time. Stairs matter less, but the volume of belongings can be large.
  • A death found late. Fluids and odour move through carpets, underlay and floorboards, so starting soon after release limits the spread.
  • Distance. Plenty of relatives live far away. A neighbour or solicitor can hold the keys while we work from photos and report back.

The town centre, flats and shops

Halesowen's centre was largely rebuilt in the 1960s and 1970s and has been added to since, including the Cornbow Centre and a new bus station. Above and around the shops are flats and storage, with the usual shared stairs and doors, and a town of this size also has plenty of businesses with their own needs: retail parks, offices and the units around Mucklow Hill.

  • Flats. Odour carries between floors, and neighbours notice first. Tell the landlord or managing agent early, and tell us about stairs and lifts.
  • Shops and food outlets. Sewage backups, rodents and spills can close a business. Food businesses should speak to Dudley Council's environmental health officers before reopening. See sewage clean-up.
  • Out of hours. Evenings and early mornings avoid lost trade, though they cost more.
  • Landlords. For a flat or house left in a state at the end of a tenancy, see biohazard cleaning services and the landlords and letting agents page. Check the tenancy position before clearing belongings.

Halesowen is often described as one of the biggest towns in the country without a railway station, so everything arrives by road. When you ring, mention parking and whether there are steps or a narrow approach.

Fields, brooks and the edge of the green belt

Halesowen is where the Black Country gives way to open land, with the Leasowes park, the Clent and Lickey hills in view and farmland not far away. Houses on the edge have their own problems.

  • Rats and mice. Outbuildings, lofts and garages near fields attract rodents. Dudley Council runs a rat and mouse service, though it doesn't treat pigeons or squirrels; the Brierley Hill page explains the scope. Pest control comes first, then after rodent clean up.
  • Droppings in lofts. Don't sweep them dry, and don't disturb insulation yourself.
  • Blocked drains and surface water. Heavy rain can flood low roads and gardens. If water has reached your home, see flood damage clean up. Sewage from a public sewer is the water company's concern, and a private drain is generally the owner's.

Halesowen biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover B62 and B63?

Yes, including Halesowen, Hasbury, Hayley Green, Lutley, Mucklow Hill and Cradley, plus nearby parts of Stourbridge, Rowley Regis and Birmingham.

Which council is Halesowen in?

Dudley Council, despite the Birmingham postcodes. Check your council tax bill if you're near the edge.

Can you clear a house for probate?

Yes. We can clean, disinfect and clear, set aside whatever the executor wants kept, and provide dated photographs and a report.

Do you remove rat or mouse droppings from a loft?

Yes, including nests and contaminated insulation. Stopping the rodents is a separate pest-control job you'll need to arrange.

How long before a property can be sold?

It depends on the extent of the work and on the probate and estate-agent timetable. We'll tell you how long the clean takes once we've seen the property, but not how long a sale will take.

What if I can't be there?

That's fine: a trusted person can hold the keys and the records are sent to you afterwards.
