Biohazard cleaning in Walsall, WS1 to WS5

Walsall is a market town and the administrative centre of its Metropolitan Borough, which also includes Aldridge, Brownhills, Bloxwich, Willenhall and Darlaston. Postcodes WS1 to WS5 cover the town and its inner suburbs. Walsall is known as the leather capital of Britain, a trade that gave the town its saddlery and leather-goods heritage, and it still has a Leather Museum.

The specialist teams cover the whole of the borough. Describe what has happened, give the postcode and ask for a written quote before work starts.

Inner-city terraces and rented homes

Caldmore, Pleck, Palfrey and Birchills are densely built areas of Victorian and Edwardian terraces, with a mix of owner-occupiers and private landlords. Rented houses and flats make up a large share of the stock. Landlords and agents regularly need rubbish removed, human waste and sharps dealt with, and properties cleaned and deodorised before they can be re-let.

Our guide for landlords and letting agents explains what a proper record should include, and the cost guide shows how clean-ups are priced.

Flats, tower blocks and communal areas

Walsall also has post-war estates and flats where communal stairwells, bin stores and entrances are managed by housing providers. Needles, human waste and bedding in communal areas are common, and clean-ups often need to happen quickly to reduce risk to residents.

Housing providers can ask for out-of-hours attendance, a risk assessment on request and documentation for each address. See needle sweep service and human waste cleaning.

Canals, the Arboretum and local contacts

The Walsall Canal runs close to the town centre, and the Arboretum is a large Victorian park on the edge of the centre. Walsall railway station is on the line between Birmingham and Rugeley. Walsall Council is the local authority, and West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the area.

Walsall Council can advise about neglected and empty properties, and anyone can refer a household for a fire service Safe and Well visit. If a death is unexplained, cleaning can only begin once the police have released the property.

Making communal areas safe quickly

In flats, hostels and houses with shared entrances, the first priority is to protect residents. If there are needles, human waste or blood in a shared area, restrict access and call a specialist rather than allowing a caretaker or cleaner to deal with it. Tell the team the exact location, the amount and how long it has been there.

Housing providers should keep a log of incidents, times of attendance and the work done. That record helps with repeat problems and with any later complaint. Ask the team for a short written report and photographs after each call-out.

A common situation in Walsall

A housing officer or letting agent receives a report that a flat has been abandoned or that a tenant has not been seen for some time. On entering, they find rubbish, human waste and possibly sharps. They need the property made safe quickly, with a clear record for the file.

A team can attend, make the scene safe, remove waste, disinfect and deodorise, and report back with photographs and a summary. If the case involves a sudden death or a crime, the police and coroner may need to release the property first; the cleaning team should wait for confirmation.

Who to contact in Walsall

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Walsall is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Walsall Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes WS1 to WS9, and nearby places include Bloxwich, Aldridge, Brownhills, Darlaston, Pelsall, Caldmore.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
