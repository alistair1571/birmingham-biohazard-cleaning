Biohazard cleaning in Sutton Coldfield

Discreet specialist clean-up for family homes, bungalows, flats and rentals across Sutton Coldfield, from Four Oaks and Wylde Green to Walmley, Boldmere, Mere Green and Streetly. After a death, a long illness, a flood, a break-in or years of neglect, we arrange a team that will treat the house, and the family, with care.

You don't need to have the whole picture. Tell us what you know, who is dealing with the house and whether anyone has the keys, and we'll help you work out the order things need doing in.

A call from Sutton Coldfield is rarely about a student let that was left in a mess. More often it comes from a son or daughter who lives two hours away, an executor who has just been given a set of keys, or a neighbour who has noticed something isn't right at a house that has been quiet for too long. The houses are big, the contents often represent a lifetime, and the people involved usually want it dealt with properly and quietly.

This page is for those situations. It covers what is different about clean-ups in a town of large houses and long-term owners, how to deal with an executor's worries about valuables and paperwork, what the nearby park and gardens bring with them, and a point that catches people out: Sutton Coldfield's postcodes cross council boundaries, so who you ring depends on exactly where the house is. Ring or send an enquiry and we'll line up a specialist team for the house. You'll see an itemised price in writing before anything begins, and the attending team does the hands-on work while we coordinate.

What's included

  • A first call at your pace, with no pressure to describe more than you want to
  • A look at the house from your photos or in person, then a quote broken down room by room and task by task
  • A plan that fits a bigger house: which rooms first, what can be done in stages, and how outbuildings, lofts and garages are handled
  • Removal of contaminated and unsalvageable materials, then cleaning and full disinfection of what remains
  • Smell dealt with where it starts, not covered up
  • Careful handling of belongings, with valuables, documents and sentimental items set aside on request
  • Contaminated waste carried off by a registered carrier, and the records handed to you
  • A dated photo record and short log of each stage for the executor, solicitor or insurer's file

Larger homes mean a different kind of job

Sutton Coldfield has a lot of detached and semi-detached houses on generous plots, and plenty of bungalows and flats where people have lived for decades. That affects the work in a few ways.

  • More to deal with. Several bedrooms, a conservatory, a garage, a shed, a loft and a garden all need to be looked at. A problem that begins in one room can have spread further than it appears.
  • More contents. A long-term home usually holds a great deal. Not everything that needs to be removed is contaminated, and not everything contaminated needs to go. Tell us what matters and we'll plan around it.
  • Staged work is common. A big job doesn't have to happen in one push. Agreeing a sequence can spread the cost and give a family room to make decisions. We can document each stage with photographs.
  • Older houses. Floors, loft insulation, old plumbing and years of accumulated items all influence the approach. We'll tell you if a floor needs lifting or a loft needs inspecting.

After a bereavement: executors and families

The most common reason people ring from Sutton Coldfield is that someone has died and the house now has to be dealt with. If you are the executor, or the family member holding the keys, these are the questions we hear most.

Do I have to do anything before the house is released? You can't start cleaning until the police or coroner have finished and released it. That said, ringing early helps. We can plan and quote in the meantime.

Who is responsible for the cost? Usually the estate, though a family member may need to pay first and claim it back. Keep itemised invoices. Our blog guide on who cleans up after a death in a property explains how that works for different homes, and our after death cleaning page sets out the process step by step.

Who sees the valuables? Cash, jewellery, important papers and keepsakes can be anywhere in a long-lived home. Tell us what you'd like set aside and who should be present. Agree in advance how anything that turns up is to be handled, and ask for a list of anything removed.

Does the house need to be empty before it's sold? A clean is not the same as a clearance. If you also need the property emptied, say so and we'll quote for the two jobs separately, so the invoices are clear for the solicitor.

What about the insurer? An empty house usually has conditions attached to its insurance. Tell the insurer that the property is now unoccupied. For more on how insurers tend to treat these situations, see our guide on whether insurance covers biohazard cleaning.

When a home hasn't been looked after

Not every call follows a death. In a town with many older residents living alone, families sometimes find that a parent has struggled for a long time: rubbish building up, rooms closed off, food left out, pets unwell, the kitchen or bathroom unusable. It isn't a judgement, and it's more common than people think.

  • Approach it gently. Clearing a home without the person's agreement can do harm, and you may not have the right to do it. A GP, adult social care or Hoarding UK can help with the person; we help with the house.
  • Go in stages. Safety first: exits, kitchen, bathroom, stairs and smoke alarms. Hoarders clean up explains how it works, and the blog guide on who pays for a hoarder clean up covers the money.
  • Human waste. Where there are faeces, urine or vomit, see human waste cleaning.

Sutton Park, big gardens and what lives in the loft

Sutton Park is one of the largest urban parks in England, and much of the town is green, with mature trees and large gardens. That is part of the appeal, and it also means more contact between homes and wildlife.

  • Rodents and squirrels in lofts and walls. Droppings, nests, chewed cables and contaminated insulation are a regular problem in houses with trees close by. Pest control comes first, and then the clean-up. See after rodent clean up.
  • Pigeons and roosting birds. Where droppings have built up in lofts, outbuildings or on ledges, don't sweep them dry. See pigeon guano cleaning.
  • Cleaning isn't proofing. A clean loft will be re-used if the gaps are still there, so make sure a pest specialist deals with entry points.

Drains, brooks and flooding

Parts of the town sit near brooks and low ground, and some properties along the southern edge of the park are in flood-risk zones, so check the Environment Agency's flood map for your address. Older drains in established streets can also back up. If you've had sewage or floodwater in the house, start with sewage clean-up, flood damage clean up or flooded basement clean up. Sewage from a public sewer is a matter for the water company, while a blocked private drain is generally the owner's.

Which council, police and coroner? It depends on the postcode

Most of Sutton Coldfield is covered by Birmingham City Council, which is why it comes under the Birmingham and Solihull Coroner Service. It also has its own town council, which runs local services but is not the body that deals with housing or environmental health.

The catch is the edges. Streetly, for example, has a B74 postcode and a Sutton Coldfield address, but lies in the Walsall area, and parts of it fall under Lichfield. Places to the north, such as Little Aston, are generally in Staffordshire. The council, environmental health team and sometimes the coroner can therefore differ from the one you'd expect.

If you aren't sure, ring the council tax office or check your council tax bill to see which council the property belongs to. If it's about a death, ask the police officer in charge or the funeral director which coroner is dealing with it. We can help work this out when you call.

Rented homes and landlords

Many Sutton Coldfield homes are let, often by owners who live elsewhere and rely on an agent. If a tenant has left a property contaminated, or has died, or a rental has been used for something it shouldn't have been, the landlord can arrange everything remotely: photographs, an itemised quote, a documented clean and a handover to the agent. See our landlords and letting agents page for who has to approve the work and what paperwork to keep, and cannabis farm clean up if the property has been used for growing.

Neighbours will still see people at the property, but nothing needs to be said about why.

Sutton Coldfield biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover Sutton Coldfield and B72 to B76?

Yes, including Four Oaks, Boldmere, Mere Green, Walmley, Wylde Green, Streetly and nearby Aldridge and Lichfield. If you're on the edge, give us your postcode.

Is Sutton Coldfield part of Birmingham?

Yes, since 1974 it has been in the Birmingham City Council area, though it has its own town council. Some neighbouring areas, such as Streetly, fall under other authorities.

Can you clean a large house after a death?

Yes, in stages if needed, with photographs for the executor.

Can you remove squirrel or rat droppings from lofts?

Yes, including nests and contaminated insulation. A pest controller should deal with how the animals are getting in.

Do I have to be there?

No. Keys can be left with someone you trust.

What if I live far away?

Many of our callers do. We can work from photographs, send photos and a report back, and talk to the solicitor or managing agent on your behalf.

Who pays?

Usually the estate, the homeowner or the landlord, and sometimes an insurer. Ask for an itemised quote and check who can approve the spending.
