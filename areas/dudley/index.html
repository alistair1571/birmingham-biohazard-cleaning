Biohazard cleaning in Dudley, DY1 to DY3

Dudley is the administrative centre of the Metropolitan Borough of Dudley, and it sits on a ridge of limestone and coal measures that gives the town its hills. Dudley Castle crowns the highest point, with the zoo in its grounds, and the Black Country Living Museum lies at the foot of the hill. Postcodes in the town centre are DY1 and DY2, with DY3 further north towards Sedgley and Gornal.

Dudley is also the local authority for neighbouring Brierley Hill, Halesowen, Stourbridge, Kingswinford and Sedgley, which means an address in any of those towns will be dealt with by the same council. The specialist teams cover the whole of the borough.

Hills, steps and awkward access

Dudley's geography matters for cleaning work. Many streets climb steeply, some roads are narrow, and older terraces have steps to the front door and long stairs inside. Waste has to be carried out in sealed containers, so access, parking and the distance to the nearest legal loading point all affect how long a job takes.

When you call, mention steps, stairs, the floor of any flat and where a van can stop. Good quotes take these details into account, so there are no surprises on the day.

Post-war estates, tower blocks and private lets

The Priory estate was built after the Second World War to rehouse families from slum clearance in the town, and other estates were added around Woodside, Kates Hill and Netherton. There are also flats and maisonettes, many now privately owned or let. This mix of tenures means different people may be responsible when a property becomes contaminated: the owner, a landlord, a housing association or a family dealing with an estate.

Our guides explain who is responsible and who pays, and the cost guide shows how clean-ups are priced. For hoarded or neglected homes, see the hoarder clean-up page.

Limestone, caves and canal tunnels

Dudley's limestone was mined and quarried on a large scale, and the Wren's Nest hill on the north side of town is a national nature reserve known for its fossils. Tunnels and caverns lie beneath parts of the town, and the Dudley Canal runs through the Dudley Tunnel. Ground conditions and old workings can affect drainage and cause damp in some properties.

Dudley Council is the local authority, and West Midlands Police and West Midlands Fire Service cover the area. For sewage that backs up in the home, see the sewage clean-up page, and call your water company's emergency line if the fault is in the public sewer.

Working with steps, slopes and old drainage

Dudley's hills mean that waste often has to be carried down steps or long paths to a van parked some distance away. That affects labour time and sometimes the number of people needed. Mention the nearest place a van can stop, how many steps there are and whether any neighbours' access will be blocked.

In older parts of the town, drains can be shared between properties, and a blockage next door may be the cause of your sewage backing up. Your water company is responsible for public sewers, and you or your landlord are responsible for the pipes within the property boundary, so a drainage survey is often worth doing alongside the clean-up.

A common situation in Dudley

A housing association tenant has not been seen for weeks. After a welfare check, the property is found to be heavily cluttered with limited walkways and food waste. The housing officer needs a safe route into the property and a plan that respects the tenant.

Specialists can work with the housing provider and, where appropriate, support services. The aim is to make the home safe and clean without forcing the tenant out or discarding things they value. Staged clearing, with the tenant's input, is more successful than a single sweeping clear-out.

Who to contact in Dudley

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Dudley is West Midlands Police, and the fire and rescue service is West Midlands Fire Service. The local authority is Dudley Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes DY1 to DY3, and nearby places include Brierley Hill, Kingswinford, Gornal, Sedgley, Netherton, Lye.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
