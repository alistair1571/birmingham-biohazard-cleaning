First, which Dudley?

"Dudley" is the town, with the castle, the zoo and the DY1 to DY3 postcodes. It is also the name of the Metropolitan Borough of Dudley, which includes Brierley Hill, Stourbridge, Halesowen, Kingswinford and Sedgley, and whose council covers all of them. If your address is in one of those towns, our pages for Brierley Hill and the neighbouring areas explain the local detail, and we cover them all the same way. The council is the same in every case: Dudley Council, which you can reach on 0300 555 2345.

Hills, steps and awkward access

Dudley is built on hills, and that shapes the job as much as the contamination does. Streets are steep, some properties are reached by steps or alleys, and parking can be a distance from the front door. A team that arrives expecting a flat drive and a short carry finds itself taking waste down a hillside by hand.

What this means in practice:

  • Waste is carried, not wheeled. Where a van can't stop outside, every bag and every piece of furniture is carried to wherever the van can. That adds hours, and sometimes people, to the job.
  • Narrow stairs and tight turns. A mattress or sofa that was brought up years ago may not come down in one piece, and a team will need to say so.
  • Flats with stairs only. Tell us the floor and whether there's a lift.
  • Tell us before we arrive. Steps, distance from parking, restrictions on the street and any shared entrance all affect the quote. Photographs of the approach help as much as photographs of the room.

None of this stops a job. It just means we plan the carry, and the quote reflects it.

Council homes, tower blocks and who is responsible

Dudley has a long history of council housing. The first council houses in the town were built from 1915 at the Brewery Fields estate on Kates Hill, and large estates followed in the 1930s, including Wren's Nest, built mostly between 1934 and 1939 to rehouse about 1,000 families from slum clearance. In the 1960s the council added tower blocks, some of which have since been demolished: Arley Court and Compton Court in Netherton, for example, were taken down after a survey found them no longer fit for purpose.

Why does that matter to you? Because a lot of Dudley's homes have been rented from a social landlord, passed through right-to-buy, or let on privately, and the answer to "who deals with this?" depends on which it is.

  • A council or housing association tenant. Contact your housing officer first. They have processes for hoarding, repairs and what happens after a death, and may charge for work they carry out. Our guide to who cleans up after a death in a property covers the tenancy side.
  • An owner who bought their council home. It's your responsibility, or your estate's. Long-term owners are the ones most likely to have decades of belongings, and families are often unsure where to start. See who pays for a hoarder clean up.
  • A private let. The landlord or agent arranges and pays first, and recovers what they can. See the landlords and letting agents page.
  • A flat in a block. Odour travels, and neighbours notice. Let the landlord or managing agent know early.

Limestone, caverns and canal tunnels

Under parts of Dudley are old limestone mines and caverns. The workings at Wren's Nest, Castle Hill and Mons Hill are protected as a Scheduled Monument, mining at Wren's Nest continued until 1924, and the Dudley Tunnel was once the second longest canal tunnel in Britain. Many caverns have since been infilled or blocked off for safety.

For anyone dealing with a damaged or contaminated home, there are two sensible points:

  • Cleaning and ground are different problems. If you notice new cracks, sloping floors or doors that no longer close, that is a matter for a structural surveyor and your insurer, not a cleaner. We'll tell you if what we see suggests you need one.
  • Drains and damp need their own check. A repeated backup or persistent damp points to a drainage or building fault that cleaning won't cure. See sewage clean-up and flood damage clean up, and ask a drainage specialist to survey before you redecorate.

Pests: a short note

Dudley Council runs its own rat and mouse service, unlike some neighbouring boroughs, but doesn't treat pigeons or squirrels. The Brierley Hill page has the council's current scope and how it deals with rubbish that attracts rats. Either way the order doesn't change: stop the pests, then clean. See after rodent clean up.

After a hospital stay or a move into care

A common Dudley call goes like this: a parent has gone into hospital or a care home, and the family opens the front door for the first time in months. The kitchen is unusable, the fridge is full, or the house is full of belongings and there's no way to move through it.

Some ways through it:

  1. Don't clear anything in a rush, and don't clear it without agreement or authority.If the person has capacity, it is their decision. If a Lasting Power of Attorney is registered, the attorney must act in their best interests. A solicitor can explain.
  2. Separate the urgent from the sentimental.Food, waste, human waste and anything that is a fire risk come first. Photographs, papers and keepsakes can wait.
  3. Look after the person as well as the house.A GP, social worker or the charity Hoarding UK can address the cause; cleaning alone often doesn't last.
  4. Stage the work and keep records.A phased clean is easier on everyone. Dated photographs help if there's later a question about what was removed.

If there's a bereavement involved, see after death cleaning.

Dudley biohazard cleaning questions

Do you cover the whole of Dudley borough?

Yes: the town and DY1 to DY3, plus Netherton, Gornal, Sedgley, Brierley Hill, Kingswinford, Halesowen and Stourbridge.

Can the team manage steep streets and flats with only stairs?

Yes. Tell us the steps, the stairs, the lift if there is one and where a van can stop, so the carry can be planned and priced properly.

Who do I report a neglected property to?

Dudley Council covers the whole borough, so one number, Dudley Council Plus, is the place to start.

Can a family get help clearing a relative's home?

Yes. We can clean and clear after a death or a hospital move, and papers, valuables and keepsakes can be set aside. Agree in advance who has authority to approve the work.

Is it a problem if the property is a council flat?

Not for us, but tell your housing officer first. They may have their own steps and charges.

I live too far away to visit. Can you manage without me?

Yes. We work from photographs, can liaise with a neighbour or solicitor who holds the keys, and send the records back afterwards.
