Biohazard cleaning in Lichfield, WS13 and WS14

Lichfield is a cathedral city in Staffordshire, about 16 miles north of Birmingham, and the seat of Lichfield District Council. Its postcodes are WS13 and WS14, with Burntwood in WS7. Because it is in Staffordshire rather than the West Midlands, Staffordshire Police and Staffordshire Fire and Rescue Service cover the city.

The specialist teams cover Lichfield and the surrounding villages, including Burntwood, Whittington, Shenstone, Fradley and Alrewas. Give the postcode when you call and describe what has happened.

Georgian streets and cathedral views

Lichfield Cathedral, with its three spires, is the only medieval English cathedral to have them, and the city centre contains many Georgian and medieval buildings. Samuel Johnson was born here. Old buildings and conservation areas bring particular care: fragile finishes, awkward access and sometimes limited parking.

If your property is listed or in a conservation area, tell the team so suitable methods can be used. Our after-death cleaning page explains how properties are cleaned with care.

Pools, canals and rural edges

Minster Pool and Stowe Pool lie in the heart of the city, and the Coventry Canal and Trent and Mersey Canal pass nearby. Beyond the built-up area are villages and farms, and rural properties are more exposed to rats, mice and wildlife. Droppings in lofts and outbuildings need removing with the right protective equipment: see after-rodent clean-up.

Rural homes may also have septic tanks or private drainage, so check with a drainage specialist if sewage is backing up. For sewage in the home, read our sewage clean-up page.

Homes, trains and local contacts

Lichfield has two railway stations, Lichfield City on the Cross-City line and Lichfield Trent Valley on the main line. Many homes belong to long-term owners, and probate and bereavement clean-ups are common. Staffordshire Police and Staffordshire Fire and Rescue Service cover the area, and Lichfield District Council is the local authority.

Staffordshire Fire and Rescue offers Home Fire Safety Visits, which can include conversations about hoarding. If a death is unexplained, cleaning can only begin once the police have released the property.

Older properties and careful methods

Lichfield's historic centre and nearby villages have many older houses with lime plaster, old timber floors and delicate finishes. Harsh chemicals or too much water can damage them. Tell the team about the age and construction of the property, and ask what products and methods they will use.

If the property is listed or in a conservation area, check whether any work requires consent before it starts. Lichfield District Council's conservation officers can advise. A careful clean is quite different from stripping out, and you should agree which you need.

A common situation in Lichfield

Lichfield has an older population than much of the West Midlands, and many enquiries involve a family member who lives some distance away. They may be dealing with a house they have not visited in months and are anxious about what they will find. The first step is a phone conversation and photographs, so the team can judge the scale before anyone travels.

From there, a plan can be agreed: what to clear, what to clean, what to save, and when. Teams can work around your schedule, and many provide a video walk-through so you can see progress without being present. Keep a list of the items you want, and agree who holds the keys.

Who to contact in Lichfield

For an emergency, always call 999. For a non-emergency police matter, the force covering Lichfield is Staffordshire Police, and the fire and rescue service is Staffordshire Fire and Rescue Service. The local authority is Lichfield District Council, which handles environmental health, empty and neglected properties, and waste. The area uses the postcodes WS13 and WS14, and nearby places include Burntwood, Whittington, Shenstone, Fradley, Alrewas, Streethay.

If a sudden death, crime or fire has happened, no cleaning can start until the emergency services have released the property. Once they have, call for an assessment.
